📌 Key Insight

The nominal ranking and the real ranking are almost opposites. Massachusetts pays the highest average salary ($76,600) but its high cost of living drops it to roughly $52,500 in real purchasing power — below the national average. Meanwhile Texas, 15th in raw pay, tops the list at about $68,000 adjusted, thanks to a below-average cost of living and no state income tax. The lesson: a bigger salary in an expensive state can leave you poorer than a smaller one somewhere cheaper. What you keep and invest matters more than the headline figure.

Average vs. Median: Why the Gap Matters

The national average salary ($63,795) is meaningfully higher than the median ($56,420). That gap exists because a relatively small number of very high earners — in tech, finance, medicine, and law — pull the average up. The median is the true midpoint and a better benchmark for "am I typical?" When you compare yourself, use the median. When you read a flashy "average salary" headline, remember it's skewed upward.

What Actually Grows Your Wealth

Your state and salary set the starting line, but they don't determine the finish. Two things matter more over a career:

  • Your savings rate. Someone earning $55,000 who saves 20% out-builds someone earning $80,000 who saves 5%. Rate beats raw income.
  • Cost of living. A cheaper state effectively raises your income by lowering what you spend — money that can go straight into investments. For remote workers especially, geography is a wealth lever.

Pair a solid salary with a low-cost location and a high savings rate, and even a "below-average" paycheck compounds into real wealth.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average salary in the US in 2026? ▼

About $63,795 on average, with a median of about $56,420, per Bureau of Labor Statistics data. The average exceeds the median because top earners pull the mean up; the median better reflects a typical worker.

Which state has the highest average salary? ▼

Massachusetts at about $76,600, followed by Washington ($72,300), New York ($72,000), California ($70,800), and Connecticut ($70,200). Washington, D.C. leads all areas at ~$92,600 but is a district, not a state.

Which state has the lowest average salary? ▼

Mississippi at about $51,200, followed by West Virginia, Arkansas, Alabama, and South Carolina. These states also have low costs of living, so the paychecks stretch further than the raw figures imply.

Which state has the best salary after cost of living? ▼

Texas — its ~$63,200 average is worth about $68,000 in national-average purchasing power, helped by a low cost of living and no state income tax. Ohio, Indiana, Tennessee, and Iowa also rise sharply, while Hawaii, California, and New York fall.

Is a $60,000 salary good in 2026? ▼

It's close to the national median, so it's a solid, roughly typical income — but its value depends on where you live. $60,000 is comfortable in low-cost states and tight in high-cost ones like California or Hawaii. Your savings rate matters more than the headline number.
