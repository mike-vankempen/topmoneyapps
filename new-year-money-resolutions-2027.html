New Year's Money Resolutions for 2027

📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read ✍️ Mike Van Kempen

📋 General information, not financial advice. Pick the goals that fit your situation.

Most money resolutions fail for one reason: they're vague and rely on willpower. "Save more" is a wish; "transfer $250 every payday" is a plan. The eight goals below are specific, measurable, and automatable — set them up once in January and they run on autopilot all year. Pick two or three, not all eight.

1.Build (or top up) a starter emergency fund

Target a $1,000 starter fund first, then work toward 3–6 months of expenses. Keep it in a high-yield savings account so it earns ~4% instead of sitting idle. Use our emergency fund calculator to set your exact number.

2.Kill your highest-interest debt

Credit-card APRs often top 20%, so paying them off is a guaranteed double-digit return. Pick the avalanche method (highest rate first) to save the most, or snowball (smallest balance first) for momentum. Our debt payoff calculator shows months-to-free and total interest for each.

3.Automate your saving

This is the one that makes the rest work. Set an automatic transfer from checking to savings (or investing) that fires every payday. Automation removes the monthly decision — it's the single biggest predictor of whether a money goal sticks.

4.Capture your full 401(k) match

If your employer matches, say, 50% up to 6% of pay, not contributing enough to get it is leaving free money on the table — an instant 50% return. Raise your contribution to at least the match in January. See the contribution limits for the current caps.

5.Open or fund a Roth IRA

A Roth IRA grows tax-free and gives you investment options a 401(k) often lacks. Even small automatic monthly contributions add up over decades. Pair it with a low-cost index fund and let compounding do the work.

6.Move idle cash to a high-yield account

If your savings sits in a big-bank account earning 0.01%, you're losing money to inflation. The best high-yield savings accounts pay around 4% — on $10,000 that's ~$400/year versus a few dollars, for the same FDIC protection.

7.Track your net worth

What gets measured gets managed. Log your net worth once a month (assets minus debts). Watching the number climb is motivating, and it turns abstract goals into a concrete scoreboard. See where you stand with our net worth by age benchmarks.

8.Audit your subscriptions and bills

The average household wastes real money on forgotten subscriptions. Spend an hour cancelling what you don't use and negotiating one or two bills. It's a one-time effort that pays off every month for the rest of the year.

Make it stick: write each resolution as "[specific action] every [frequency], automated." "Save $250 every payday, automatic transfer" beats "save more" every time. Then set a calendar reminder to bump each number up a notch mid-year.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are good money resolutions for 2027?

Specific, automatic ones: build an emergency fund, pay off high-interest debt, automate a set savings amount each payday, capture your full 401(k) match, and move idle cash to a high-yield account. Tie each to a number and a date.

Why do money resolutions fail?

They're vague, rely on willpower, and have no system. Ones that stick are specific, automated, and tracked. Automation is the biggest predictor of success.

How much should I save in 2027?

Aim for 15–20% of gross income including any match. If that's too much, start with whatever is sustainable and raise it one point every few months.

What's the first move in January?

Automate it — set an automatic payday transfer to savings or bump your 401(k) contribution in the first week, so the goal runs on autopilot all year.
