Open Enrollment Checklist: Health, HSA, FSA and 401(k)

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 7 min read

General information, not personalized advice. Limits are from the IRS; deadlines are from HealthCare.gov, CMS and Medicare.gov. Your employer's plan documents control your own options.

Open enrollment is the one time each year you can change most workplace benefits without a life event, so it pays to check every box. For 2027, the HSA limit rises to $4,500 for self-only coverage and $9,000 for family coverage, the health FSA limit rises to $3,500, and the 401(k) limit rises to $25,500. HealthCare.gov open enrollment runs from November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027, and you must pick a plan by December 15 for coverage that starts January 1. Use the checklist below to review health coverage, tax-advantaged accounts and retirement savings before your window closes.

Key Takeaways

  • For 2027, the HSA contribution limit is $4,500 for self-only coverage and $9,000 for family coverage, according to IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-24.
  • The 2027 health FSA salary-reduction limit is $3,500, and plans that allow a carryover can let up to $700 of unused money roll into the next year.
  • The 2027 401(k) employee contribution limit is $25,500, up from $24,500 in 2026; the IRA limit stays at $7,500.
  • HealthCare.gov open enrollment for 2027 coverage runs from November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027; enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1.
  • In KFF's 2026 survey, workers paid an average of $1,672 a year for single coverage and $7,471 for family coverage through employer plans.

Step 1: Review Your Health Plan Options

Don't let your current plan roll over by default. Premiums, networks and drug coverage can change every year. Compare each plan on these points:

  • Premium: what comes out of each paycheck. In KFF's 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey, the average total premium was $9,804 for single coverage and $28,303 for family coverage, and workers paid $1,672 and $7,471 of that, respectively.
  • Deductible: what you pay before the plan starts sharing costs.
  • Out-of-pocket maximum: the most you could pay in a bad year for covered, in-network care. This is your worst case.
  • Network: confirm your doctors, hospitals and pharmacy are in network for 2027.
  • Prescriptions: look up each drug you take in the plan's 2027 formulary (its list of covered drugs).

A quick way to compare: add up a year of premiums plus the out-of-pocket maximum for each plan. That total is the most each plan could cost you. Then estimate a typical year based on how much care you used last year. Our average health insurance cost guide has more benchmarks.

Step 2: Decide Between an HSA and an FSA

A health savings account (HSA) is only available with a high-deductible health plan (HDHP). A flexible spending account (FSA) works with most other plans. Here are the 2027 numbers:

HSA contribution limit$4,500$9,000
HDHP minimum deductible$1,750$3,500
HDHP out-of-pocket maximum$8,700$17,400
Health FSA salary-reduction limit$3,500 per employee
Health FSA maximum carryover$700 (if your plan allows carryovers)
Dependent care FSA$7,500 ($3,750 if married filing separately)

Sources: IRS Rev. Proc. 2026-24 (HSA and HDHP); IRS Rev. Proc. 2026-35 and IR-2026-133 (health FSA); IRS Publication 15-B (dependent care). If you are 55 or older, IRS Publication 969 allows an extra $1,000 HSA catch-up contribution.

  • FSAs are generally "use it or lose it." The IRS lets a plan offer either a grace period of up to 2 1/2 months after the plan year or a carryover, but not both for health FSAs. Ask HR which one your plan has before you pick an amount.
  • Size an FSA to costs you are confident about, such as regular prescriptions or planned dental work.
  • Dependent care FSA: the limit was raised from $5,000 to $7,500 starting in 2026, according to IRS Publication 15-B. The amount is not adjusted for inflation, so it stays $7,500 for 2027. If you pay for daycare, compare costs in our childcare cost by state guide.

Step 3: Set Your 401(k) Contribution for 2027

  • Get the full match. Find your employer's matching formula in the plan summary and contribute at least enough to collect all of it.
  • Check your investments, not just your rate. Confirm where new contributions go and what each fund charges.
  • Roth or traditional: if your plan offers a Roth 401(k), decide whether you want to pay tax now (Roth) or later (traditional).

Step 4: Review the Rest of Your Benefits

  • Life insurance: check how much employer coverage you have and whether you could keep it if you left the job. See our life insurance guide to estimate how much your family would need.
  • Disability insurance: check whether short- and long-term disability are included or optional, and what share of pay each replaces.
  • Dental and vision: enroll only if expected use is worth the premium.
  • Beneficiaries: update beneficiaries on your 401(k), HSA and life insurance after a marriage, divorce or birth.
  • Dependents: add a new spouse or child, and remove anyone who no longer qualifies.

Step 5: Confirm Your Elections

Before the deadline, submit your elections and save the confirmation page. In January, check your first paycheck to make sure the premium, HSA or FSA amount and 401(k) rate match what you chose. Fixing an error is much easier in the first weeks of the year.

Missed Open Enrollment?

Outside open enrollment, you generally need a qualifying life event to change coverage. HealthCare.gov lists examples "like getting married, new baby or dependent, moving, or losing health coverage." For the Marketplace, some events come with a 60-day window: for example, when COBRA coverage ends, you have 60 days to enroll in a Marketplace plan through a Special Enrollment Period. Employer plans set their own rules for mid-year changes, so ask HR right away if your situation changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is open enrollment for 2027?

Employer plans set their own dates, so check with HR. HealthCare.gov open enrollment runs from November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027, and you must enroll by December 15 for coverage that starts January 1. Medicare open enrollment runs from October 15 to December 7.

What is the 2027 HSA contribution limit?

For 2027, you can contribute up to $4,500 with self-only coverage or $9,000 with family coverage under a high-deductible health plan. If you are 55 or older, you can add a $1,000 catch-up contribution.

What is the 2027 FSA limit?

The 2027 health FSA salary-reduction limit is $3,500. If your plan allows a carryover, up to $700 of unused money can roll into the next year. The dependent care FSA limit is $7,500, or $3,750 if married filing separately.

What is the 401(k) limit for 2027?

Employees can contribute up to $25,500 to a 401(k) in 2027, up from $24,500 in 2026. Workers 50 and older can add $8,500, and workers aged 60 to 63 can add $11,750 instead.

Can I change my benefits after open enrollment ends?

Generally only if you have a qualifying life event, such as getting married, having or adopting a child, moving or losing other health coverage. Employer plans set their own rules for mid-year changes, so contact HR as soon as your situation changes.
