Rent vs. Buy in 2026: Is It Cheaper to Rent or Buy?

📅 Updated October 2026 ⏱ 7 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen 📊 Source: Realtor.com

Right now, the monthly math is lopsided: in 2026, renting is cheaper than buying in all 50 of the largest U.S. metros — by about $920 a month on average, according to Realtor.com.[1] High mortgage rates near 6.5–7% and still-elevated home prices are the reason. But "cheaper this month" isn't the whole story — buying usually wins over time. Here's the gap, the break-even point, and how to decide. More in our Personal Finance Statistics Hub →

The monthly cost gap

A Realtor.com analysis found that renting a starter home costs less per month than buying a comparable one in every one of the 50 biggest metros — the widest this gap has been in years.[1] But the size of the gap swings enormously by market:

Why buying costs so much more per month right now

A typical renter pays around $1,700 a month nationally; the all-in cost to own a comparable home runs closer to $2,600 once you add everything up:

Owning isn't just principal and interest. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, and maintenance (budget ~1% of the home's value a year) add 30% or more on top of the base mortgage payment — costs renters never see. Combine those with a ~6.5–7% rate on a ~$420,000 median home and the monthly number climbs well past comparable rent.

When buying wins: the break-even

Monthly cost is only half the equation. Every payment you make as an owner builds equity, and your housing cost stays roughly fixed while rent keeps rising. Those two forces mean buying usually becomes the cheaper option over time — commonly after 5 to 7 years, though the break-even can range from about 2 to 12 years depending on your market, rate, and how fast rents and values climb.[1] The shorter your expected stay, the more renting wins; the longer you'll stay put, the more buying does.

🏘️ Rent if you…

  • Might move within ~5 years
  • Want flexibility and no maintenance
  • Would be stretched by taxes, insurance & upkeep
  • Can invest the monthly difference
  • Live in a high-gap metro (coastal)

🏠 Buy if you…

  • Will stay put 5+ years
  • Can afford the full cost comfortably
  • Want stability and to build equity
  • Are in a low-gap, affordable metro
  • Can refinance later if rates fall

📊 Key insight

"Renting is throwing money away" is outdated advice in 2026. With the monthly gap near $920, a renter who invests the difference can come out ahead of a buyer for years — especially if they move before the break-even point. Buying still wins for long-haul stability and equity, but it's no longer the automatic choice. The honest answer depends on two things: how long you'll stay, and your specific local market — not a national headline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to rent or buy in 2026? ▼

Monthly, renting is cheaper in all 50 largest metros — about $920/month less on average — because of high mortgage rates (~6.5–7%) and elevated prices.

How much cheaper is renting? ▼

About $920/month on average, ranging from ~$64 in Pittsburgh to ~$2,425 in San Jose. Smallest in affordable metros, largest in expensive coastal ones.

When does buying become cheaper? ▼

Usually after 5–7 years (range ~2–12), as you build equity and your payment stays fixed while rent rises.

Why is buying so expensive now? ▼

Prices rose sharply and rates roughly doubled to ~6.5–7%. Add taxes, insurance, and maintenance and owning can cost 30%+ more than the base payment.

Should I rent or buy? ▼

Rent for flexibility or a short horizon (and invest the difference); buy if you'll stay 5+ years and can comfortably afford the full cost. It hinges on your timeline and local market.
