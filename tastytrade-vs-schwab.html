tastytrade vs. Schwab for Options (2026): Fees, Margin and Platforms Compared

📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen

For options commissions alone, tastytrade usually costs less: $1 per contract to open, capped at $10 per leg, and $0 to close, versus Schwab's $0.65 per contract on both the opening and closing trade. Schwab lists $0 commissions on exercise and assignment, has the higher TopMoneyApps score (4.93/5 vs. 4.13/5) and offers far more account types, while tastytrade charges less for futures and offers a lower flat margin rate to qualifying accounts. All fees below were checked in November 2026.

Key Takeaways

  • As of November 2026, tastytrade charges $1.00 per stock or ETF option contract to open (capped at $10 per leg) and $0 to close; Schwab charges $0.65 per contract on every online option trade.
  • Schwab lists a $0 commission on exercised and assigned options, while tastytrade charges a $5-per-strike fee for exercise or assignment.
  • Futures cost $2.25 per contract at Schwab, versus $1.00 per side for E-mini futures and $1.25 per side for options on E-mini futures at tastytrade.
  • Schwab's margin rates run from 12.075% to 10.325% on its published tiers (base rate 10.25% since September 18, 2026); tastytrade's standard rates run from 11.00% to 8.00%, with a 6.75% flat rate for qualifying accounts.
  • Both brokers have a $0 minimum to open, require $2,000 for margin privileges and are SIPC members; futures accounts at both are not SIPC protected.

tastytrade vs. Schwab: Quick Verdict

Charles Schwab has the higher overall rating, with a TopMoneyApps score 4.93/5, versus a TopMoneyApps score 4.13/5 for tastytrade. That score covers the whole broker, not only options. For options-focused traders, our best options trading apps guide highlights tastytrade's capped commissions.

Choose tastytrade if you trade multi-contract option positions, trade futures or futures options, or can qualify for its flat margin rate. Choose Schwab if you want options inside a full-service brokerage with IRAs, 401(k)s, custodial and education accounts, $0 exercise and assignment commissions, and the thinkorswim platform.

Options Commissions: What You Actually Pay

An options contract gives the buyer the right to buy or sell an asset at a set price by a set date. A standard stock option contract represents 100 shares, according to FINRA. Each contract in a trade is called a "leg" when you combine several, as in a spread.

tastytrade charges $1.00 per contract to open a stock or ETF option position and $0 to close it. The opening commission is capped at $10 per leg, so a 30-contract leg costs $10, not $30. Exchange, clearing and regulatory fees still apply, including a $0.10-per-contract clearing fee. Broad-based index options such as SPX also cost $1.00 to open and $0 to close, but they are excluded from the $10 cap and carry exchange fees (for example, $0.60 per contract on SPX).

Schwab charges a $0 base commission plus $0.65 per contract, quoted per side, so you pay on the opening and the closing trade. Multi-leg orders such as spreads are charged $0.65 per contract. Industry fees and, on some index options, an exchange's proprietary index fee also apply. Placing an option trade with a broker adds a $25 service charge.

Here is how commissions alone compare for a single-leg trade that you open and later close (before exchange, clearing and regulatory fees):

  • 1 contract: tastytrade $1.00; Schwab $1.30.
  • 10 contracts: tastytrade $10.00; Schwab $13.00.
  • 50 contracts: tastytrade $10.00 (capped); Schwab $65.00.

If you hold positions to expiration, the math changes. Schwab's commission for exercised and assigned options is $0. tastytrade's help center says it charges a $5-per-strike fee for exercise and for assignment. Assignment is when the seller of an option is required to fulfill the contract, such as delivering or buying 100 shares per contract. With American-style options, FINRA notes that a seller can be assigned at any time before expiration.

Futures and Futures Options

tastytrade charges $1.00 per contract to open and $1.00 to close E-mini futures, $0.75 per side on micro futures, and $1.25 per side on options on E-mini futures. A $0.30-per-contract clearing fee, exchange fees and an NFA fee are added. Futures options are excluded from the $10 cap.

Schwab charges $2.25 per contract for futures and futures options, online or through a broker, plus exchange and regulatory fees. Futures trade through Charles Schwab Futures and Forex LLC and require margin approval and a $1,500 account minimum. Trading futures in a Schwab IRA requires $25,000 in net liquidation value and margin and option spreads approval. At tastytrade, futures require "The Works" permission level, or "IRA The Works" in an IRA.

Margin Rates

Margin means borrowing from your broker against your account to trade; the loan accrues interest and losses can exceed what you deposited. As of November 2026:

  • Schwab: base rate 10.25%, last changed September 18, 2026. Rates are 12.075% on debit balances of $100 to $24,999.99, 11.575% from $25,000, 10.625% from $50,000, 10.575% from $100,000 and 10.325% from $250,000 to $499,999.99. Balances of $500,000 and up are quoted by phone.
  • tastytrade: standard rates of 11.00% under $25,000, stepping down to 8.00% at $1,000,000 and up. A 6.75% flat rate (base rate minus 3.25%) applies to the whole margin balance for new accounts opened on or after October 23, 2026, and for existing clients who bring in $25,000 or more in new assets.

Both brokers can change these rates without notice.

Platforms: thinkorswim vs. tastytrade

thinkorswim comes in desktop, web and mobile versions, all free for Schwab clients. Schwab says it offers "hundreds of technical indicators, studies, and drawing tools," and its paperMoney mode lets you practice with hypothetical money. Schwab also supports options trading on Schwab.com and the Schwab Mobile app, with tools such as an options screener, probabilities and simulated trades listed on its options page.

tastytrade offers desktop, web and mobile platforms plus an Open API for connecting your own tools. Its desktop platform has an analysis tab to "evaluate probabilities, and test volatility shifts before committing to a trade," and a ladder view for futures and stocks. tastytrade also lists an options backtesting tool.

Options Approval Levels and Account Types

Both brokers require you to apply for options trading, and higher levels allow riskier strategies. tastytrade uses three levels: Limited/Cash (buy options, covered calls, cash-secured puts), Basic (adds defined-risk spreads and uncovered puts) and The Works (adds uncovered calls, short selling and futures). In an IRA, tastytrade allows spreads at the Basic level, and uncovered calls only in IRA The Works with $25,000 in start-of-day net liquidation value; naked puts are not allowed in any IRA. Schwab's platform help documentation describes levels 0 to 3, from covered calls up to uncovered options at Level 3, and Schwab's application offers to add margin access if your account does not have it.

tastytrade offers individual, joint, IRA (traditional, Roth, SEP and beneficiary) and entity or trust accounts. Schwab lists more than 15 account types with $0 minimums, including brokerage, IRA, custodial (UGMA/UTMA), 529, individual 401(k) and trust accounts. If you are new to these, see what a brokerage account is.

Cash, Protection and Other Fees

Schwab paid 0.01% APY on uninvested brokerage cash as of November 26, 2026, and says accounts approved for options use the Schwab One Interest feature, which earns the same yield as Bank Sweep. NerdWallet's tastytrade review (last updated December 2025) lists 0.01% on uninvested cash at tastytrade. Money market funds can pay more: Schwab listed a 3.79% 7-day yield on its Prime Advantage Money Fund as of November 25, 2026, and yields change daily.

Both are SIPC members. SIPC protects up to $500,000 per customer, including $250,000 in cash, if a brokerage fails, not if your trades lose money. Schwab adds excess coverage with a $600 million aggregate limit. tastytrade's clearing firm, Apex, carries excess coverage with a $150 million aggregate limit and per-customer limits of $37.5 million for securities and $900,000 for cash. Futures accounts at both brokers are not covered by SIPC. tastytrade is owned by IG Group.

Other tastytrade fees include $75 for an outgoing ACAT transfer, $60 to close an IRA and $25 for an outgoing domestic wire. Schwab says there are no fees to open or maintain an account. Frequently Asked Questions

Is tastytrade or Schwab cheaper for options?▼
It depends on how many contracts you trade. As of November 2026, tastytrade charges $1 per contract to open, capped at $10 per leg, and $0 to close. Schwab charges $0.65 per contract to open and again to close. For one contract opened and closed, that is $1.00 at tastytrade vs. $1.30 at Schwab in commissions; both add exchange, clearing or regulatory fees.
Does Schwab or tastytrade charge for option exercise and assignment?▼
Schwab lists a $0 commission for exercised and assigned options. tastytrade's help center says it charges a $5-per-strike fee for exercise and for assignment on equity and ETF options. tastytrade charges no fee for out-of-the-money options that expire worthless.
Is thinkorswim free to use?▼
Yes. Schwab says its clients can use thinkorswim desktop, web and mobile at no charge, though standard trade pricing applies, such as $0.65 per options contract. thinkorswim includes paperMoney, a simulated trading mode that uses hypothetical money.
What is the minimum to trade options at tastytrade or Schwab?▼
Neither broker has a minimum to open a cash account. Both require $2,000 for margin privileges. Options trading at both needs separate approval, and the strategies you can use depend on your approval level and account type.
Are tastytrade and Schwab accounts SIPC protected?▼
Yes, for securities accounts. SIPC covers up to $500,000 per customer, including up to $250,000 in cash, if a member firm fails. It does not cover trading losses. Futures accounts at both brokers are not SIPC protected.
