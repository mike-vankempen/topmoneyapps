Fundrise vs. Arrived (2026): Which Real Estate App Is Better?

📅 Updated November 27, 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen

Fundrise is the better fit for most investors who want diversified real estate funds from $10 with quarterly withdrawals, while Arrived suits investors who want to pick individual rental homes from $100 and collect monthly distributions. Neither requires you to be an accredited investor. Here is how they compare on fees, products, liquidity, IRAs and track record.

Key Takeaways

  • Fundrise has a TopMoneyApps score of 3.83/5 and Arrived 3.33/5 in the real estate category.
  • Fundrise's minimum is $10 for a taxable account and $1,000 for an IRA; Arrived's minimum is $100 for all offerings.
  • Fundrise charges a 0.15% annual advisory fee plus a 0.85% annual management fee on its real estate funds.
  • Arrived charges an asset management fee of 0.1% to 0.30% per quarter plus a one-time sourcing fee built into the share price, and property managers take 8% of rent on single-family homes.
  • Fundrise's Flagship and Income funds allow penalty-free quarterly withdrawals; Arrived's single properties can only be sold in a monthly secondary-market window after a 6-month hold, and buyers aren't guaranteed.

Fundrise vs. Arrived: Quick Verdict

Fundrise ranks higher in our real estate rankings, with a TopMoneyApps score 3.83/5 versus TopMoneyApps score 3.33/5 for Arrived. Both let non-accredited investors put small amounts into private real estate, but they work differently. Fundrise sells shares in large, diversified funds. Arrived sells shares in individual rental houses, plus a few pooled funds.

Choose Fundrise if you want a low minimum, diversification across a portfolio Fundrise says spans more than 20,000 units, and a clear way to withdraw each quarter.

Choose Arrived if you want to choose specific single-family rentals, see each property's numbers and get distributions monthly, and you can leave money in for years.

⭐ Our Verdict

Fundrise is the simpler, more liquid choice for most first-time real estate investors. Arrived fits hands-off landlords-at-heart who want to own slices of specific houses and accept limited ways to sell. Both are long-term, illiquid investments that can lose value.

Fees

Fundrise charges a 0.15% annual advisory fee on your account plus a 0.85% annual management fee on its real estate funds, for 1.00% a year in total. On a $5,000 balance that's about $50 a year. Its Innovation Fund, which invests in private tech companies, charges a 1.85% management fee instead.

Arrived fees depend on what you buy. Its asset management fee "ranges from 0.1% to 0.30% per quarter":

  • Single-family homes: 0.15% of the purchase price per quarter (0.6% a year).
  • Single Family Residential Fund and Seattle City Fund: 0.25% of net assets per quarter (1% a year).
  • Real Estate Income Fund: 0.3% of net assets per quarter (1.2% a year).

Arrived also charges a one-time sourcing fee that is built into the offering price; in one 2025 SEC filing for an Arrived property series, it was 3.5% of the purchase price. On top of that, property managers take 8% of gross rental income on single-family rentals and 15% to 20% of revenue on vacation rentals, paid from the property's income before distributions.

Products and Minimums

Fundrise

  • Flagship Real Estate Fund: an interval fund registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940, with $1.1 billion in net assets as of November 25, 2026, and a $10 minimum. An interval fund buys back a set share of its stock at regular intervals.
  • Income Real Estate Fund: an interval fund that makes real estate loans (private credit).
  • Innovation Fund (VCX): now listed on the NYSE, so new shares are bought through a brokerage account, not the Fundrise app.

Fundrise says it has more than 385,000 investors. You must be 18 or older, a U.S. resident and a U.S. taxpayer.

Arrived

  • Single-family rentals: buy shares of one specific house, from $100. You can invest at most 9.8% of the equity in any one property.
  • Single Family Residential Fund and Seattle City Fund: pooled funds that own many homes, from $100.
  • Real Estate Income Fund: a fund of real estate loans, from $100.

Arrived is open to U.S. citizens and green card holders who live in the 50 states and are 18 or older.

Liquidity: Getting Your Money Out

This is where the two differ most. Private real estate is illiquid, meaning you can't sell instantly at a known price like a stock.

Fundrise lets you request withdrawals from the Flagship and Income funds quarterly, with no penalty. Older eREIT shares held under five years carry about a 1% charge. All requests are subject to limits, so in a stressed market you may not get all your money out at once.

Arrived asks you to hold single-property shares for at least six months. After that, you can list them in a secondary market that opens for one week each month. Arrived says "liquidity is not guaranteed," you may sell for less than you paid, and transaction fees may apply. Its funds offer redemptions four times a year at Arrived's valuation, generally capped at 5% of the fund's value per quarter and 20% per year, and a redemption cost may apply.

Returns and Distributions

Fundrise publishes yearly returns for its advisory clients: 6.24% in 2025, 5.75% in 2024, −7.45% in 2023, 1.50% in 2022 and 22.99% in 2021. These are averages across clients, not what any one investor earned, and past performance does not guarantee future results. Fundrise aims to pay distributions after the end of each quarter, but they aren't guaranteed.

Arrived pays property cash flow monthly. Its Real Estate Income Fund reported a trailing 12-month annualized dividend yield of 8.35% as of August 2026; that is income only and is not a total return. Arrived did not publish actual historical total returns for its single-family properties on the pages we checked.

IRAs and Taxes

Fundrise offers traditional and Roth IRAs with a $1,000 minimum, held by custodian Inspira Financial Trust. Fundrise pays Inspira's annual custody fee if you invest at least $3,000 by its cutoff date each year or hold at least $25,000 in the IRA. Arrived supports retirement money only through a self-directed "checkbook" IRA or solo 401(k) set up with partner Rocket Dollar, which takes more paperwork. (For IRA basics, see Roth vs. traditional IRA.)

Fundrise sends a 1099-DIV for each fund that paid you at least $10 in the year, plus a 1099-B if you sold shares; its last K-1 forms, for older eFund shares, are expected for tax year 2025. Arrived says it sends a 1099-DIV by the end of January.

Who Should Choose Fundrise?

  • Beginners who want to start with $10 and stay diversified
  • Investors who may need to withdraw within a few years
  • People who want a standard Roth or traditional IRA
  • Anyone who prefers one fund to picking properties

Who Should Choose Arrived?

  • Investors who want to choose specific rental homes
  • People who want monthly distributions
  • Savers who can leave money in for years and accept limited resale options
  • Self-directed IRA users who already work with Rocket Dollar

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Fundrise or Arrived cheaper?▼
It depends on the product. Fundrise charges 1.00% a year on its real estate funds (a 0.15% advisory fee plus a 0.85% management fee). Arrived's asset management fee is 0.6% a year on single-family homes and 1% to 1.2% a year on its funds, plus a one-time sourcing fee and property management fees of 8% of rent on single-family homes.
Do you need to be accredited to invest in Fundrise or Arrived?▼
No. Both Fundrise and Arrived are open to non-accredited investors. Fundrise requires you to be 18 or older, a U.S. resident and a U.S. taxpayer; Arrived requires U.S. citizens or green card holders, 18 or older, living in the 50 states.
Can I withdraw money from Fundrise or Arrived anytime?▼
Not anytime. Fundrise's Flagship and Income funds allow penalty-free withdrawal requests each quarter, subject to limits. Arrived's single-property shares can be listed on a secondary market that opens one week a month after a 6-month hold, with no guaranteed buyer, and its funds allow redemptions four times a year.
Can I invest in Fundrise or Arrived with an IRA?▼
Yes. Fundrise offers traditional and Roth IRAs with a $1,000 minimum through custodian Inspira Financial Trust. Arrived accepts retirement money only through a checkbook IRA or solo 401(k) set up with partner Rocket Dollar.
What returns has Fundrise had?▼
Fundrise reports average advisory client returns of 6.24% in 2025, 5.75% in 2024, -7.45% in 2023, 1.50% in 2022 and 22.99% in 2021. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
