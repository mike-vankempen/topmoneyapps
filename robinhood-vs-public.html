Robinhood vs. Public.com (2026): Which Investing App Is Better?

📅 Updated November 27, 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen

Public.com is the better choice if you don't want to pay for a subscription, and Robinhood is the better value if you will pay for one. Both charge $0 commission on U.S. stocks, ETFs and stock options. Public's free tier pays 3.30% APY on cash and rebates on options, while Robinhood pays interest on cash only with Gold, which costs $5 a month. Here is how they compare on fees, plans, IRAs and safety.

Robinhood vs. Public: Quick Verdict

Public.com ranks higher in our investing rankings, with a TopMoneyApps score 4.12/5 versus TopMoneyApps score 4.00/5 for Robinhood. Both charge $0 commission on U.S. stocks, ETFs and stock options, both offer an IRA match, and both have paid plans that raise your cash yield and match.

Choose Public if you want to earn interest without a subscription, get a rebate on options trades, or buy Treasuries and bonds. Public's cash account paid 3.30% APY with no plan as of September 17, 2026; Robinhood pays interest on brokerage cash only to Gold members.

Choose Robinhood if you want the cheapest paid plan, the lowest fractional-share minimum or the widest menu: Robinhood Gold costs $5 a month, fractional shares start at $1, and the app adds futures, prediction markets, a managed portfolio from $50 and a free desktop platform, Robinhood Legend.

Trading Fees

Stock, ETF and stock option trades are commission-free at both. Each passes through about $0.04 per options contract in regulatory and clearing fees: Robinhood charges $0.04 per contract to cover both, and Public lists $0.02 each. Index options cost $0.50 per contract at Robinhood ($0.35 with Gold) plus exchange fees, and $0.35 at Public.

Public adds an options rebate of $0.06 to $0.18 per stock or ETF contract, set monthly by your trading volume. Public says the rebate shares its payment for order flow revenue with customers.

Leaving costs the same: both charge $100 for an outgoing account transfer. Public also charges $3.99 a month on accounts under $70 with no activity for six months.

Paid Plans: Robinhood Gold vs. Public Reserve

Robinhood Gold costs $5 a month or $50 a year, with a 30-day free trial. It unlocks interest on uninvested cash (3.60% APY as of September 17, 2026), raises the IRA contribution match from 1% to 3%, makes the first $1,000 of margin interest-free and lowers index option, futures and event contract fees.

Public Reserve costs $20 a month or $200 a year. It raises Public's cash APY from 3.30% to 3.75% and the IRA contribution match from 1% to 4%, paid over 12 months.

Here is what that means in dollars on a full 2026 IRA contribution of $7,500 (under age 50): the Gold match is $225 against a $50 fee, and the Reserve match is $300 against a $200 fee. On cash alone, Reserve's extra 0.45% over Public's free rate pays for the plan only on balances above about $44,000.

Cash, IRAs and Margin

  • Cash: Robinhood's brokerage cash sweep earns interest only with Gold (cash in Strategies-managed accounts is the exception), and sweep deposits are eligible for up to $2.5 million of FDIC coverage for individual accounts. Public's high-yield cash account pays 3.30% APY with no subscription and offers up to $5 million of FDIC coverage through partner banks. See money market vs. high-yield savings for other places to park cash.
  • IRA transfers: Robinhood pays 1% on IRA transfers and 401(k) rollovers whether or not you have Gold, with no cap on rollovers. Public pays 1% on transfers over 60 months. At both, matched money must stay put for five years or the match can be taken back. Read how to roll over a 401(k) before moving old workplace savings.
  • Margin: Robinhood charges 5.25% on balances up to $50,000 with or without Gold; Public charges 5.15% up to $50,000 (both as of September 17, 2026).

Features and Investments

Robinhood

  • 24 Hour Market: trade select stocks and ETFs 24 hours a day, five days a week, with limit orders.
  • Futures and prediction markets: futures cost $0.75 per contract ($0.50 with Gold) plus fees, and event contracts trade through Robinhood Derivatives, which is not SIPC-protected.
  • Robinhood Strategies: managed portfolios for 0.25% a year with a $50 minimum.
  • Robinhood Legend: a free web-based platform for active traders.
  • Crypto: no separate fee on default orders; Robinhood's revenue is built into the price. As of June 15, 2026, it received 0.95% of the order value on those orders.

Public.com

  • 24/5 trading: eligible stocks and ETFs from Sunday 8 p.m. to Friday 8 p.m. ET.
  • Bonds and Treasuries: Treasuries cost $0.10 to $0.25 per $100 of face value.
  • Public Advisors ($1,000 minimum): a Treasury Account, Direct Indexing (0.19% a year) and Generated Assets (0.49%), which builds a custom index from a text prompt.
  • Crypto: flat fees from $0.49 on orders up to $10, then 1.25% on orders above $500.

Order Routing, Safety and Track Record

Both brokers earn payment for order flow, meaning market makers pay them to handle your stock orders. Public offers an optional "Smart Order" route without PFOF for $0.003 per share. Robinhood says all market makers pay it rebates at the same rate.

Both are SIPC members, so securities are protected up to $500,000, including $250,000 in cash, if the broker fails. Robinhood adds excess coverage from Lloyd's of London with a $1 billion aggregate limit. Public clears through Apex.

Robinhood has a longer regulatory record. FINRA ordered it to pay about $70 million in fines and restitution in 2021, the SEC fined two Robinhood broker-dealers $45 million combined in January 2025, and FINRA ordered $26 million in fines plus $3.75 million in restitution in March 2025.

Who Should Choose Robinhood?

  • Investors who will pay $50 a year for Gold's cash yield and 3% IRA match
  • Beginners who want fractional shares from $1 (see our Robinhood review)
  • Traders who want futures, prediction markets or a free desktop platform
  • Savers with a 401(k) to roll over who want a 1% match without a subscription

Who Should Choose Public?

  • Investors who want interest on cash without paying for a plan
  • Options traders who want a per-contract rebate
  • People who want Treasuries and bonds in the same app
  • Investors who want AI-built custom indexes or direct indexing

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Robinhood or Public better for beginners?▼
It depends on whether you will pay for a plan. Public's free tier pays 3.30% APY on cash and has no subscription requirement for interest. Robinhood offers fractional shares from $1 instead of $5, and its Gold plan at $5 a month is the cheaper way to get a cash yield and a higher IRA match.
Is Robinhood Gold or Public Reserve worth it?▼
Robinhood Gold costs $50 a year and gives 3.60% APY on cash and a 3% IRA contribution match. Public Reserve costs $200 a year and gives 3.75% APY and a 4% match. On a $7,500 IRA contribution, Gold's match is $225 and Reserve's is $300, so Gold leaves more after its fee.
Does Public pay you for options trades?▼
Yes. Public pays a rebate of $0.06 to $0.18 per stock or ETF options contract, depending on your monthly trading volume. Public says the rebate shares its payment for order flow revenue with customers.
Do Robinhood and Public sell your orders?▼
Both receive payment for order flow, meaning market makers pay them to execute customer stock orders. Public offers an optional Smart Order route that does not receive payment for order flow, for $0.003 per share.
Are Robinhood and Public safe?▼
Both are SIPC members, so securities are protected up to $500,000, including $250,000 in cash, if the broker fails. Robinhood adds excess coverage from Lloyd's of London. SIPC does not protect against investment losses.
