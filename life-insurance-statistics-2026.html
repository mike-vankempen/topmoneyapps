Life Insurance Statistics 2026: Who Has Coverage and How Big the Gap Is

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 7 min read

General information, not financial advice. Ownership and coverage-gap figures are from the LIMRA and Life Happens 2026 Insurance Barometer Study; other sources are listed at the end.

About 52% of American adults have life insurance in 2026, according to the LIMRA and Life Happens 2026 Insurance Barometer Study. Another 38%, roughly 92 million adults, say they need coverage or more of it. Cost is the main reason people give for not buying, yet many guess at the price rather than checking. Below are the latest figures on who has coverage, the gap, workplace benefits, sales and how much insurers pay out.

How Many Americans Have Life Insurance?

About half of U.S. adults have some life insurance. The 2026 Insurance Barometer Study from LIMRA and Life Happens puts ownership at 52%, counting both individual policies and group coverage through work. That is up one point from 51% in 2025, and Life Happens describes ownership as steady.

Cash value life insurance, meaning permanent policies such as whole life that build a savings component, is much less common. The Federal Reserve's 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances found that 15.2% of families held cash value life insurance, down from 16.1% in 2022. Among families that had it, the median cash value was $12,000 in 2025.

The Life Insurance Coverage Gap

The "coverage gap" is the share of people who say they need life insurance, or more of it, than they have. In 2026, 38% of American adults said so, which LIMRA estimates at about 92 million people. Life Happens breaks that 38% into two groups: 29% who have no coverage but say they need it, and 9% who own a policy but say they need more.

  • Women: 41% of women say they need life insurance or more of it, about 50 million adults, according to LIMRA.
  • Current owners: 18% of people who already own life insurance say they do not have enough.
  • Trend: in 2025 the gap was 40%, down from 42% a year earlier and 45% before the pandemic, according to Life Happens.

The gap matters because many households have little cushion. In the 2026 study, 47% of Americans said they would have trouble paying living expenses within six months if their household's primary wage earner died. Only 29% said they would stay financially secure for more than two years. For context on savings cushions, see our emergency fund statistics.

Why People Don't Buy Life Insurance

Cost is the most common reason. These are the reasons adults gave for not having life insurance or not having more of it in the 2026 Barometer, as reported by Life Happens (people could pick more than one):

Many people are guessing at the price. In 2026, 46% said their estimate of what life insurance costs was a "gut feeling" or a "wild guess," and only 4% of consumers aged 30 and younger correctly priced a basic policy. In the 2025 study, healthy adults aged 18 to 30 overestimated the median cost of a $250,000, 20-year level term policy by about 10 to 12 times.

Knowledge is low overall. Only 32% of consumers consider themselves knowledgeable about life insurance. Among women, 25% feel very or extremely knowledgeable, compared with 38% of men.

Life Insurance Through Work

Group life insurance through an employer is the most common way people get covered. The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reports that 59% of private industry workers had access to life insurance plans in March 2026, compared with 84% of state and local government workers.

Access depends heavily on employer size. In March 2025, BLS found that 42% of private industry workers at establishments with fewer than 100 workers had access to life insurance, compared with 72% at establishments with 100 to 499 workers and 87% at those with 500 or more.

LIMRA's 2026 workplace fact sheet adds that 47% of insured Americans have life insurance through work, and 27% of insured Americans have only workplace coverage, with no individual policy. If a work policy is your only coverage, check what your plan says happens to it when you leave the job.

Life Insurance Sales Trends

Sales hit a record in 2025. LIMRA reports that U.S. individual life insurance new annualized premium rose 10% to $17.5 billion, and the number of policies sold rose 7%.

Term life is a small share of premium dollars but a large share of policies because each policy costs less. In the third quarter of 2026, term made up about 18% of new premium but 38% of policies sold, according to LIMRA. Total new annualized premium in that quarter was $4.5 billion, up 2% from a year earlier, while the number of policies sold rose 6%. Through the first three quarters of 2026, premium was up 2% and policy count up 4%. For how term and whole life differ, see our life insurance guide.

How Much Life Insurance Pays Out

The American Council of Life Insurers (ACLI) Life Insurers Fact Book 2025, which covers 2024 data, shows the size of the industry:

  • Coverage in force: about $22.0 trillion of life insurance was in force at the end of 2024, including about $14.1 trillion of individual coverage and $7.8 trillion of group coverage.
  • Death benefits: life insurers paid $89 billion to beneficiaries of policyholders who died during 2024.
  • Cash surrenders: cash surrender values paid on life policies rose 13.1% to $47 billion in 2024.
  • Number of companies: 711 life insurers were in business at year-end 2024.

Lost and Unclaimed Life Insurance

Some benefits go unclaimed because beneficiaries do not know a policy exists. The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) runs a Life Insurance Policy Locator that searches participating insurers for policies and annuities of a person who has died. Since it launched in November 2016 through July 31, 2026, the tool has matched about $17.0 billion in benefits, with more than 780,000 matches from more than 1.5 million search requests. Matched benefits rose by more than $3 billion since August 31, 2025.

What These Numbers Mean for You

The statistics point to a few practical checks. If you rely only on coverage through work, it may not follow you to your next job and may not be enough on its own. If you think life insurance is too expensive, get an actual quote before deciding, since surveys show people overestimate prices. Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage of Americans have life insurance?

52% of American adults have some life insurance in 2026, counting individual policies and coverage through work, according to the LIMRA and Life Happens Insurance Barometer Study. Ownership was 51% in 2025. Men are more likely to be covered (55%) than women (49%).

How many Americans need more life insurance?

38% of U.S. adults, about 92 million people, say they need life insurance or more coverage than they have, according to LIMRA's 2026 study. That includes 29% with no coverage who say they need it and 9% who own a policy but need more.

Why don't people buy life insurance?

Perceived cost is the top reason people give for not having life insurance or more of it, cited by 45% in the 2026 Barometer. Other reasons are other financial priorities (35%), confusion about what or how to buy (23%) and procrastination (23%). Many people overestimate the price: 46% say their cost estimate is a gut feeling or a wild guess.

What percentage of workers get life insurance through their job?

59% of private industry workers had access to employer life insurance plans in March 2026, compared with 84% of state and local government workers, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Access is much lower at small employers.

How much do life insurers pay out each year?

Life insurers paid $89 billion to beneficiaries of policyholders who died in 2024, according to the ACLI Life Insurers Fact Book 2025. About $22.0 trillion of life insurance coverage was in force at the end of 2024.
