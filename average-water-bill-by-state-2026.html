Average Water Bill by State (2026): Monthly Water and Sewer Cost

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 7 min read

State figures from the doxo 2026 state-by-state bill pay report; price trends from the BLS; usage data from the EPA. Checked November 2026.

The typical U.S. household pays about $88 a month ($1,056 a year) for water and sewer service, based on doxo's 2026 state-by-state bill pay report, which uses median (middle) bills. Maryland is the most expensive state at $244 a month and Alabama the cheapest at $53. Bills are rising faster than overall inflation: water, sewer and trash prices were up 4.8% in the year to September 2026, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

Key Takeaways

  • The median U.S. household water and sewer bill is $88 a month, or $1,056 a year, according to doxo's 2026 state-by-state report; 72% of households pay this bill.
  • Maryland has the highest median water and sewer bill at $244 a month, followed by Vermont ($168), Washington ($152), Wisconsin ($144) and Hawaii ($142), per doxo's 2026 report.
  • Alabama has the lowest median water and sewer bill at $53 a month, followed by South Carolina ($56), Colorado ($58) and Tennessee ($58).
  • The BLS consumer price index for water, sewer and trash collection services rose 4.8% from September 2025 to September 2026.
  • The average American uses 82 gallons of water a day at home, and household leaks can waste 9,400 gallons a year, according to the EPA.

Maryland's median bill is more than four times Alabama's. Each state's median reflects local utility rates and how much water households use. Because these are combined water and sewer bills, they also depend on what each utility charges for wastewater.

Water Bills by City

doxo's 2025 water and sewer report (data for the 12 months ending March 2025) ranked large cities. The most expensive were Milwaukee ($200 a month), Seattle ($162), San Jose ($150), San Francisco ($146) and San Diego ($144). The least expensive were Jacksonville ($50), Indianapolis ($60), Orlando ($63), Cleveland ($65) and San Antonio ($70). That report put the national median at $86 a month.

Looking only at drinking water (no sewer), Food & Water Watch found the average annual bill at the 500 largest U.S. community water systems was $531 in 2025, assuming 60,000 gallons of use a year. San Jose Water Company in California had the highest at $1,416, and Hempstead, New York, the lowest at $133.

Some states publish their own surveys. In North Carolina, the UNC Environmental Finance Center's 2026 data across 467 utilities show an average residential bill of $46.57 a month for water and $60.10 for wastewater, both at 5,000 gallons of use.

How Fast Water Bills Are Rising

  • 4.8% in a year. The BLS consumer price index for water and sewer and trash collection services rose 4.8% from September 2025 to September 2026, according to the September 2026 CPI release. BLS could not publish 12-month changes for October 2026 because October 2025 data were not collected during the lapse in federal appropriations.
  • 62% in a decade. Food & Water Watch found residential drinking water bills rose 62% on average between 2015 and 2025.
  • 5.1% in 2025. Bluefield Research's index of 50 U.S. cities found combined water and sewer bills rose 5.1% in 2025, a five-year high, as reported by WaterOnline.
  • Household spending. In the BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey, households spent $883 on "water and other public services" in 2025, up from $826 in 2024. That category also includes services such as trash collection.

How Much Water a Household Uses

The EPA's WaterSense program says each American uses an average of 82 gallons of water a day at home, and the average American family uses more than 300 gallons a day. Outdoor use accounts for more than 30% of household water use on average, and as much as 60% in dry regions.

How to Lower Your Water Bill

  1. Fix leaks. The EPA says the average family can waste 9,400 gallons a year through household leaks, and fixing them can save about 10% on water bills.
  2. Replace old toilets. The EPA estimates that WaterSense-labeled toilets can save a family 13,000 gallons and $130 in water costs a year.
  3. Swap the showerhead. WaterSense showerheads can cut the average family's water and electricity costs by $70 a year, according to the EPA.
  4. Water the lawn smarter. WaterSense irrigation controllers can use up to 30% less irrigation water, the EPA says.
  5. Run full loads. The EPA says ENERGY STAR clothes washers use about 30% less water than standard models.
