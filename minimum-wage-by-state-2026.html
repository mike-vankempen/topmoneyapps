Minimum Wage by State (2026): All 50 States & Federal Rate

📅 Updated August 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen 📊 Source: U.S. DOL

The federal minimum wage is still $7.25 an hour in 2026 — frozen since 2009 — but where you work changes everything: from $7.25 in 20 states to $17.95 in Washington, D.C. That's a $10.70 gap for the same hour of work, or about $22,000 a year full-time. Here's the minimum wage in all 50 states plus D.C., what it earns annually, and how far it really stretches. 📌 Key Insight

The same 40-hour week earns wildly different pay depending on the state line. A full-time worker at $7.25 in Texas or Mississippi makes $15,080 a year; the same hours in D.C. earn $37,336 — a $22,256 difference for identical work. And because the federal floor hasn't moved since 2009, its real value has fallen roughly 50% to inflation: the 1968 minimum wage, adjusted, was worth about $14.50/hr in today's dollars — nearly double the current federal rate.

Tipped Minimum Wage

The federal tipped minimum is $2.13 an hour — as long as tips bring a worker's total to at least $7.25. If tips fall short, the employer must make up the difference. Eight states (Alaska, California, Minnesota, Montana, Nevada, Oregon, Washington, and D.C.) ban the tip credit entirely, meaning tipped workers there earn the full state minimum before tips.

Minimum Wage vs. a Living Wage

Even the highest state minimums fall short of a living wage — the income needed to cover housing, food, transportation, and healthcare without assistance. The MIT Living Wage Calculator estimates a single adult needs roughly $17–$28 an hour depending on location. At $7.25, a full-time worker earns just above the poverty line for one person and falls below it with a single child. This gap is the core reason 30 states and D.C. now set their own higher minimums.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the federal minimum wage in 2026? ▼

$7.25 per hour, unchanged since July 2009 — the longest stretch without an increase in FLSA history. Full-time, that's about $15,080 a year. The federal tipped minimum is $2.13/hr, provided tips bring total pay to at least $7.25.

Which state has the highest minimum wage in 2026? ▼

Washington, D.C. at $17.95/hr, followed by Washington State ($17.13), Connecticut ($16.94), California ($16.90), and New York ($16.50 statewide). Seattle's $20.29 for large employers is the highest city rate in the country.

Which states have a $7.25 minimum wage? ▼

Twenty states use the $7.25 federal floor: Alabama, Georgia, Idaho, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, New Hampshire, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Wisconsin, and Wyoming. Five of them have no state law at all and default to federal.

How much is minimum wage per year? ▼

At $7.25 full-time (40 hrs × 52 weeks), $15,080 before taxes. At $15.00 it's $31,200; at D.C.'s $17.95, about $37,336. After FICA and minimal federal tax, take-home at $7.25 is roughly $13,500–$14,000.

Is minimum wage a living wage in 2026? ▼

Rarely. The MIT Living Wage Calculator estimates a single adult needs about $17–$28/hr depending on location. Even the highest state minimums trail local living-wage estimates once housing is included, and $7.25 falls short everywhere.
