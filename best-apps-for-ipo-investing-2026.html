With big AI names like Anthropic heading for the public market, "how do I get IPO shares?" is a hot question. The honest answer: IPO-price allocations are limited and never guaranteed, but a handful of apps make it possible for regular investors to request them. Our top picks are Robinhood and Moomoo for no-minimum IPO access, with SoFi, Webull, and E*TRADE close behind. Here's how they compare.

Key Takeaways

  • For most retail investors, Robinhood and Moomoo are the easiest ways to request IPO shares at the offer price, because both have no account minimum and a simple in-app IPO request flow.
  • Most of the apps here charge no commission to buy shares, whether at the IPO price or on the open market.

1. Robinhood — Best for Beginners Top Pick

$0 minimum · IPO Access program · commission-free

Robinhood's IPO Access is the simplest way for a first-timer to request shares at the IPO price: no account minimum, a clean in-app request flow, and it's widely available. Robinhood has also been named as a retail participant in recent high-profile IPO prospectuses. The one rule to know: a 30-day flipping guideline — selling IPO shares within 30 days can limit your access to future IPO Access deals. As always, allocation isn't guaranteed.

2. Moomoo — Best for Breadth of Access

$0 minimum · in-app IPO Center · fractional shares

Moomoo is frequently ranked first for IPO access: no minimum, an in-app IPO Center where you submit subscription requests, and fractional shares. Allocations still aren't guaranteed, but its breadth of deals and low barrier make it a strong primary or secondary app for IPO hunters.

3. SoFi — Best All-in-One

Low minimum · IPO investing in the SoFi app

SoFi folds IPO investing into its broader money app (banking, loans, investing), and has appeared as a retail participant in recent marquee IPOs. A good fit if you want IPO access alongside the rest of your finances in one place.

4. Webull & E*TRADE — Strong Runners-Up

Low/no minimum · active-trader tools

Webull opens IPO requests to account holders (you'll typically need around $100 in settled cash) and pairs them with advanced charting. E*TRADE (owned by Morgan Stanley) offers a new-issue hub with no minimum and an edge on deals its parent helps underwrite.

The honest truth about IPO allocation: for a hot IPO, even with the right app, your allocation may be small or zero. That's normal — demand vastly exceeds the shares set aside for retail. Many investors skip the allocation lottery entirely and just buy on the open market once the stock trades, using a limit order on the volatile first day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best app for IPO investing in 2026?

Robinhood and Moomoo for easy, no-minimum IPO requests; SoFi, Webull, and E*TRADE are strong low-minimum options. Fidelity and Schwab offer access too but often need higher balances.

How do I get IPO shares as a retail investor?

Fund a brokerage account, opt into its IPO program, read the prospectus, and submit a request. Allocation isn't guaranteed and is small for hot deals — most people who miss out just buy once the stock trades.

Does Robinhood offer IPO access?

Yes — IPO Access lets eligible users request shares at the IPO price with no account minimum. Note the 30-day flipping guideline. Allocation isn't guaranteed.

Can I buy IPO stock the day it lists?

Yes — once trading starts, any broker on that exchange lets you buy. Use a limit order on the volatile first day.

Do IPO brokers charge fees?

Most charge no commission to buy. Watch eligibility and allocation rules instead — they vary by broker and by deal.
