Average Credit Card Interest Rate (2026)

📅 Updated October 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen 📊 Source: Federal Reserve, WalletHub

The average credit card interest rate sits at about 22–23% APR in 2026 — near the highest level ever recorded.[1] At that rate, a balance you carry doesn't just linger; it compounds against you fast. 📊 Key insight

A 22% APR is why credit card debt is the single most urgent balance to kill — it's roughly triple a mortgage rate and quadruple a typical auto loan. Paying it off is a guaranteed 22% return, which no investment can reliably match. If you carry a balance, attacking it almost always beats investing the same dollars. And the only APR that never touches you is the one on a balance you clear in full each month.

How to pay less interest

  • Pay the statement balance in full every month — then the APR is irrelevant and your card is effectively free.
  • Ask for a lower APR. A quick call to your issuer works more often than people expect, especially with a history of on-time payments.
  • Use a 0% balance-transfer card to pause interest for 12–21 months and attack the principal.
  • Consolidate high-rate balances into a lower-rate personal loan.
  • Raise your credit score to qualify for better rates — see average credit score by age.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average credit card interest rate in 2026? ▼

About 22–23% APR — roughly 22.4% on new offers and ~21% across balances carried (Fed G.19 / WalletHub). Near record highs.

What is a good credit card APR? ▼

Below the ~22% average. Excellent credit averages ~17%, good ~23%, fair ~27%. Under 20% is solid; the best "rate" is clearing your balance so the APR never applies.

Why are credit card rates so high? ▼

Card APRs track the prime rate, which follows the Fed's benchmark. Rate hikes pushed APRs to record highs, and issuers price in the risk of unsecured debt.

How much does a balance cost in interest? ▼

On $5,000 at 22%, minimum payments can take 10+ years and cost $5,000+ in interest. Paying $250/month clears it in ~2 years with ~$1,200 interest.

How do I lower my rate? ▼

Pay in full monthly, call and ask for a lower APR, use a 0% balance-transfer card, consolidate into a personal loan, and improve your credit score.
