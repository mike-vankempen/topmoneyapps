States With No Income Tax (2026)

📅 Updated October 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen 📊 Tax data

Nine states charge no tax on your paycheck in 2026: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. Living in one can mean thousands more in take-home pay each year — but there's a catch. These states still need revenue, so they collect it through higher sales or property taxes instead. The catch: no income tax doesn't mean low taxes

Every state needs money, so the ones without an income tax raise it elsewhere — and the total bite varies a lot:

  • High property taxes: Texas and New Hampshire have some of the highest effective property-tax rates in the country, which hits homeowners hard.
  • High sales taxes: Tennessee and Washington carry some of the highest combined state-and-local sales-tax rates, so you pay more every time you spend.
  • Resource & tourism revenue: Alaska (oil), Wyoming (minerals), and Nevada/Florida (tourism/gaming) can keep the overall burden genuinely low because outsiders and industry foot much of the bill.

So the overall tax burden is truly low in places like Wyoming, Alaska, and Florida, but closer to average in a high-property-tax state like Texas once you own a home.

📊 Key insight

No-income-tax states are a clear win for high earners and people with large retirement or investment income — skipping a 5–9% state income tax on a big salary saves real money (a $100K earner leaving California keeps roughly $9,000 more). For a middle-income renter the math is murkier: higher sales and property taxes can quietly claw back much of the "savings." Compare your total tax burden and cost of living, not just the income-tax line.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which states have no income tax in 2026? ▼

Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. New Hampshire now has no income tax of any kind after its interest-and-dividends tax phased out in 2025.

Does Washington really have no income tax? ▼

No wage income tax, so yes for most people. But it levies a 7% tax on long-term capital gains above ~$270,000/year, which affects high earners selling investments.

How do these states make money? ▼

Higher sales taxes (TN, WA, NV), higher property taxes (TX, NH), and resource or tourism revenue (AK oil, WY minerals, FL/NV tourism).

Will I actually save money by moving? ▼

Often for high earners, not always for others. Higher sales/property taxes and cost of living can offset the income-tax savings. Compare total burden, not just income tax.

Is it better to live in a no-income-tax state? ▼

It most benefits high earners and those with big investment/retirement income. For others it can be a wash once sales and property taxes are factored in.
