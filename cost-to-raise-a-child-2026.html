Cost to Raise a Child (2026): ~$331,000 to Age 18

📅 Updated August 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen 📊 Source: USDA framework

Raising a child to age 18 costs a middle-income family about $331,000 in 2026 — roughly $18,400 a year, or $1,530 a month — and that's before a single college bill. It's one of the biggest financial commitments most people ever make, yet the costs are predictable enough to plan for. Here's where the money actually goes, how it shifts as kids grow, and how to get ahead of it. More in our Personal Finance Statistics Hub →

📌 Key Insight

The cost isn't spread evenly across childhood. The two most expensive stretches are the early years — when full-time childcare can run $10,000–$24,000 a year on its own — and the teen years, driven by food, a bigger transportation bill, and activities. The school-age years in between are usually the cheapest. Knowing this lets you front-load savings before a baby arrives, when childcare will hit hardest.

And Then There's College

The $331,000 figure stops at age 18. College is a separate, major cost — a four-year public in-state degree can add roughly $100,000, and private schools far more. The earlier you open a 529 plan, the more compounding does the heavy lifting. Even small monthly contributions from birth can cover a meaningful share of tuition by the time your child enrolls.

How to Financially Prepare

  • Build a bigger emergency fund — aim for 3–6 months of your new, higher expenses before the baby arrives.
  • Sort out health insurance and add the child to your plan promptly after birth.
  • Get term life insurance so your family is protected if something happens to you. See our life insurance guide.
  • Automate two savings streams — one for near-term costs like childcare, one long-term (a 529 for college).
  • Rework your budget for the added monthly load, and plan ahead for the childcare-heavy early years.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to raise a child in 2026? ▼

About $331,000 from birth to age 18 for a middle-income family — roughly $18,400/year or $1,530/month. That excludes college.

What is the biggest expense? ▼

Housing, at about 29% of the total. Food (18%), childcare and education (16%), and transportation (15%) follow, with healthcare around 9%.

How much does a child cost per year? ▼

About $18,400/year on average, but it's uneven: infancy/toddlerhood (childcare) and the teen years are priciest, while school-age years are cheapest.

Does the cost include college? ▼

No — it covers birth through 18 only. A four-year public in-state degree can add ~$100,000; private schools far more. A 529 plan started early helps.

How can I prepare financially? ▼

Build a bigger emergency fund, sort health insurance, get term life insurance, automate savings for both childcare and college, and rework your budget for the added load.
