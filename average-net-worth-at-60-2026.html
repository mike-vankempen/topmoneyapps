Average Net Worth at 60 (2026)

📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read 📊 Source: Federal Reserve (SCF)

📋 Source: Federal Reserve — Survey of Consumer Finances (households aged 55–64, latest release)

At 60, the average household net worth is about $1.57 million — but that number is misleading. The median (the true middle) is roughly $364,000, because a wealthy minority drags the average far above where most people actually stand. Key Takeaways

  • For households in the 55–64 age range (which covers age 60), the average (mean) net worth is about $1.57 million, based on the Federal Reserve's most recent Survey of Consumer Finances.
  • For the 55–64 age group, a net worth around $1 million sits near the 75th percentile — meaning roughly three-quarters of households that age have less.

Mean vs. Median: Why the Gap Matters

The $1.57M average and the $364K median describe the same group — so why the four-fold gap? Because net worth is extremely top-heavy. A relatively small number of very wealthy households pull the average way up, while the median (the person standing exactly in the middle) reflects a far more ordinary reality. If you're comparing yourself to anything, compare to the median — it's the honest benchmark for a typical 60-year-old.

The number that actually matters: not your percentile, but whether your net worth can fund your retirement. A common rule of thumb is to have roughly 6–8× your annual income saved by your early 60s — but your real target depends on your spending, your Social Security, and when you plan to stop working.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average net worth at age 60?

For ages 55–64, the average (mean) is about $1.57 million, but the median is about $364,000 — and the median is the realistic benchmark, since a wealthy minority skews the average up.

What is a good net worth at 60?

Beating the ~$364,000 median puts you ahead of half your peers; ~$1 million is around the top 25%. A rule of thumb is 6–8× your income saved by your early 60s — but what matters is whether it funds your retirement.

What percentile is $1 million at 60?

For ages 55–64, about $1 million sits near the 75th percentile. The top 10% starts around $2.5 million. Figures are approximate (Federal Reserve data).

Does net worth include retirement accounts and home equity?

Yes — net worth is everything you own minus everything you owe: 401(k)s, IRAs, brokerage, cash, and home equity, minus mortgage and other debts. Home equity and retirement accounts are usually the two biggest pieces at 60.

How can I catch up before retirement?

Use the larger over-50 and ages 60–63 catch-up limits, pay down high-interest debt, delay Social Security, and work a few extra years. Even modest changes compound fast over 5–10 years.
