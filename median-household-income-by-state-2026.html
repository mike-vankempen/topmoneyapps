Median Household Income by State (2026): All 50 Ranked

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 7 min read

State figures from the U.S. Census Bureau, Household Income in States and Metropolitan Areas: 2025 (American Community Survey 1-year estimates, released October 2026). Checked November 2026.

The median U.S. household earned $84,780 in 2025, up 1.2% from 2024 after inflation, according to the Census Bureau's American Community Survey released in October 2026. New Jersey ($107,797) and Maryland ($107,700) had the highest medians among states, and Mississippi ($59,346) had the lowest. The District of Columbia was higher than every state at $109,505.

Key Takeaways

  • The U.S. median household income was $84,780 in 2025, up 1.2% from $83,808 in 2024 after adjusting for inflation, according to the Census Bureau's 2025 American Community Survey.
  • New Jersey had the highest median household income of any state in 2025 at $107,797, followed by Maryland at $107,700; the District of Columbia was $109,505.
  • Mississippi had the lowest median household income of any state in 2025 at $59,346, followed by West Virginia at $61,340.
  • Median household income rose in 14 states from 2024 to 2025, fell only in California, and did not change significantly in 35 states and the District of Columbia.
  • The U.S. Gini index of income inequality was 0.482 in 2025; New York had the highest of any state (0.519) and Utah the lowest (0.435).

Mississippi had the lowest median household income of any state. The gap between New Jersey and Mississippi is $48,451 a year, which means the typical New Jersey household earns about 82% more than the typical Mississippi household.

Eight states had a median above $100,000 in 2025: New Jersey, Maryland, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Hawaii, California, Washington and Connecticut. The District of Columbia was also above that line. Colorado ($99,729), Virginia ($99,322) and Utah ($99,311) were just below that line.

How Household Income Changed From 2024 to 2025

  • U.S. median up 1.2%. The ACS median household income was $84,780 in 2025, up from $83,808 in 2024 after adjusting for inflation (in 2025 dollars). The Census Bureau calls the 1.2% increase statistically significant.
  • 14 states rose. Median household income increased in 14 states and Puerto Rico.
  • One state fell. California was the only state with a statistically significant decline.
  • The rest held steady. Changes in 35 states and the District of Columbia were not statistically significant.

The Census brief adds a caution: the 2025 survey includes some income reported for 2024, and new population-estimate methods were introduced, so year-to-year differences may partly reflect those changes rather than real shifts in income.

Income Inequality by State

The Gini index measures how evenly income is spread, on a scale from 0 (everyone has the same income) to 1 (one household has all the income). The U.S. Gini index was 0.482 in 2025, up from 0.481 in 2024, a small increase the Census Bureau says was statistically significant. New York had the highest Gini index of any state at 0.519, meaning income there is the least evenly spread. Utah had the lowest at 0.435. So a high median income and an even income spread do not always go together: New York's median ($89,210) is close to the national figure, while its inequality is the highest of any state.

Why You May See a Different National Number

The Census Bureau publishes two national median household income figures each September, and they come from different surveys:

Survey2025 U.S. medianBest used for
American Community Survey (ACS), 1-year$84,780Comparing states and metro areas
Current Population Survey (CPS ASEC)$87,460The Census Bureau's annual national income report

The CPS figure of $87,460 was up 2.6% from $85,210 in 2024 in real terms, and the Census Bureau says it was the highest on record since its series began in 1967. The ACS is the survey the Census Bureau uses for its state and metro-area income brief, which is the source of the state figures on this page. Use one survey at a time when you compare numbers, and do not mix a CPS national figure with ACS state figures.

What Your State's Median Means for You

A state median is a benchmark, not a target. It counts every household, from single renters to large families with two earners, so a household of one will often fall below it and a two-income household above it. It also says nothing about costs. Hawaii's median is the fifth highest among states, but prices there are high as well, so compare income with our cost of living by state and salary to live comfortably by state pages before drawing conclusions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the median household income in the U.S.?

The median U.S. household income was $84,780 in 2025, according to the Census Bureau's American Community Survey, up 1.2% from 2024 after inflation. The Census Bureau's other survey, the CPS, puts the 2025 median at $87,460.

Which state has the highest median household income?

New Jersey, at $107,797 in 2025, followed closely by Maryland at $107,700 and New Hampshire at $106,759. The District of Columbia, which is not a state, was higher at $109,505. The Census Bureau says the top figures are not statistically different from each other.

Which state has the lowest median household income?

Mississippi, at $59,346 in 2025, followed by West Virginia at $61,340, Louisiana at $65,139 and Arkansas at $65,588.

Why are there two different national median household income figures?

The Census Bureau runs two surveys. The American Community Survey gives $84,780 for 2025 and is used for state comparisons. The Current Population Survey gives $87,460 and is used in the Census Bureau's annual national income report. Compare figures from the same survey only.

Did household income go up in 2025?

Nationally, yes. The ACS median rose 1.2% after inflation from 2024 to 2025. By state, income rose in 14 states, fell only in California, and showed no statistically significant change in 35 states and the District of Columbia.
