What Is UNest?

UNest is a mobile app that lets parents open and fund an investment account for their child. Rather than a bank savings account earning next to nothing, UNest puts contributions into diversified, low-cost ETF portfolios so the money has a chance to grow over the 15–20 years until the child is an adult.

The account UNest opens is a custodial account under the Uniform Transfers to Minors Act (UTMA). The parent or guardian controls the account until the child reaches the age of majority in their state — usually 18 or 21 — at which point the assets become legally the child's. UNest has been named one of the best custodial account options by mainstream outlets and carries a strong app-store rating from hundreds of thousands of reviews.

UNest is available on iOS and Android. It's designed to be fast to set up and mostly hands-off: you choose a portfolio, set a monthly contribution, and let it run.

How UNest Works

Getting started is simple. You download the app, enter some basic details about yourself and your child, choose an investment portfolio based on how much risk you're comfortable with, and set an initial and recurring contribution amount. From there, UNest handles the investing automatically.

The gifting feature is where UNest separates itself from a plain brokerage custodial account. You get a shareable link that lets grandparents, aunts, uncles, and friends contribute directly to your child's account. Instead of another toy that gets forgotten, relatives can add to a fund that compounds for years. Gifts can be set up to recur, which is popular with grandparents who want to contribute every month.

Because it's a UTMA account, the funds are flexible: unlike a 529 plan that locks money into education expenses, UTMA money can go toward a first car, a deposit on an apartment, starting a business, or anything else that benefits the child once they come of age.

UNest Pricing & Fees

UNest is a subscription service, and this is the single most important thing to understand before signing up. As of late 2026, UNest advertises no fees on balances up to $300 for the first 12 months, after which it charges a low monthly subscription (around $2/month) plus a small annual fee on invested assets (roughly 0.25%).

UNest has changed its pricing more than once over the years — earlier members may be grandfathered onto older plans, and some past reviews quote different monthly figures. Always confirm the current price inside the app before you commit.

Here's the honest math: a flat monthly fee is a larger percentage drag when your balance is small. On a $500 balance, $2/month is about 4.8% a year — high. On a $10,000 balance, that same $2/month is only about 0.24% — very reasonable. UNest makes the most sense once you're contributing regularly and the balance grows into the thousands, where the fee becomes a rounding error relative to the convenience and gifting features.

Who Should Use UNest

UNest is a good fit if you are:

  • A busy parent who wants to start investing for a child without the hassle of setting up a brokerage account
  • Someone who wants family — especially grandparents — to contribute easily via a shared gifting link
  • A fan of "set it and forget it" automated monthly investing
  • Looking for flexibility — a fund that can be used for more than just college

UNest is probably not for you if you are:

  • A cost-focused investor who would happily open a free custodial account at a major brokerage
  • Saving specifically and only for college — a 529 plan usually offers better tax treatment
  • Starting with a very small balance where a flat monthly fee is a large percentage drag
  • Uncomfortable with the child gaining full control of the money at 18 or 21 (a feature of all UTMA accounts)

Frequently Asked Questions

Is UNest worth it?
It depends on what you value. UNest is worth it for parents who want a simple, automated way to invest for a child and to collect gifts from family. The tradeoff is cost — UNest charges a subscription fee plus a small annual asset fee, while you can open the same type of custodial account free at a major brokerage. If convenience and gifting matter more than squeezing out every dollar of fees, UNest is reasonable.
What type of account is UNest?
UNest opens a custodial UTMA account (Uniform Transfers to Minors Act). The parent manages it until the child reaches the age of majority in their state — usually 18 or 21 — when the assets become the child's. Unlike a 529 plan, UTMA funds can be used for anything that benefits the child, not just education.
How much does UNest cost?
UNest currently advertises no fees on balances up to $300 for the first 12 months, then a low monthly subscription (around $2/month) plus a small annual fee on invested assets (roughly 0.25%). Pricing has changed over time and earlier members may be on different legacy plans, so always confirm the current price in the app before signing up.
How is UNest different from a 529 plan?
A 529 plan is tax-advantaged but education-only — withdrawals are tax-free only for qualified education expenses. UNest uses a UTMA custodial account, which is far more flexible: the money can be used for anything that benefits the child. The tradeoff is fewer tax advantages than a 529 and a bigger potential impact on financial aid.
Can family members contribute to a UNest account?
Yes — this is one of UNest's best features. You can share a link so grandparents, relatives, and friends contribute directly to your child's account for birthdays and holidays, and gifts can be set to recur automatically. It's a major reason parents pick UNest over a plain brokerage custodial account.
What happens to a UNest account when my child grows up?
Because it's a UTMA custodial account, control transfers to the child at the age of majority in their state (typically 18 or 21), and the assets become legally theirs to use as they wish. Be comfortable with that before choosing a custodial account over vehicles where the parent keeps control.
