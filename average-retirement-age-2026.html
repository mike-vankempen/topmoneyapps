Average Retirement Age in the U.S. (2026)

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 7 min read

General information, not financial advice. Figures are from Gallup (May 2026), the 2026 Retirement Confidence Survey, BLS and the Social Security Administration.

The average American retiree stopped working at age 61, according to Gallup's 2026 poll, and the 2026 Retirement Confidence Survey puts the median at 62. Workers expect to retire later: at 66 on average (Gallup) or a median of 65 (EBRI). Nearly half of retirees, 46%, say they retired earlier than planned, most often because of health problems or changes at work. Here is what each major data source shows.

Key Takeaways

  • Retirees say they retired at age 61 on average, while nonretirees expect to retire at 66, according to Gallup's April 2026 Economy and Personal Finance poll.
  • The median age at which retirees say they retired is 62, and workers' median expected retirement age is 65, according to the 2026 EBRI Retirement Confidence Survey.
  • 46% of retirees retired earlier than planned in 2026, up from 40% in 2025, and 41% of early retirees cited a health problem or disability (EBRI).
  • 19.1% of Americans 65 and older were in the labor force in 2025, up from 12.9% in 2000, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
  • Among people awarded Social Security retired-worker benefits in 2025, the average age was 65.2 for men and 65.0 for women (SSA).

Average Retirement Age: What Each Source Says

There is no single official "average retirement age." Surveys ask retirees when they stopped working, researchers look at when people leave the labor force, and the Social Security Administration (SSA) tracks when people start benefits. Each method gives a slightly different answer:

The Center for Retirement Research at Boston College defines the average retirement age as the age at which the labor force participation rate for a group drops below 50%, using Current Population Survey data. By that measure, men's retirement age was 64.6 in 2024, three years later than in 1994, and women's rose from about 55 in the 1960s to 62.6. The SSA figures are claiming ages, which are not the same as stopping work: some people claim while still working, and others stop working years before they claim.

Expected vs. Actual Retirement Age

People consistently retire earlier than they plan to. In Gallup's April 2026 poll, nonretirees expected to retire at 66 on average, five years later than the average age current retirees said they actually retired. In 2025 the gap was even wider: retirees reported 62 and nonretirees expected 68.

Gallup says retirees' actual retirement age has stayed in a narrow range of 59 to 62 since 2002, while nonretirees' expected age has risen from 63 in 2002 and peaked at 68 in 2025.

The Employee Benefit Research Institute (EBRI) and Greenwald Research found a similar pattern in their 2026 Retirement Confidence Survey. Workers' median expected retirement age held at 65, but three in five retirees said they retired before 65, with a median of 62. Almost half of workers expect a gradual transition into retirement, yet three in four retirees said they stopped working entirely.

Why People Retire Earlier Than Planned

In the 2026 EBRI survey, 46% of retirees said they retired earlier than planned, up from 40% in 2025. Another 48% retired about when they planned and 6% later. Among those who left early, the most common reasons were (respondents could pick more than one):

EBRI says 76% of early retirees gave at least one reason outside their control, up from 68% in 2025. Health problems rose to 41% from 31% the year before. The lesson for planning: a target retirement age is not guaranteed, so a plan that only works if you keep earning until 67 has little room for error.

How Many Older Americans Still Work

More people are working past 65 than a generation ago. The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reports that 19.1% of people 65 and older were in the labor force in 2025, meaning working or looking for work. That is down from a recent peak of 20.2% in 2019 but up from 12.9% in 2000.

BLS projections show the oldest workers growing fastest. The civilian labor force age 75 and older is projected to rise from about 2.2 million in 2025 to 3.7 million in 2035, and the 65-to-74 group from about 9.6 million to 10.7 million.

Social Security and Medicare Ages That Shape Retirement

Three government ages drive many retirement decisions:

  • 62: the earliest you can start Social Security retirement benefits. If your full retirement age is 67, claiming at 62 cuts your monthly benefit by 30%, according to the SSA.
  • 65: Medicare eligibility for most people. Medicare.gov describes Medicare as health insurance for people 65 or older who meet citizenship or residency requirements.
  • 67: the Social Security full retirement age for anyone born in 1960 or later. See our full retirement age by birth year table for earlier birth years.

Waiting past full retirement age adds delayed retirement credits of 8% a year, up to age 70, for people born in 1943 or later. In practice, few people wait that long. Among people awarded Social Security retired-worker benefits in 2025, 22.3% of men and 23.8% of women started at 62, while 7.6% of men and 7.0% of women started at 70 or older, according to the SSA's 2026 Annual Statistical Supplement. For how claiming age changes your check, see the average Social Security check by claiming age.

How Long Retirement Lasts

Your retirement age matters because it sets how many years your savings must cover. Based on the SSA's 2023 period life table, a 65-year-old man has a life expectancy of about 18.1 more years and a 65-year-old woman about 20.7 more years. At 62, those figures are about 20.3 years for men and 23.1 years for women. These are averages, so many people live longer than them.

Retiring at 62 instead of 67 adds five years without a paycheck and, if you claim Social Security early, locks in a smaller benefit. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average retirement age in the U.S.?

Retirees say they retired at 61 on average, according to Gallup's 2026 poll, and the 2026 EBRI Retirement Confidence Survey puts the median at 62. The Center for Retirement Research estimates 64.6 for men and 62.6 for women in 2024, based on when labor force participation drops below 50%.

At what age do most people expect to retire?

Nonretirees expect to retire at 66 on average, according to Gallup's 2026 poll. Workers in the 2026 EBRI Retirement Confidence Survey have a median expected retirement age of 65.

Why do people retire earlier than planned?

In the 2026 EBRI survey, 46% of retirees retired earlier than planned. The top reasons were a health problem or disability (41%), being able to afford it (36%) and changes at the company such as downsizing or closure (35%). 76% gave at least one reason outside their control.

What is the earliest age you can retire with Social Security?

How many people over 65 still work?

19.1% of Americans 65 and older were in the labor force in 2025, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, up from 12.9% in 2000. Participation was 38.1% for men and 28.9% for women aged 65 to 69.
