How to Roll Over a 401(k): Steps, Rules and Taxes

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 8 min read

General information, not tax or investment advice. Confirm details with the IRS and your plan or account provider.

To roll over a 401(k), open the account you want the money to go to, then ask your old plan for a direct rollover so the money moves straight from the plan to the new account. A direct rollover has no tax withheld and no 60-day deadline to worry about. If the plan pays you instead, it must withhold 20%, and you have 60 days to deposit the full amount or part of it becomes taxable. Here are the steps and the IRS rules.

Key Takeaways

  • In a direct rollover, your plan pays the money straight to another retirement plan or IRA, and no tax is withheld (IRS).
  • If a 401(k) distribution is paid to you, the plan must withhold 20%, even if you intend to roll it over, and you have 60 days to complete the rollover.
  • To roll over the full amount after withholding, you must replace the withheld 20% with other money; otherwise that portion is taxable and may face the 10% additional tax.
  • The IRS limit of one IRA-to-IRA rollover per 12 months does not apply to rollovers from a 401(k) or other plan to an IRA, or to trustee-to-trustee transfers.
  • Required minimum distributions, hardship distributions and loans treated as distributions cannot be rolled over.

Your Options for an Old 401(k)

When you leave a job, you generally have four choices for the money in that employer's 401(k). Each has trade-offs:

OptionProsCons
Leave it in the old planNo paperwork; keeps current investmentsLimited to that plan's funds; small balances may be moved or cashed out by the plan
Roll it into your new employer's planKeeps everything in one workplace accountThe new plan must accept rollovers; limited to its fund menu
Roll it into a traditional IRAYou pick the provider and investments; no tax if done correctlyYou choose and manage the investments yourself
Roll it into a Roth IRAFuture qualified withdrawals tax-freePre-tax money moved to a Roth is generally taxable in the year you do it
Cash it outMoney in hand nowTaxable as income; 20% withheld; may face 10% additional tax before 59½

About leaving it in place: the IRS says that if you do not make an election, a plan may roll a balance between $1,000 and $5,000 into an IRA for you, and may pay out a balance of $1,000 or less, usually with 20% withheld. Check your plan's rules if your balance is small.

Direct vs. Indirect (60-Day) Rollovers

There are two ways the money can move:

  • Direct rollover: you ask the plan administrator to pay the distribution directly to another retirement plan or to an IRA. No tax is withheld. A check made payable to the receiving plan or IRA also counts and is not subject to withholding.
  • Indirect (60-day) rollover: the plan pays the money to you, and you deposit all or part of it into an IRA or another plan within 60 days. The plan must withhold 20% of the distribution, even if you intend to roll it over.

The IRS also describes trustee-to-trustee transfers, which move money directly from one IRA to another. These are not counted as rollovers for the one-per-year limit.

How to Roll Over a 401(k): Step by Step

  1. Decide where the money goes. Use the table above. If your old account holds both pre-tax and Roth 401(k) money, plan where each part will go.
  2. Open the receiving account. If you are moving to an IRA, open it first so you have the account number and the provider's rollover instructions. Our best apps for retirement and IRA investing page compares providers and their fees.
  3. Contact your old plan administrator. Ask for a direct rollover. The IRS says plan administrators must give you a written explanation of your rollover options, and must offer a direct transfer for eligible distributions of $200 or more.
  4. Give the payment instructions. Have the money sent electronically or by a check payable to the new provider for your benefit, not to you personally. That keeps it a direct rollover with no withholding.
  5. Confirm the money arrived and invest it. A rollover moves money; it does not choose investments. Check that the deposit posted to the right account type and pick your investments.
  6. Keep your records. Save the confirmation from both providers and the year-end tax forms they send, in case you need to show the money was rolled over.

The 20% Withholding Trap

If you take an indirect rollover, the 20% withholding can cost you. The IRS gives this example: you receive a $10,000 distribution from your plan, and $2,000 is withheld, so you get a check for $8,000.

What you deposit within 60 daysResult
$8,000 (the check only)$2,000 is taxable as income, and may face the 10% additional tax if you are under 59½ and no exception applies
$10,000 ($8,000 + $2,000 from savings)Full amount rolled over; nothing is taxed as a distribution

The way to avoid this entirely is to ask for a direct rollover from the start.

Key IRS Rules

  • 60-day deadline: you have 60 days from the date you receive a distribution to roll it over. The IRS may waive the deadline if you missed it because of circumstances beyond your control.
  • One rollover per 12 months, for IRAs only: you can make only one IRA-to-IRA rollover in any 12-month period, counting all of your IRAs. The IRS says this limit does not apply to rollovers from a plan to an IRA, plan-to-plan rollovers, trustee-to-trustee transfers, or conversions from traditional to Roth IRAs.
  • What cannot be rolled over: required minimum distributions, loans treated as distributions, hardship distributions, excess contributions and their earnings, and substantially equal periodic payments, among others listed by the IRS.
  • Early withdrawals: money you do not roll over is taxable (other than qualified Roth distributions and amounts already taxed) and may face the 10% additional tax on early distributions unless an exception applies.

Rolling a 401(k) Into a Roth IRA

Moving pre-tax 401(k) money into a Roth IRA is a Roth conversion. Schwab notes that the converted amount is generally taxable in the year of the conversion and can affect other parts of your tax picture, such as deductions, credits and Medicare premiums. A large conversion can push part of your income into a higher bracket; check the 2026 tax brackets before you decide. Fidelity notes that each conversion has its own 5-year clock for penalty-free withdrawal of the converted amount if you are under 59½. For more on the trade-off, see Roth IRA vs. traditional IRA.

Plan for the tax bill: if you convert, pay the tax from money outside the account if you can. Paying it out of the rollover itself means less goes into the Roth, and money not rolled over may be taxed and penalized.

Before You Roll Over

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to roll over a 401(k)?

If the money is paid to you, you have 60 days from the date you receive it to deposit it in an IRA or another plan. There is no 60-day clock with a direct rollover, because the money goes straight from your old plan to the new account. The IRS may waive the deadline if you missed it because of circumstances beyond your control.

Do I pay taxes when I roll over a 401(k)?

Not if you roll pre-tax money into a traditional IRA or another employer plan and complete the rollover correctly. Money you do not roll over is generally taxable and may face a 10% additional tax before age 59½. Moving pre-tax money into a Roth IRA is generally taxable in the year you do it.

What is the difference between a direct and an indirect rollover?

In a direct rollover, your plan pays the money straight to another plan or IRA and no tax is withheld. In an indirect rollover, the plan pays you, withholds 20%, and you have 60 days to deposit the money; you must replace the withheld 20% from other funds to roll over the full amount.

Can I roll a 401(k) into a Roth IRA?

Yes. Rolling pre-tax 401(k) money into a Roth IRA is a Roth conversion, and the converted amount is generally taxable in the year of the conversion. Each conversion also has its own 5-year clock for penalty-free withdrawal of the converted amount if you are under 59½.

Does the one-rollover-per-year rule apply to 401(k) rollovers?

No. The IRS limit of one rollover per 12 months applies to IRA-to-IRA rollovers. It does not apply to rollovers from a 401(k) or other plan to an IRA, plan-to-plan rollovers, trustee-to-trustee transfers, or traditional-to-Roth IRA conversions.
