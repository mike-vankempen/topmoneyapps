What Is a Good Credit Score in 2026?

📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ Mike Van Kempen

📋 Based on the FICO scoring model (300–850). VantageScore uses the same range with similar bands.

On the FICO scale of 300–850, a "good" credit score is 670 or higher. Climb to 740+ and you're in "very good" territory; 800+ is "exceptional." The average US score sits around 715 — already in the good range. Here's the full breakdown of what each band means, what actually moves your score, and the fastest ways to raise it.

Why the bands matter: lenders price risk by score. Crossing from "fair" into "good," or from "good" into "very good," can meaningfully lower the interest rate on a mortgage, auto loan, or card — sometimes saving tens of thousands of dollars over time.

How to Improve Your Credit Score

  1. Pay every bill on time. Payment history is the single biggest factor. Set autopay for at least the minimum so you never miss a due date.
  2. Lower your utilization. Keep card balances under 30% of your limit — ideally under 10%. This updates monthly, so paying a card down can lift your score within a cycle or two.
  3. Don't close your oldest cards. Length of history helps. Keep old accounts open (and used lightly) to preserve your average account age.
  4. Apply for new credit sparingly. Each hard inquiry dings your score slightly. Space out applications.
  5. Check your reports for errors. Pull your free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com and dispute any mistakes — errors are common and can cost you points.

High balances dragging you down? Credit utilization is 30% of your score. Paying off high-interest card debt is the rare move that both raises your score and saves you money — see the average credit-card interest rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good credit score in 2026?

On the FICO scale (300–850), 670+ is "good", 740–799 is "very good", and 800+ is "exceptional." Good or better qualifies you for most loans at competitive rates.

What is the average credit score in the US?

Around 715–718 in recent years — already in the "good" range. Scores rise with age as histories lengthen and balances fall.

What credit score do I need to buy a house?

Conventional loans often want 620+, FHA can go to ~580 with 3.5% down, but the best rates go to 740+. A higher score can save tens of thousands over a mortgage.

What affects my score the most?

Payment history (~35%) and credit utilization (~30%) together are nearly two-thirds. Paying on time and keeping balances low are the highest-impact habits.

How can I raise my score fast?

Pay down card balances (lower utilization), never miss a payment, keep old accounts open, and dispute report errors. Utilization updates monthly, so results can show in a cycle or two.
