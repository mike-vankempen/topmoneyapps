Income Percentiles 2026: Are You Top 1%, 10%, or 50%?

📅 Updated August 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen 📊 Source: Census / DQYDJ

Ever wondered exactly where your income ranks? In 2026, a household needs about $231,000 to crack the top 10% — and a jaw-dropping $630,000 to reach the top 1%. Meanwhile the median household earns $81,600, so if you're above that, you're already in the top half. 📌 Key Insight

The jump from "comfortable" to "rich" is brutally steep. Going from the top 10% ($231K household) to the top 1% ($630K) means nearly tripling your income — yet the gap from the median ($81.6K) to the top 10% is much smaller. That's the shape of income inequality: the ladder gets exponentially steeper at the very top. And remember, income isn't wealth — plenty of high earners have little saved, while modest earners can build real net worth over time.

Income Isn't the Whole Story

Where you rank on income says nothing about what you keep. Two households earning $150,000 can be worlds apart depending on savings rate, debt, and cost of living. A top-10% income in Mississippi buys a very different life than the same income in Hawaii or California. Frequently Asked Questions

What income puts you in the top 1% in 2026? ▼

About $630,000 in household income, or roughly $350,000 for an individual. It's far higher in wealthy states (over $900K in Connecticut) and lower in states like West Virginia and Mississippi.

What income is the top 10%? ▼

About $231,000 for a household and ~$135,000 for an individual. The top 5% takes roughly $295,000 (household) or $185,000 (individual).

What is the median household income in 2026? ▼

About $81,600 — half of households earn more, half earn less. Above that puts you in the top 50%. Median individual income is lower (~$45,000) since many households have two earners.

Household vs. individual percentiles — which do I use? ▼

Use household figures to rate your whole household's combined income, and individual figures to rate just your own salary. Household thresholds are higher because many homes have multiple earners.

Does the top 1% threshold vary by state? ▼

A lot. The national top-1% household threshold is ~$630K, but it exceeds $900K in Connecticut, Massachusetts, and New Jersey and can be under $400K in West Virginia and Mississippi.
