Average Grocery Bill Per Month (2026)

📅 Updated September 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen 📊 Source: USDA Food Plans

Groceries are one of the few big expenses you can actually move week to week — which is why knowing the benchmark matters. In 2026, a single adult spends about $430 a month on the USDA's moderate plan, while a family of four runs about $1,380. 📌 Key Insight

These are home-cooking budgets — the USDA plans assume nearly every meal is made from scratch. The moment you add takeout, delivery, and convenience foods, your total food spending jumps well past the "grocery" number. That's why so many people feel over budget: they're comparing their all-in food spending to a groceries-only benchmark. Track groceries and eating-out separately, and you'll quickly see where the money actually goes.

How to Lower Your Grocery Bill

  • Plan meals and shop with a list — impulse buys are where budgets break.
  • Buy store brands — often 20–30% cheaper than name brands for the same quality.
  • Cook at home more — the single biggest swing between a moderate and a blown budget.
  • Shop sales and buy staples in bulk, and use cash-back or loyalty apps.
  • Cut food waste — use what you have before it spoils; wasted food is wasted money.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average grocery bill per month in 2026? ▼

About $430/month for a single adult and $1,380 for a family of four on the USDA moderate plan (roughly $345 and $1,119 on the low-cost plan). These assume mostly home-cooked meals.

How much should a family of four spend? ▼

USDA plans range from ~$1,013 (thrifty) to ~$1,668 (liberal), with moderate at ~$1,380. Most families land around $1,100–$1,400/month.

How much for one person? ▼

About $345 (low-cost) to $430 (moderate) per month. Since buying for one is less efficient, the USDA suggests adding ~20%, so ~$415–$515 is realistic.

Why is my grocery bill higher than average? ▼

USDA plans assume scratch cooking. Convenience foods, brand names, organic/specialty items, and high-cost cities push you above the benchmark — and takeout/delivery isn't even counted as groceries.

How can I lower my grocery bill? ▼

Plan meals and use a list, buy store brands, cook at home more, shop sales and bulk staples, use cash-back apps, and cut food waste.
