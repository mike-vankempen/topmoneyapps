Sales Tax by State (2026): Rates for All 50 States

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 7 min read

Rates from the Tax Foundation, State and Local Sales Tax Rates, Midyear 2026 (rates as of July 1, 2026), cross-checked against its January 2026 edition. Checked November 2026.

Louisiana has the highest combined state and local sales tax rate in 2026 at 10.13%, followed by Tennessee (9.61%) and Washington (9.57%), according to the Tax Foundation's midyear 2026 data. Five states have no statewide sales tax: Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon. California has the highest statewide rate at 7.25%, and the population-weighted national average combined rate is 7.53%.

  • No state changed its statewide sales tax rate between January and July 2026, but local changes raised combined rates in states including North Carolina, Georgia and Washington.

Louisiana has the highest combined rate even though its state rate is a moderate 5%. Its local rates average 5.13%, the second highest after Alabama's 5.46%. Tennessee is the reverse: a high 7% state rate and lower local rates. The Tax Foundation's population-weighted national average combined rate is 7.53%.

A low rate does not always mean a low tax bill. Hawaii's 4.5% combined rate looks low, but the Tax Foundation describes Hawaii as having the broadest sales tax in the country, meaning it applies to more goods and services than most states. Hawaii, New Mexico and South Dakota also tax many business-to-business services.

States With No Sales Tax

Five states have no statewide sales tax: Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon. Of these, only Alaska lets local governments charge a sales tax. Alaska's local rates average 1.82%, with a maximum local rate of 7.85%, so you can still pay sales tax in many Alaska towns. Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have a 0% combined rate in the Tax Foundation table.

In all, 45 states and the District of Columbia have a statewide sales tax, and 38 states allow local sales taxes. If you are weighing a move, remember that sales tax is only one part of the picture. Some no-sales-tax states lean on income or property taxes instead. Compare with our states with no income tax and cost of living by state guides.

Highest Statewide Rates

Looking only at the state-level rate, California's 7.25% is the highest. Four states tie for second at 7%: Indiana, Mississippi, Rhode Island and Tennessee. Colorado has the lowest statewide rate among states that have one, at 2.9%, but its local rates average 4.99%, which brings its combined rate to 7.89%. Five states charge a 4% state rate: Alabama, Georgia, Hawaii, New York and Wyoming.

What Changed in 2026

No state changed its statewide sales tax rate between January and July 2026, according to the Tax Foundation. Vertex, a tax software company, counted zero state rate changes in the first half of 2026 but 463 rate changes and new rates in all through June 30, including 85 county, 186 city and 24 special district rate changes. One change affects what you pay rather than the rate: effective January 1, 2026, Illinois eliminated its 1% state sales tax on food items, but many local governments chose to impose their own local taxes on food. Rules on what is taxed, such as groceries, clothing or digital goods, vary widely by state, so check your state revenue department's site for exemptions.

How Sales Tax Fits Your Budget

Sales tax adds up on large purchases. At Louisiana's 10.13% combined rate, a $1,000 purchase costs $1,101.30. At Hawaii's 4.5%, it costs $1,045. For budgeting, it helps to track spending after tax rather than sticker prices. A budgeting app that imports card transactions does that automatically, since your statements already include tax. Frequently Asked Questions

Which state has the highest sales tax?

Louisiana has the highest combined state and average local sales tax rate at 10.13% as of July 1, 2026, according to the Tax Foundation. Tennessee (9.61%), Washington (9.57%), Arkansas (9.48%) and Alabama (9.46%) follow.

Which states have no sales tax?

Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have no statewide sales tax. Alaska lets local governments charge sales tax, averaging 1.82%, so only Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have a 0% combined rate.

What state has the highest statewide sales tax rate?

California, at 7.25%, which includes a mandatory 1.25% statewide local add-on. Indiana, Mississippi, Rhode Island and Tennessee are next at 7%.

What is the average sales tax rate in the U.S.?

The population-weighted average combined state and local sales tax rate is 7.53%, according to the Tax Foundation's midyear 2026 report.

Did any state change its sales tax rate in 2026?

No state changed its statewide rate between January and July 2026. Local changes raised combined rates in North Carolina, Georgia, Washington, California and Louisiana, and Wyoming's combined rate fell after several localities cut their rates.
