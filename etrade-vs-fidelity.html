E*TRADE vs. Fidelity (2026): Which Broker Is Better?

📅 Updated November 27, 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen

Fidelity is the better choice for most investors, and E*TRADE is the better choice for frequent options and futures traders. Both charge $0 for online U.S. stock and ETF trades, but Fidelity pays far more on uninvested cash, sells fractional shares from $1 and charges no transfer-out fee, while E*TRADE discounts options to $0.50 a contract for active traders. Here is how they compare on fees, cash, margin, accounts and safety.

Key Takeaways

  • Fidelity and E*TRADE both charge $0 commission on online U.S. stock and ETF trades and have no account minimum.
  • Fidelity's default money market fund, SPAXX, had a 3.61% 7-day yield on November 25, 2026; E*TRADE's default cash sweep paid 0.01%–0.15%, per NerdWallet.
  • Options cost $0.65 per contract at both, but E*TRADE drops to $0.50 per contract for customers who make 30 or more trades a quarter.
  • Fidelity sells fractional shares from $1 and charges $0 to transfer out; E*TRADE's fractional minimum is $5 and a full transfer out costs $75.
  • Fidelity has a TopMoneyApps score of 4.93/5 and E*TRADE 4.87/5.

E*TRADE vs. Fidelity: Quick Verdict

Fidelity ranks higher in our investing rankings, with a TopMoneyApps score 4.93/5 versus TopMoneyApps score 4.87/5 for E*TRADE. Both charge $0 for online U.S. stock and ETF trades and have no account minimum, so the choice comes down to what you do with your account.

Choose Fidelity if you hold cash in your brokerage account, want fractional shares from $1, or want an HSA, 529 or a teen account at the same firm. Fidelity's default money market fund, SPAXX, had a 7-day yield of 3.61% as of November 25, 2026, and Fidelity charges $0 to transfer your account out.

Choose E*TRADE if you trade options often or want futures. E*TRADE cuts its options fee to $0.50 per contract once you place 30 or more trades a quarter, and it offers futures at $1.50 per contract per side. Its parent, Morgan Stanley, also offers banking products through Morgan Stanley Private Bank.

⭐ Our Verdict

For most long-term investors, Fidelity is the stronger all-around pick: lower fractional-share minimum, a much higher default yield on uninvested cash, lower margin rates on small balances and no transfer-out fee. E*TRADE is the better fit for active options and futures traders who will use its volume discount and Power E*TRADE platform.

Trading Fees

Both brokers charge $0 commission on online U.S. stock and ETF trades. Options are where they differ. Fidelity charges $0.65 per contract, and buy-to-close orders priced at $0.65 or less are free. E*TRADE also starts at $0.65 per contract, but drops to $0.50 if you make 30 or more trades in a quarter. On 100 contracts a month, that discount saves you $15 a month.

Some fees only matter in specific cases:

  • Broker-assisted trades: E*TRADE adds $25 when a representative places a trade for you.
  • Mutual funds: Fidelity's own funds have no transaction fee. Non-Fidelity no-transaction-fee funds are free to buy but cost $49.95 if you sell within 60 days, and transaction-fee funds cost $49.95 per purchase. E*TRADE lists more than 6,000 mutual funds, and NerdWallet counts nearly 6,000 without transaction fees.
  • Leaving the broker: Fidelity charges $0 to transfer assets out. E*TRADE charges $75 for a full outgoing transfer.
  • Small balances: Fidelity may charge a quarterly fee of up to $5 on leftover balances under $100.

Cash: Where Your Uninvested Money Earns More

This is the biggest practical difference for buy-and-hold investors. At Fidelity, uninvested cash sits in a money market fund that pays a market yield. SPAXX had a 7-day yield of 3.61% as of November 25, 2026.

At E*TRADE, the default sweep for uninvested brokerage cash paid between 0.01% and 0.15%, according to NerdWallet's July 2026 review. On $10,000 of idle cash, that's the difference between roughly $361 and $15 a year or less. To earn more at E*TRADE, you move cash into a separate account: E*TRADE's homepage advertised a 4.25% APY boosted rate on Premium Savings for six months for offers ending November 30, 2026. The rate after the boost was not shown. APY (annual percentage yield) is the yearly return including compounding; see APR vs. APY.

Margin Rates

Margin lets you borrow against your investments, and both brokers charge high rates on small loans. Fidelity's base rate was 10.825%, effective September 18, 2026, which works out to 12.075% on debit balances under $25,000 and 11.575% from $25,000 to $49,999. E*TRADE's base rate was 10.20% as of September 21, 2026, per StockBrokers.com, with 12.70% under $10,000, 12.45% from $10,000 to $24,999.99 and 12.20% from $25,000 to $49,999.99. Either way, borrowing at more than 11% is expensive.

Accounts and Investments

Fidelity

  • Retirement and tax-advantaged accounts: traditional, Roth, SEP and SIMPLE IRAs, a solo 401(k), an HSA and 529 plans (per NerdWallet). See HSA vs. FSA.
  • Teens: custodial UGMA/UTMA accounts plus a Youth Account for ages 13 to 17.
  • Fidelity Go: a robo-advisor that costs $0 under $25,000 and 0.35% a year above that, and starts investing once you have $10.
  • International: trading in 25 countries and 16 currencies.
  • Crypto: a 1% fee on each buy and sell, charged separately since December 4, 2025.

E*TRADE

  • Retirement and education accounts: traditional, Roth, rollover and inherited IRAs, IRAs for minors, traditional and Roth Individual 401(k)s, small-business plans and Coverdell education savings accounts.
  • Futures: $1.50 per contract per side plus fees; crypto futures are $2.50.
  • Core Portfolios: automated investing for 0.30% a year with a $500 minimum.
  • Crypto: bitcoin, ether and solana through zerohash for a 0.50% fee. Morgan Stanley said the rollout was complete on July 16, 2026. Crypto is not FDIC- or SIPC-protected.
  • Banking: checking, savings and CDs through Morgan Stanley Private Bank.

Platforms and Apps

Fidelity's advanced platform is Fidelity Trader+, available on desktop, web and mobile; Fidelity says it will begin sunsetting the older Active Trader Pro in 2027. E*TRADE offers its standard web platform and Power E*TRADE for active traders, both free according to NerdWallet. On the Apple App Store in November 2026, the Fidelity Investments app was rated 4.8 from about 3.4 million ratings, and E*TRADE was rated 4.7 from about 381,000 ratings.

Safety: SIPC and Excess Coverage

Both are large, regulated brokers. Fidelity Brokerage Services and Morgan Stanley Smith Barney (which carries E*TRADE accounts) are SIPC members, so securities are protected up to $500,000, including $250,000 in cash, if the firm fails. Each firm also buys excess-SIPC insurance with a $1 billion aggregate limit and a $1.9 million per-customer limit for cash. SIPC does not protect you from market losses.

Who Should Choose Fidelity?

  • Buy-and-hold investors who keep some cash in their account
  • Beginners who want fractional shares from $1 (see how to start investing)
  • Families who want an HSA, 529 or teen account at one firm
  • Anyone who might move their account later and wants no exit fee

Who Should Choose E*TRADE?

  • Options traders who place 30 or more trades a quarter
  • Traders who want futures alongside stocks and options
  • Morgan Stanley clients who want banking and brokerage together
  • Small-business owners who want an Individual 401(k) or a small-business plan

Frequently Asked Questions

Is E*TRADE or Fidelity cheaper?▼
For stock and ETF trades they cost the same: $0 online at both. Fidelity is cheaper if you hold cash, buy fractional shares or transfer your account out ($0 versus $75 at E*TRADE). E*TRADE is cheaper for active options traders, at $0.50 per contract with 30 or more trades a quarter versus $0.65 at Fidelity.
Which pays more interest on cash, Fidelity or E*TRADE?▼
Fidelity. Its default money market fund, SPAXX, had a 7-day yield of 3.61% as of November 25, 2026. E*TRADE's default brokerage sweep paid 0.01% to 0.15% according to NerdWallet's July 2026 review; to earn more there you must move cash into a separate account such as Premium Savings.
Does E*TRADE or Fidelity have fractional shares?▼
Both do. Fidelity sells fractional shares of NMS-listed stocks and ETFs from $1. E*TRADE sells fractional shares of select stocks and ETFs from $5.
Can you trade futures on E*TRADE?▼
Yes. E*TRADE charges $1.50 per futures contract per side plus fees, and $2.50 for crypto futures.
Are Fidelity and E*TRADE safe?▼
Yes. Both carry SIPC protection up to $500,000, including $250,000 in cash, plus excess-SIPC insurance with a $1 billion aggregate limit. SIPC protects you if a broker fails, not against investment losses.
