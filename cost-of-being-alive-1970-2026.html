Why this hits so hard

The paycheck grew — median household income went from about $8,700 in 1970 to roughly $81,600 in 2026, nearly 9×. But the things that define a stable life grew faster. A median home was about 2.6 years of income in 1970; today it's over 5. College went from a rounding error to a five-figure annual bill. And childcare, which barely registered as a line item in 1970, can now rival rent.

That's the squeeze in one picture: incomes rose, but rent, homes, college, and childcare rose faster — so the same salary covers less of a "normal" life than it used to. The best defense is the boring stuff done consistently: a real budget, an emergency fund, and steady investing so your money grows faster than the cost of being alive.

Take control of what you can

You can't undo 50 years of price history — but you can budget, cut the bills you're overpaying, and start investing today.
