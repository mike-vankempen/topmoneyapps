Average Internet Bill in 2026: Monthly Cost by State

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 7 min read

Plan prices from BroadbandNow and Reviews.org; bill-pay data from doxo; price trends from the BLS; discount rules from the FCC. Checked November 2026.

The average U.S. home internet plan costs about $63 to $81 a month in 2026, depending on the source: Reviews.org puts the average internet bill at $62.72, and BroadbandNow's average advertised plan price is $81.16. Households that pay a combined cable and internet bill pay a median of $124 a month, according to doxo. Internet prices are barely rising: the BLS price index for internet services was up 0.2% in the year to September 2026.

Key Takeaways

  • The average U.S. internet bill is $62.72 a month in 2026, down 8% from 2025, according to Reviews.org's 2026 consumer media spending report.
  • The average advertised price of an internet-only home plan is $81.16 a month, or about $973 a year, based on BroadbandNow's March 2026 data from more than 2,100 providers.
  • The median household cable and internet bill is $124 a month in doxo's 2026 U.S. Household Bill Pay Report; Delaware and Vermont are highest at $159, Arkansas lowest at $94.
  • The BLS consumer price index for internet services rose 0.2% from September 2025 to September 2026, compared with 3.2% for telephone services.
  • The FCC's Affordable Connectivity Program ended June 1, 2024; Lifeline still offers eligible households up to $9.25 a month off phone or internet service.

What Americans Pay for Internet: Three Measures

No federal agency publishes a single "average internet bill." The Bureau of Labor Statistics tracks how internet prices change, not what a typical bill is. So the dollar figures come from private sources that measure different things. An average adds up all bills and divides by the count; a median is the middle bill, so half pay more and half pay less.

The doxo figure is the highest because it combines cable TV and internet into one category, so it includes bundles. doxo bases it on bill payments across 97% of U.S. ZIP codes. Its 2025 report put the cable and internet bill at $121 a month and found that 73% of households pay one.

Reviews.org's 2025 report, which analyzed prices and plans across nearly 100 major internet providers, put the average internet bill at $68.36, up from $67.29 in 2024. Its 2026 report puts total household spending on internet, mobile, TV and streaming at $283.39 a month.

Price by Speed: The FCC's 2026 Benchmarks

Each year the FCC surveys urban broadband prices and sets "reasonable comparability" benchmarks. Providers that receive federal Universal Service Fund support must offer rates at or below these levels. The benchmarks are ceilings, not typical bills, but they show how price ranges by speed. The figures below are from the FCC's 2026 public notice (DA 25-1088), released December 19, 2025.

Mbps means megabits per second, the standard measure of connection speed. The 10/1, 25/3 and 100/20 tiers assume an 800 GB or unlimited data allowance; the gigabit tier assumes unlimited data.

Are Internet Prices Rising?

  • 0.2% in a year. The BLS consumer price index for internet services and electronic information providers rose 0.2% from September 2025 to September 2026, per the September 2026 CPI release. Prices fell 1.3% from August to September 2026.
  • Phone service rose faster. In the same release, telephone services and wireless telephone services were each up 3.2% over 12 months.
  • Bills fell in one survey. Reviews.org found the average internet bill fell 8% from 2025 to 2026, even as total spending on internet, mobile, TV and streaming rose 1.5%.
  • Many still feel squeezed. In BroadbandNow's 2026 survey, more than one in four Americans said they pay more for home internet than a year earlier.

How Many Households Pay for Internet

About 91.0% of U.S. households, or roughly 117.6 million, had a broadband internet subscription in the Census Bureau's 2020–2024 American Community Survey five-year estimates, and 95.5% had a computer. In BroadbandNow's 2026 survey, 88% of respondents said a home internet plan (cable, fiber, DSL, fixed wireless or satellite) is their primary connection.

How to Lower Your Internet Bill

  1. Check Lifeline. The FCC's Lifeline program takes up to $9.25 a month off phone or internet service, or up to $34.25 on qualifying Tribal lands. You qualify with household income at or below 135% of the federal poverty guidelines, or if you get SNAP, Medicaid, SSI, federal public housing assistance or the Veterans Pension benefit. Only one Lifeline benefit is allowed per household.
  2. Ask for a low-income plan. The Affordable Connectivity Program, which gave up to $30 a month ($75 on Tribal lands), ended June 1, 2024; April 2024 was the last month of the full discount. The FCC encourages former ACP households to ask their provider whether it has a lower-cost plan or low-income program.
  3. Own your equipment. Reviews.org says modem and router rentals usually cost $10 to $15 a month. If your provider allows customer-owned equipment, compare that monthly fee with the cost of buying a compatible device.
  4. Read the Broadband Facts label. The FCC introduced Broadband Facts labels in 2024 so that, at the point of sale, you can see a plan's prices, introductory rates, fees and speeds. Compare the price after any promotion ends, not just the first-year rate.
  5. Match speed to your use. The FCC benchmarks above show that faster tiers can cost more. If you are paying for a gigabit plan but rarely stream or work from home, ask about a lower tier.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average internet bill per month?

For internet alone, about $63 to $81 a month in 2026: Reviews.org puts the average bill at $62.72, and BroadbandNow's average advertised plan price is $81.16. Households that pay a combined cable and internet bill pay a median of $124 a month, according to doxo.

Which state has the most expensive internet?

Alaska, where the average advertised internet-only plan costs $109.88 a month, per BroadbandNow's March 2026 data. Iowa ($93.15) and Nevada ($89.12) follow. In doxo's bill-pay data, Delaware and Vermont have the highest cable and internet bills at $159 a month.

Which state has the cheapest internet?

New Jersey, at an average advertised price of $65.43 a month, followed by Connecticut ($65.50) and New Hampshire ($66.09), per BroadbandNow. In doxo's data, Arkansas ($94) and Florida ($95) have the lowest cable and internet bills.

Are internet prices going up?

Slowly. The BLS consumer price index for internet services rose 0.2% from September 2025 to September 2026, compared with 3.2% for telephone services. Reviews.org found the average internet bill fell 8% from 2025 to 2026.

Is there still a government discount on internet bills?

The Affordable Connectivity Program, which gave up to $30 a month, ended on June 1, 2024. The FCC's Lifeline program still offers up to $9.25 a month off phone or internet service, or up to $34.25 on Tribal lands, for households at or below 135% of the federal poverty guidelines or in programs such as SNAP or Medicaid.
