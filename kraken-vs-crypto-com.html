Kraken vs. Crypto.com (2026): Which Crypto App Is Better?

📅 Updated November 27, 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen

Kraken is the better choice for most U.S. crypto buyers who want a low-fee trading platform and recent proof of reserves, while Crypto.com suits people who want one app for crypto, stocks, prediction contracts and a crypto debit card. Kraken ranks higher in our crypto scores. Here is how they compare on fees, coins, staking, availability and safety.

Kraken vs. Crypto.com: Quick Verdict

Kraken ranks higher in our crypto rankings, with a TopMoneyApps score 4.07/5 versus TopMoneyApps score 3.57/5 for Crypto.com. Both let U.S. users buy and sell hundreds of cryptocurrencies, trade stocks and ETFs commission-free, and access regulated crypto futures. They differ on fee structure, where they operate and how much they disclose about reserves.

Choose Kraken if you trade often and want a published maker-taker fee schedule, or you want a recent proof-of-reserves report.

Choose Crypto.com if you want a broader all-in-one app with a Visa prepaid card and prediction contracts, and you live in a state Kraken does not serve, such as Maine.

⭐ Our Verdict

Kraken is the stronger pick for cost-conscious traders who will use Kraken Pro. Crypto.com is the broader consumer app, but its app spreads aren't published and its U.S. proof of reserves is out of date. With either, crypto prices can fall sharply, so only invest money you can afford to lose.

Trading Fees

Both companies have two ways to buy: a simple app and an advanced exchange. A maker order adds a resting order to the order book (like a limit order that doesn't fill right away); a taker order fills immediately against an existing order. Taker fees are higher.

Kraken. On Kraken Pro, the lowest tier ($0 or more in 30-day volume) pays 0.40% maker and 0.80% taker. Fees fall as volume rises: 0.30%/0.60% from $2,500 and 0.22%/0.38% from $10,000. In the simple Kraken app, instant and recurring trades cost 1% and custom orders 1.5%, and a spread is built into the price. Small instant trades can cost up to $2 instead of the 1% fee. Kraken+ members pay $0 trading fees on up to $10,000 a month, but spreads and card fees still apply.

Crypto.com. Crypto.com says its app has "zero trading fees up to the transaction limit specified per Level Up tier," adding that "other transaction fees and spread may apply." It doesn't publish the spread, so compare the quoted price with the market price before you buy. Its exchange fee schedule starts at 0.25% maker and 0.50% taker for under $10,000 in 30-day volume, with small discounts if you hold its CRO token; the page doesn't say whether those rates apply to U.S. users. USD bank transfers are free, and debit card, Apple Pay and Google Pay top-ups cost 1.49%.

On a $1,000 instant buy in the simple apps, Kraken's 1% fee is $10 plus spread. Crypto.com's cost depends on its unpublished spread. Active traders who use limit orders will usually pay less on either company's advanced exchange.

Coins, Staking and Other Products

Kraken

  • Coins: Kraken's own figures vary by page; its app page lists 633 assets globally.
  • Staking: Kraken relaunched U.S. staking on January 30, 2025, in 37 states and 2 territories, covering 17 assets. Staking means locking up coins to help run a blockchain in exchange for rewards.
  • Stocks: more than 11,000 U.S. stocks and ETFs, commission-free, through Kraken Securities LLC, a FINRA and SIPC member. Not offered in Maine or New York.
  • Futures: CME micro futures and CFTC-regulated crypto perpetual futures, which launched for U.S. traders on June 15, 2026. Kraken bought futures broker NinjaTrader for $1.5 billion in 2025.

Crypto.com

  • Coins: 400+ cryptocurrencies for U.S. users on the app.
  • Staking: NerdWallet counts staking on 30 coins in the U.S. CRO staking is not available in New York, California, Maryland, New Jersey or Wisconsin.
  • Stocks: commission-free stocks and ETFs through Foris Capital US LLC, a FINRA and SIPC member that clears through Apex Clearing. SEC and FINRA fees apply on sales.
  • Prediction contracts and futures: CFTC-regulated event contracts, plus crypto perpetual futures with up to 5x leverage for eligible U.S. traders since October 27, 2026. Leverage multiplies losses as well as gains.
  • Visa prepaid card: tiered cards; lower tiers charge a 3% foreign transaction fee, and an inactivity fee applies after 12 months.

Availability by State

Kraken is not available to residents of Maine or New York. Crypto transfers aren't available in Indiana, and bank (fiat) transfers aren't available in Indiana, Louisiana, Massachusetts or Utah. Crypto.com is available in 49 states, Washington, D.C. and U.S. territories, but not New York. New York residents can't use either app.

Safety and Regulatory History

Crypto held on an exchange is not protected by FDIC or SIPC insurance if the exchange fails. Crypto.com notes that FDIC coverage applies only to USD held at its partner banks, up to $250,000, and "does not protect your funds in the event of Crypto.com's failure." That makes reserve disclosures more important.

Kraken publishes proof-of-reserves snapshots; its latest, dated September 30, 2026, showed client bitcoin backed at 103.2% and ether at 100.7%. It holds U.S. registrations including a FinCEN money services business registration and a Wyoming special purpose depository institution charter. In February 2023, Kraken paid $30 million to settle SEC charges over its staking program without admitting or denying the allegations. The SEC dismissed a separate 2023 lawsuit against Kraken, with prejudice, in March 2025.

Crypto.com says it holds customer assets on a 1:1 basis and lists ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II and PCI DSS certifications. Its U.S. proof-of-reserves page still shows a report dated December 7, 2022. The SEC closed its investigation of Crypto.com with no enforcement action on March 27, 2025.

Kraken remains a private company. It filed a confidential draft registration statement for an initial public offering on November 19, 2025, and no listing date had been set when checked.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Kraken or Crypto.com cheaper?▼
It depends on how you buy. On their advanced exchanges, Crypto.com's lowest tier is 0.25% maker and 0.50% taker versus 0.40% and 0.80% on Kraken Pro, though Crypto.com doesn't say those rates apply to U.S. users. In the simple apps, Kraken charges 1% on instant buys plus a spread, while Crypto.com advertises zero trading fees up to a tier limit but says a spread may apply.
Can I use Kraken or Crypto.com in New York?▼
No. Kraken is not available to residents of Maine or New York, and Crypto.com is available in 49 states but not New York.
Does Kraken offer staking in the U.S.?▼
Yes, in most states. Kraken relaunched U.S. staking on January 30, 2025, in 37 states and 2 territories, covering 17 assets. Check the app for whether your state is eligible.
Is my crypto insured on Kraken or Crypto.com?▼
No. Crypto held on either exchange is not covered by FDIC or SIPC insurance. Crypto.com says FDIC coverage applies only to USD at its partner banks and does not protect funds if Crypto.com fails. Stocks held through each company's brokerage arm are SIPC-protected.
Which has more coins, Kraken or Crypto.com?▼
Both offer hundreds. Crypto.com lists 400+ cryptocurrencies for U.S. users, and Kraken's app page lists 633 assets globally, though not every asset is available in every U.S. state.
