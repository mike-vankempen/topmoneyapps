Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) in 2026

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 7 min read

General information, not tax advice. Rules and figures are from Charles Schwab's 2026 RMD Reference Guide, Kiplinger and The Motley Fool; confirm your situation with your plan administrator, the IRS or a tax professional.

A required minimum distribution (RMD) is the amount you must withdraw each year from most tax-deferred retirement accounts once you reach your RMD age. If you were born from 1951 through 1959, your RMDs start at age 73, so people who turn 73 in 2026 owe their first RMD by April 1, 2027; every later RMD is due by December 31. Your RMD equals your account balance on December 31 of the prior year divided by a distribution period from the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. Miss it and the penalty is 25% of the shortfall.

Key Takeaways

  • Required minimum distributions start at age 73 for people born from 1951 through 1959 and at age 75 for people born after December 31, 1959, according to The Motley Fool's summary of the SECURE 2.0 Act.
  • Your first RMD can be delayed until April 1 of the year after you reach RMD age, but every later RMD is due by December 31, so delaying means taking two RMDs in one year.
  • An RMD equals the account's December 31 balance from the prior year divided by the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table distribution period, which is 26.5 at age 73 and 24.6 at age 75.
  • Roth IRAs have no RMDs while the original owner is alive, and designated Roth accounts in 401(k) and 403(b) plans have been exempt since 2024.
  • A missed RMD triggers a 25% excise tax on the amount not withdrawn, which can drop to 10% if you correct it within two years.

When RMDs Start: Your RMD Age by Birth Year

The SECURE 2.0 Act raised the RMD age in steps. Your starting age depends on when you were born:

BornRMD age
Before July 1, 194970½
July 1, 1949 to December 31, 195072
January 1, 1951 to December 31, 195973
After December 31, 195975

Source: The Motley Fool, January 2026. In practice, someone born in 1953 turns 73 in 2026, so 2026 is their first RMD year.

RMD Deadlines for 2026

  • Your first RMD: due by April 1 of the year after the year you reach RMD age. If you turn 73 in 2026, your 2026 RMD can wait until April 1, 2027.
  • Every RMD after that: due by December 31 of that year. If you already took RMDs in prior years, your 2026 RMD is due by December 31, 2026.
  • The double-RMD trade-off: if you delay your first RMD to April 1, 2027, your second RMD (for 2027) is still due by December 31, 2027. Both withdrawals then count as income in 2027, which can push you into a higher tax bracket.

Still-working exception: Schwab says that if you are still employed, you may be able to delay RMDs from your current employer's plan until April 1 of the year after you retire. It applies only to the plan of the employer you still work for, only if the plan allows it, and not if you own more than 5% of the business.

How to Calculate Your RMD

For each account, take the balance on December 31 of the previous year and divide it by the distribution period for the age you reach this year. Most owners use the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table (Table III in IRS Publication 590-B). If your spouse is your sole beneficiary and is more than 10 years younger than you, you use the Joint Life and Last Survivor table instead.

Example 1: you turn 73 in 2026 and your traditional IRA held $400,000 on December 31, 2025. Your 2026 RMD is $400,000 ÷ 26.5 = $15,094.34.

Example 2: you turn 75 in 2026 with $500,000 in IRAs at the end of 2025. Your RMD is $500,000 ÷ 24.6 = $20,325.20.

The divisor shrinks each year, so the share you must withdraw rises with age: about 3.8% of the balance at 73 and about 8.2% at 90.

Taking RMDs From Several Accounts

You must calculate the RMD for each account, but the rules on where you withdraw it differ by account type, according to Schwab:

  • IRAs (traditional, rollover, SEP and SIMPLE): add up the RMDs and take the total from any one IRA or a mix of them.
  • 403(b)s: can be combined only with other 403(b)s.
  • 401(k)s and governmental 457(b)s: each plan's RMD must come out of that plan.

The Penalty for Missing an RMD

If you withdraw less than your RMD, you owe a 25% excise tax on the amount you should have taken. Kiplinger and The Motley Fool report that the penalty can fall to 10% if you correct the shortfall within about two years (by the end of the second year after the RMD was due). You report it on IRS Form 5329; Schwab notes you can include a letter of explanation when you take the missed amount.

Plan ahead for December 31. If you still owe a 2026 RMD, request it early enough for the money to leave the account by December 31. Our year-end tax moves checklist covers other deadlines that land on the same day.

Giving Your RMD to Charity

A qualified charitable distribution (QCD) sends money from your IRA directly to a qualified charity. Schwab says that for 2026, people age 70½ or older can donate up to $111,000 a year this way, a limit that is indexed for inflation. Ask your IRA custodian how a QCD is processed and reported before you request one. For how deductions work, see the 2026 standard deduction.

Frequently Asked Questions

What age do RMDs start in 2026?

RMDs start at age 73 for people born from 1951 through 1959. People born after December 31, 1959 start at age 75. Someone born in 1953 turns 73 in 2026, so 2026 is their first RMD year.

When is the 2026 RMD deadline?

If 2026 is your first RMD year, you can wait until April 1, 2027. Otherwise your 2026 RMD is due by December 31, 2026. Delaying the first RMD means you take two RMDs in 2027.

How do I calculate my RMD?

Divide each account's December 31, 2025 balance by the distribution period for the age you reach in 2026 from the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. At 73 the divisor is 26.5, so a $400,000 balance means an RMD of $15,094.34.

Do Roth IRAs have required minimum distributions?

No, not while the original owner is alive. Designated Roth accounts in 401(k) and 403(b) plans have also been exempt since 2024. Heirs who inherit a Roth account may have to take RMDs.

What happens if I miss an RMD?

You owe a 25% excise tax on the amount you did not withdraw. If you take the missed amount and correct it within about two years, the penalty can drop to 10%. You report it on IRS Form 5329.
