Average Life Insurance Cost by Age in 2026

📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen 📊 Source: MoneyGeek / NerdWallet

A healthy 30-year-old nonsmoker pays about $24 a month (women) or $30 (men) for a $500,000, 20-year term life policy, according to MoneyGeek quotes published in November 2026. By age 50 the same policy costs about $79 and $103, and by 60 about $204 and $293. Age is the biggest driver of price, followed by tobacco use, health, the term length and the coverage amount. Below are average rates by age from two independent rate surveys, plus what changes your price.

Key Takeaways

  • A 40-year-old nonsmoker in good health pays about $37 a month (women) or $44 a month (men) for a $500,000, 20-year term life policy, according to MoneyGeek's November 2026 quotes.
  • A woman's monthly rate for the same policy rises from $24 at age 30 to $79 at age 50, a 229% increase, according to MoneyGeek.
  • Smokers pay roughly 180% to 270% more than nonsmokers for term life; at age 40, MoneyGeek found $119 a month for women and $154 for men who smoke.
  • Whole life costs far more than term: NerdWallet's October 2026 data show about $3,180 a year for a 40-year-old nonsmoking man with $500,000 of whole life, versus $321 a year for 20-year term.
  • Roughly three-quarters of consumers overestimate the cost of life insurance, according to the 2026 Insurance Barometer Study by LIMRA and Life Happens.

How Fast Life Insurance Gets More Expensive With Age

Price increases are small in your 20s and 30s and steep after 50. In ValuePenguin's quotes for a man in excellent health, the monthly rate rose about 6% from age 25 to 30 but about 86% from age 60 to 65. In MoneyGeek's data, a man's monthly premium goes from $44 at 40 to $103 at 50 and $293 at 60. Insurify estimates rates can climb by about 9% for each year you wait to buy. Every source agrees on the direction: the same coverage costs more the older you are when you buy it, and the Connecticut Insurance Department lists age first among the factors that set your premium.

Smokers vs. Nonsmokers

Tobacco use is the second-biggest price factor. MoneyGeek found smokers pay about 180% to 270% more than nonsmokers. At age 40, a $500,000, 20-year policy costs smokers about $119 a month (women) and $154 (men), versus $37 and $44 for nonsmokers. ValuePenguin put the smoker surcharge at about 218%. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance notes this includes smokeless tobacco, and that quitting may lower your rate after a waiting period set by the insurer.

A health class (or "risk class") is the rating an insurer assigns after reviewing your health. In MoneyGeek's data, Preferred Plus is the cheapest class shown and Standard the most expensive. Insurers set it from your age, height, weight, medical history, family history, occupation and driving record, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance. The Connecticut Insurance Department adds chronic conditions, heavy drinking and dangerous hobbies such as skydiving or rock climbing.

Most People Guess Too High

Cost is the top reason people give for not buying life insurance, cited by 45% in the 2026 Insurance Barometer Study (LIMRA and Life Happens), yet roughly three-quarters of consumers overestimate what it costs. Forty-six percent said their estimate was a "gut feeling" or a "wild guess," and adults under 31 overestimated the price of a $250,000, 20-year term policy by about five to six times. The study surveyed 5,237 U.S. adults in January 2026. For more coverage data, see our life insurance statistics.

How to Use These Numbers

Averages show the ballpark; only a quote shows your price. Start by working out how much coverage you need with the life insurance calculator, check what you already have through your job (how workplace coverage works), and compare quotes from several insurers for the same amount and term. A few points from the NAIC's Life Insurance Buyer's Guide:

  • Policies that skip detailed health questions generally cost more and provide less coverage.
  • Most policies have a "free look" period, usually 10 days after you receive the policy, when you can return it for a full refund.
  • If you renew a term policy when it ends, the new premiums are higher.

Before you buy, it also helps to know how the IRS treats life insurance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is life insurance per month for a 30-year-old? ▼

About $24 a month for a woman and $30 for a man for a $500,000, 20-year term policy, for nonsmokers in good health, according to MoneyGeek's November 2026 quotes. Your price depends on your health, the insurer and your state.

How much does life insurance cost at age 50? ▼

MoneyGeek's November 2026 average for a $500,000, 20-year term policy is about $79 a month for a nonsmoking woman and $103 for a nonsmoking man. NerdWallet's October 2026 data show about $636 and $810 a year for the best preferred-class rates.

Why does life insurance get more expensive with age? ▼

Insurers price coverage by age; the Connecticut Insurance Department puts it simply: the older you are, the more expensive the premiums. Increases are small in your 20s and 30s and steep after 50. ValuePenguin found rates rose about 6% from age 25 to 30 but about 86% from 60 to 65.

How much more do smokers pay for life insurance? ▼

MoneyGeek found smokers pay about 180% to 270% more than nonsmokers. At age 40, a $500,000, 20-year term policy costs about $119 a month for women and $154 for men who smoke, versus $37 and $44 for nonsmokers.

Is whole life insurance more expensive than term? ▼

Yes, by a wide margin. NerdWallet's October 2026 averages put $500,000 of whole life at about $3,180 a year for a 40-year-old nonsmoking man, versus $321 a year for a 20-year term policy.
