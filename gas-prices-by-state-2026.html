Why diesel costs $2 more than gas

Diesel drivers are getting hit hardest: the national average sits near $6.50, roughly $2 above regular gas, and it topped an all-time high earlier this fall.[2] Several forces stack up: diesel carries a higher federal excise tax, winter heating-oil demand competes for the same distillate supply, refinery capacity for diesel is tight, and inventories have run low. Because diesel powers nearly all trucking, rail, and farm equipment, expensive diesel quietly raises the price of almost everything you buy — from groceries to Amazon deliveries.

Why are gas prices so high in 2026?

The biggest single factor is the price of crude oil, which typically accounts for over half of what you pay at the pump and has stayed elevated through 2026.[2] On top of crude, four things push prices up:

  • State taxes. California levies over 70 cents a gallon in taxes and fees; low-tax states like Texas and Mississippi keep pump prices far lower.[1]
  • Special fuel blends. California and a few other states require cleaner, costlier gasoline formulations that most refineries can't produce.
  • Refinery capacity & outages. A single refinery going offline in a supply-constrained region (the West Coast especially) can spike local prices overnight.
  • Global demand. Crude is a world commodity, so a supply threat anywhere can lift prices everywhere within days.

📊 The bottom line

Where you live can cost you over $30 more per fill-up for the exact same gallon of fuel. You can't control crude oil, but you can control the rest: a Gulf Coast driver pays about $55 to fill a sedan while a Californian pays $86. If gas is straining your budget, the highest-leverage moves are cutting trips, keeping tires properly inflated, using a fuel-rewards or cash-back card, and apps that find the cheapest station nearby — small habits that add up to hundreds a year.

How to spend less at the pump

  • Use a gas-finder app (GasBuddy, Waze, AAA) — prices vary 20–40 cents between stations a mile apart.
  • Pay with a cash-back or fuel-rewards card that gives 3–5% back on gas.
  • Keep tires inflated and slow down — under-inflated tires and speeding both cut fuel economy noticeably.
  • Buy on the cheapest days and avoid highway and airport stations, which charge a premium.
  • Track it in a budget so a rising fuel bill doesn't quietly blow up your monthly spending.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average gas price in the US right now? ▼

About $4.48 per gallon for regular as of September 21, 2026 (AAA). Diesel averages around $6.50. Prices rose through 2026 on elevated crude oil costs.

Which state has the highest gas prices? ▼

California at about $6.17 regular ($8.42 diesel), due to the highest fuel taxes, a special clean-fuel blend, and limited refining. Washington, Hawaii, Nevada, and Oregon follow.

Which state has the cheapest gas? ▼

Texas at about $3.95, then Mississippi ($3.97), Louisiana ($4.00), Alabama ($4.01), and Oklahoma ($4.05). Gulf Coast states sit next to refineries and tax fuel lightly.

How much does it cost to fill up a tank in 2026? ▼

At the national average: about $54 for a compact (12 gal), $63 for a sedan (14 gal), $81 for an SUV (18 gal), and about $169 for a diesel pickup (26 gal at $6.50).

Why is diesel more expensive than gas? ▼

Diesel runs ~$2 above gas in 2026 due to a higher federal tax, competing heating-oil demand, tight refining capacity, and low inventories. It also raises freight costs, pushing up other prices.

Why are gas prices so high in 2026? ▼

Mostly elevated crude oil, which is over half the pump price. State taxes, special blends, refinery outages, and global demand add the rest. Crude is a world commodity, so prices move fast.
