The Wash Sale Rule, Explained: 30-Day Window, Examples and Reporting

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 8 min read

General information, not tax advice. Confirm details in IRS Publication 550 or with a tax professional.

The wash sale rule stops you from deducting a loss when you sell a stock or security at a loss and buy the same or a substantially identical one within 30 days before or after the sale. The loss is not lost for good in most cases: the IRS adds it to the cost basis of the new shares, so you claim it when you sell those later.

Key Takeaways

  • A wash sale occurs when you sell stock or securities at a loss and buy substantially identical ones within 30 days before or after the sale, a 61-day window (IRS Publication 550, Fidelity).
  • A wash sale loss is not deductible now; the IRS adds it to the cost basis of the replacement shares, and the old holding period carries over.
  • Purchases by your spouse, a corporation you control, or your IRA or Roth IRA count; when the replacement is bought in an IRA, the loss is not added to basis.
  • Brokers report wash sales on Form 1099-B only for the same CUSIP in the same account, but the IRS still disallows wash sale losses across accounts.
  • Net capital losses can offset up to $3,000 of ordinary income a year ($1,500 if married filing separately), with the rest carried forward (IRS Topic 409).

What Counts as a Wash Sale

IRS Publication 550 says a wash sale happens when you sell or trade stock or securities at a loss and, within 30 days before or after the sale, you:

  • Buy substantially identical stock or securities,
  • Acquire substantially identical stock or securities in a fully taxable trade,
  • Acquire a contract or option to buy substantially identical stock or securities, or
  • Acquire substantially identical stock for your individual retirement arrangement (IRA) or Roth IRA.

Fidelity describes the result as a 61-day period: the sale date, the 30 days before it and the 30 days after it. The look-back half surprises many investors. Buying more shares in the 30 days before you sell at a loss can trigger the rule just as buying after does.

The rule reaches beyond your own account. Publication 550 says you also have a wash sale if you sell and your spouse or a corporation you control buys substantially identical stock. Schwab adds that the rule applies across all of your accounts, including ones at other firms.

The rule only covers losses. A sale at a gain is taxed normally, whenever you buy the shares back. Our 2026 capital gains tax rates guide explains how gains are taxed.

What Happens to the Disallowed Loss

A wash sale does not usually erase your loss. It postpones it. Publication 550 sets out three effects:

  1. You cannot deduct the loss now.
  2. The disallowed loss is added to the cost basis of the new shares. Cost basis is what you paid, and it is what the IRS uses to figure your gain or loss when you sell. A higher basis means a smaller gain or a bigger loss later.
  3. Your holding period carries over. The time you held the old shares counts toward the new ones, which can affect whether a later gain or loss is short-term or long-term.

Here is the IRS's own example from Publication 550:

Step (IRS Pub. 550 example)Amount
Buy shares$1,000
Sell those shares$750
Loss on the sale$250
Buy the same stock again within 30 days$800
Loss you can deduct now$0
Basis of the new shares ($800 + $250)$1,050

If you later sell the new shares for $1,050 or less, the old $250 loss shows up then.

The IRA trap: buying the replacement shares in an IRA or Roth IRA is the one case where the loss is not added to the new shares' basis, under Publication 550. Fidelity describes that loss as effectively forfeited, not deferred.

What "Substantially Identical" Means

This is the gray area. Publication 550 says you must consider all the facts and circumstances, and gives two general points:

  • Stock or securities of one company are ordinarily not substantially identical to those of another company.
  • A company's bonds or preferred stock are ordinarily not substantially identical to its common stock, with exceptions for some convertible securities.

For funds, there is no bright-line test. Schwab says the government has not provided a straightforward definition, and Fidelity says there are no clear guidelines. The same ticker in another account is clearly identical. Whether two different funds that track the same index count is not settled by the IRS, so many investors ask a tax professional before relying on a swap.

Publication 550 also says the wash sale rules apply to options, and do not apply to losses on commodity futures contracts or foreign currencies.

Wash Sales and Tax-Loss Harvesting at Year-End

Tax-loss harvesting means selling investments at a loss to offset gains. Losses first offset capital gains. Under IRS Topic 409, any net capital loss left over can offset up to $3,000 of other income a year ($1,500 if married filing separately), and the rest carries forward to later years.

The wash sale rule is what makes year-end harvesting tricky:

  • The window crosses January 1. Schwab notes the rule is not confined to the calendar year. In a Publication 550 example, a sale on December 27, 2024 and a purchase on January 10, 2025 was a wash sale.
  • The 30 days before count too. In another Publication 550 example, shares bought on December 13 and December 20 made a January 3 sale at a loss a wash sale. Any automatic purchases of the same security in the window deserve a check before you sell.
  • Know the calendar. NYSE Group's 2026 schedule closes the market on Friday, December 25, with an early 1 p.m. ET close on Thursday, December 24. Thursday, December 31, 2026 is the last trading day of the year, and markets are closed on Friday, January 1, 2027.

How Wash Sales Are Reported

Your broker reports some wash sales for you. Publication 550 says box 1g of Form 1099-B shows the disallowed loss when the securities were covered securities and you bought the replacement with the same CUSIP number in the same account. (A CUSIP is the ID number assigned to a security.) But the IRS adds that you cannot deduct a wash sale loss even if it is not reported on Form 1099-B.

That gap matters if you hold the same investment at two brokers, in your spouse's account, or in an IRA. On your return, you report the sale on Form 8949: enter code W in column (f) and the disallowed loss as a positive number in column (g). If the amount in box 1g is wrong, the Form 8949 instructions say to enter the correct amount. Our guide to brokerage accounts covers the tax forms you get each year.

Does the Wash Sale Rule Apply to Crypto?

As of November 2026, the statute behind the rule, Internal Revenue Code section 1091, covers "stock or securities." Publication 550 treats cryptocurrency as a capital asset, but its wash sale section is written for stock and securities. Sources such as Count On Sheep note that crypto ETF shares are securities and are covered.

That could change. H.R. 10357, the Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act, was introduced on September 14, 2026 and includes a section applying wash sale rules to traded digital assets. EY reported that the House Ways and Means Committee approved it 38-5 in September 2026, and The Block reported on November 20, 2026 that it had not yet had a House floor vote. As written, the bill would apply to dispositions after its introduction date, so it could reach sales already made this fall if it becomes law. Watch for updates before relying on crypto losses.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the wash sale rule?

The wash sale rule says you cannot deduct a loss on stock or securities if, within 30 days before or after the sale, you buy substantially identical stock or securities, acquire them in a fully taxable trade, acquire an option to buy them, or buy them in your IRA or Roth IRA. The disallowed loss is generally added to the cost basis of the new shares.

How long do you have to wait to avoid a wash sale?

More than 30 days. The window covers the 30 days before the sale, the sale date and the 30 days after it, 61 days in total. Buying substantially identical shares anywhere in that window triggers the rule.

Does the wash sale rule apply across different accounts?

Yes. Schwab says the rule applies across all your accounts, including those at other firms, and IRS Publication 550 says a purchase by your spouse or a corporation you control also counts. Brokers only report wash sales for the same security in the same account, so tracking the rest is up to you.

Does the wash sale rule apply to gains?

No. The rule only applies to sales at a loss. If you sell at a gain and buy back right away, the gain is taxed normally.

Does the wash sale rule apply to crypto in 2026?

As of November 2026, the statute (IRC section 1091) covers stock and securities, and Congress had not enacted a change for directly held crypto. H.R. 10357, which would apply wash sale rules to traded digital assets, passed the House Ways and Means Committee in September 2026 but had not had a House floor vote as of November 20, 2026.

What does code W mean on Form 8949?

Code W marks a wash sale. You enter W in column (f) and the disallowed loss as a positive number in column (g), using the correct amount even if box 1g of your Form 1099-B is wrong or blank.
