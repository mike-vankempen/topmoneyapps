How to Freeze Your Credit at All Three Bureaus

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 8 min read

General information, not legal advice. Based on CFPB, FTC and credit bureau guidance, checked November 2026.

To freeze your credit, contact Equifax, Experian and TransUnion separately and ask each to place a security freeze, online, by phone or by mail. It is free under federal law, and a request made online or by phone must be placed within 1 business day and lifted within 1 hour. A freeze stops most new lenders from pulling your credit report, which makes it hard for an identity thief to open accounts in your name. It does not change your credit score.

Key Takeaways

  • Credit freezes have been free at all three nationwide credit bureaus since September 21, 2018, under the Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act, according to the FTC.
  • If you ask online or by phone, a bureau must place a freeze within 1 business day and lift it within 1 hour; mail requests get 3 business days after the bureau receives them.
  • You must freeze your file at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion separately, because a freeze at one bureau does not affect the other two.
  • A freeze does not affect your credit score, and your existing creditors can still see your file, according to the CFPB.
  • Parents can freeze the credit of children under 16 for free, and guardians can do the same for people they represent.

What a Credit Freeze Is

A credit freeze, also called a security freeze, "prevents prospective creditors from accessing your credit file," in the CFPB's words. Most lenders check your credit report before they approve a new card or loan. If they can't see it, they usually won't open the account. That is what blocks an identity thief who has your name and Social Security number.

The freeze stays in place until you lift or remove it. There is no expiration date. When you want to apply for credit yourself, you lift it for a period you choose, or remove it permanently.

Where to Freeze: Equifax, Experian and TransUnion

You have to contact each bureau. Online is fastest. Each bureau will have you create an account or give you a PIN or password, which you need later to lift the freeze.

How to Freeze Your Credit, Step by Step

  1. Gather your details. Have your full name, current address, date of birth and Social Security number ready. By mail, you may need copies of ID and proof of address.
  2. Freeze at Equifax. Create or sign in to a myEquifax account and place the freeze, or call (888) 298-0045. By phone, Equifax verifies you with a one-time PIN by text or questions from your credit report.
  3. Freeze at Experian. Use the Experian Freeze Center online or call 1-888-397-3742.
  4. Freeze at TransUnion. Place the freeze through a TransUnion Service Center account online, or by mail.
  5. Save your login, PIN or password. The bureaus "should give you a PIN or password," according to Massachusetts consumer officials. Store it somewhere safe. You need it to lift the freeze.
  6. Watch for confirmation. The CFPB says the bureau must send written confirmation no later than 5 business days after placing the freeze.
  7. Consider the smaller agencies. NCTUE, ChexSystems and Innovis also keep files used for phone, utility and bank accounts (see below).

How to Lift (Unfreeze) Your Credit

Before you apply for a loan, credit card, apartment, phone plan or some jobs, you may need to lift the freeze, according to Massachusetts consumer officials. Sign in to the bureau's site or call, and choose a temporary lift for a set period or a permanent removal. A lift requested online or by phone must take effect within 1 hour; by mail, within 3 business days after the bureau gets your request. Lifting is free.

A freeze at one bureau does not affect the other two, so you can lift only the file a lender says it will check, if it tells you.

The CFPB says your existing creditors, certain government agencies such as child support offices, and companies you hired to monitor your file can still see a frozen report. You can still get your own free reports. The CFPB also notes the free-freeze law does not cover reports used for employment, tenant screening or insurance.

Locks differ by bureau. Equifax says its Lock & Alert is free for U.S. consumers 18 and older, covers only Equifax, and can't be on your file at the same time as an Equifax freeze. Experian CreditLock is part of Experian Premium, which costs $24.99 a month after a 7-day free trial (November 2026). TransUnion said in 2025 that it was discontinuing its lock on several of its consumer products.

The CFPB's view: "Credit locks are no more effective than security freezes, which are free." If cost matters, a freeze gives you the protection the law guarantees.

Freezing a Child's Credit

Parents can freeze the credit of a child under 16 for free, and guardians, conservators and people with a valid power of attorney can do the same for the person they represent. If the child has no credit file, the bureau may create a record just to freeze it, and the CFPB says that record "can't be used for credit purposes." You need proof of your authority, such as a birth certificate, and you contact each bureau separately. The FTC says teens aged 16 and 17 may request and remove a freeze themselves.

Why It Matters: Fraud and Identity Theft Numbers

The FTC says consumers filed 3 million fraud reports in 2025 and reported $15.9 billion in losses, up from 2.6 million reports and more than $12 billion the year before. Identity theft reports rose to 1,358,253 in 2025 from 1,135,265 in 2024, with credit card fraud the largest category at 597,770 reports, according to Experian's summary of FTC data. A freeze is one of the few free steps that blocks new-account fraud before it starts.

If someone has already opened an account in your name, report it at IdentityTheft.gov and see how to dispute a credit report error. To understand what lenders see when you lift the freeze, read what a good credit score is and average credit scores by age.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does freezing your credit hurt your credit score?

No. The CFPB says security freezes do not affect your credit scores in any way. Your existing accounts keep reporting as usual, and you can still check your own credit reports while the freeze is on.

How long does it take to unfreeze your credit?

If you ask online or by phone, the bureau must lift the freeze within 1 hour. A mailed request must be handled within 3 business days after the bureau receives it. You can lift a freeze temporarily for a period you choose or remove it permanently.

Is a credit freeze free?

Yes. Since September 21, 2018, federal law has required Equifax, Experian and TransUnion to place, lift and remove freezes for free. Some bureaus sell credit lock products, but the freeze itself costs nothing.

What is the difference between a credit freeze and a credit lock?

A freeze is a protection set by federal law and is always free. A lock is a bureau product you control through an app; its terms are set by the bureau and some locks are part of paid plans. The CFPB says credit locks are no more effective than security freezes.

Can I freeze my child's credit?

Yes. Parents can freeze the credit of a child under 16 for free by contacting each bureau with proof of authority, such as a birth certificate. If the child has no file, the bureau can create one just to freeze it, and that record can't be used for credit.
