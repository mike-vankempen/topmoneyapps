Chase Freedom Flex vs. Sapphire Reserve (2026): Which Card Fits You?

📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen

The Chase Freedom Flex fits people who want a $0-annual-fee card that pays 5% cash back on rotating quarterly categories, while the Chase Sapphire Reserve fits frequent travelers who will use enough of its credits and lounge access to justify a $795 annual fee. You do not have to choose: Chase lets you combine points from both cards in one account. Here is how they compare on fees, rewards, credits, APRs and point value, as of November 2026.

Key Takeaways

  • As of November 2026, the Chase Freedom Flex has a $0 annual fee and the Chase Sapphire Reserve has a $795 annual fee, plus $195 for each authorized user.
  • The Freedom Flex earns 5% on up to $1,500 a quarter in rotating categories you activate, 5% on Chase Travel, 3% on dining and drugstores and 1% on everything else.
  • The Sapphire Reserve earns 8x points on Chase Travel, 4x on flights and hotels booked direct, 3x on dining and 1x on everything else.
  • The Sapphire Reserve includes up to $300 a year in travel statement credits and access to Chase Sapphire Lounges and 1,300+ Priority Pass airport lounges.
  • Freedom Flex points cannot be transferred to airline or hotel partners on their own, but they can be combined into a Sapphire Reserve account, which can transfer to Chase's 16 partners.

Freedom Flex vs. Sapphire Reserve: Quick Verdict

The Chase Freedom Flex has a TopMoneyApps score 4.63/5 and the Chase Sapphire Reserve has a TopMoneyApps score 4.33/5. They are built for different jobs. The Freedom Flex is a no-fee everyday card with bonus categories that change every quarter. The Sapphire Reserve is a premium travel card with a high fee, a long list of credits and the ability to transfer points to airlines and hotels.

⭐ Our Verdict

The Freedom Flex is the lower-risk choice for most people because it costs nothing to hold. The Sapphire Reserve only pays off for travelers who use its credits and perks. Holding both lets you earn with the Freedom Flex and redeem through the Reserve.

Annual Fee and Sign-Up Bonus

The Freedom Flex has no annual fee. As of November 2026, Chase offers new cardmembers a $200 bonus after you spend $500 on purchases in the first 3 months.

The Sapphire Reserve costs $795 a year, plus $195 for each authorized user. Chase raised the fee from $550 when it overhauled the card in June 2025. As of November 2026, the offer is 100,000 points after you spend $6,000 on purchases in the first 3 months. At 1 cent per point through Chase Travel, that is worth $1,000; it can be worth more with Points Boost or transfers. Bonus offers change often, so check the current terms before you apply.

Rewards: How Each Card Earns

Chase Freedom Flex

  • 5% cash back on up to $1,500 in combined purchases in rotating categories each quarter you activate. That caps the quarterly bonus at $75.
  • 5% on travel purchased through Chase Travel.
  • 3% on dining, including takeout and eligible delivery, and 3% on drugstore purchases.
  • 1% on everything else.

For October 1 through December 31, 2026, the 5% categories are grocery stores (excluding Walmart and Target), dining, and donations to the American Red Cross. Chase says you have until December 14, 2026 to activate them. Each $1 of Freedom Flex cash back equals 100 points.

Chase Sapphire Reserve

  • 8x points on all purchases through Chase Travel, including The Edit (Chase's hotel collection).
  • 4x on flights booked directly with airlines and 4x on hotels booked directly.
  • 3x on dining worldwide.
  • 1x on all other purchases.

Both earn 3 on dining and 1 on most other spending. The Freedom Flex earns more on drugstores (3% vs. 1x) and its 5% categories. The Reserve earns more on travel: 8x vs. 5% through Chase Travel, and 4x vs. 1% on flights and hotels booked direct.

Credits and Travel Perks

Most of the Sapphire Reserve's value comes from credits. As listed by Chase in November 2026, they include:

  • Travel: up to $300 in statement credits for travel each account anniversary year.
  • The Edit hotels: up to $250 per prepaid booking, up to $500 a year.
  • Dining: up to $300 a year at restaurants in the Sapphire Reserve Exclusive Tables program on OpenTable.
  • StubHub and viagogo: up to $300 a year through 12/31/2027.
  • Lyft: up to $10 a month in in-app credits (up to $120 a year); Peloton: $10 a month on eligible memberships through 12/31/2027.
  • Apple TV and Apple Music subscriptions (activate by June 22, 2027) and complimentary DoorDash DashPass.
  • Global Entry or TSA PreCheck: one credit of up to $120 every four years.
  • Lounges: Chase Sapphire Lounges by The Club and 1,300+ Priority Pass lounges; extra guests cost $27 each per visit.

NerdWallet reports that spending $75,000 a year on the Reserve unlocks IHG One Rewards Diamond Elite status, Southwest A-List status, a $500 Southwest credit and a $250 credit at The Shops at Chase.

The Freedom Flex has no travel credits or lounge access. Chase lists purchase protection (120 days, up to $500 per item), extended warranty protection, trip cancellation and interruption insurance up to $6,000 per trip, auto rental coverage and six months of complimentary DashPass.

The trade-off: if you use only the $300 travel credit, the Reserve's net cost is $495 a year. Many of the other credits are split into monthly or per-booking amounts, so they take active tracking to use.

APR, Fees and Foreign Transactions

APR, or annual percentage rate, is the yearly interest rate you pay on a balance you carry, as the CFPB explains. A "variable" APR moves with the prime rate.

  • Freedom Flex: 0% intro APR for 15 months from account opening on purchases and balance transfers, then 18.49%–27.99% variable. NerdWallet lists the balance transfer fee as $5 or 5% of each transfer, whichever is greater.
  • Sapphire Reserve: 19.74%–28.24% variable, with no intro APR.

Neither card charges a foreign transaction fee as of November 2026. NerdWallet reports that the Freedom Flex charged 3% until it dropped the fee on Sept. 20, 2026. If you carry a balance, interest can wipe out rewards on either card; see the average credit card interest rate for context.

Point Value and Transfer Partners

Both cards earn Chase Ultimate Rewards points. Redeemed for cash back, a point is worth 1 cent. The difference is what else you can do with them.

Points Boost: Chase Travel's Points Boost, introduced in June 2025, offers select hotels and flights for fewer points. Sapphire Reserve points are worth 1 cent each through Chase Travel and up to 2 cents on Points Boost bookings. Points the Reserve earned before Oct. 26, 2025 can still be redeemed at 1.5 cents until Oct. 26, 2027.

Transfer partners are airline and hotel loyalty programs that accept Chase points. As of November 2026, Chase has 16: 12 airlines (including United MileagePlus, Southwest Rapid Rewards and Air Canada Aeroplan) and 4 hotel programs (IHG, Marriott Bonvoy, World of Hyatt and Wyndham). Most transfer at 1:1. Only the Sapphire Preferred, Sapphire Reserve, Ink Business Preferred and Sapphire Reserve for Business can transfer; Freedom cards cannot transfer directly.

How People Pair the Two Cards

Because both cards earn Ultimate Rewards points, you can use them as a team:

  1. Use the Freedom Flex for its 5% quarterly categories and 3% drugstore purchases.
  2. Use the Sapphire Reserve for travel and for anything outside the Freedom Flex's bonus categories where the Reserve earns more.
  3. Combine points from the Freedom Flex into the Reserve in Ultimate Rewards. AwardWallet notes this is free as the primary cardholder and can include a household member at the same address.
  4. Redeem from the Reserve through Points Boost or a transfer partner.

Applying for two cards means two hard inquiries; see hard vs. soft inquiries and how credit utilization works. NerdWallet notes the Reserve requires excellent credit; check what counts as a good credit score.

Who Each Card Fits

The Freedom Flex fits people who want no annual fee, could use 15 months at 0% intro APR for a planned purchase, and will activate rotating 5% categories each quarter.

The Sapphire Reserve fits frequent travelers who use airport lounges, will use the $300 travel credit and several other credits each year, and want to transfer points to airline and hotel programs. If you would not use the credits, the $795 fee is a straight cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you have both the Chase Freedom Flex and the Sapphire Reserve?▼
Yes. Chase lets a primary cardholder combine Ultimate Rewards points between Sapphire, Freedom and Ink cards at no cost. If you hold both cards, you can move Freedom Flex points to the Sapphire Reserve, where they can be transferred to airline and hotel partners or used for Points Boost bookings. Each card is a separate application and approval is not guaranteed.
Is the Sapphire Reserve worth the $795 annual fee?▼
It depends on how much of the card you use. The Sapphire Reserve includes up to $300 a year in travel statement credits, which brings the net cost to $495 if you use the credit in full. Its other credits, such as The Edit hotel credits, Exclusive Tables dining, StubHub, Lyft, Peloton and Apple subscriptions, only add value if you would have spent that money anyway.
Does the Chase Freedom Flex have a foreign transaction fee?▼
No. As of November 2026, Chase lists no foreign transaction fees on the Freedom Flex. NerdWallet reports the card previously charged 3% and dropped the fee as of Sept. 20, 2026. The Sapphire Reserve also has no foreign transaction fee.
Can Freedom Flex points be transferred to airlines and hotels?▼
Not directly. On its own, the Freedom Flex earns points worth 1 cent each for cash back. Only transfer-eligible cards, including the Sapphire Preferred, Sapphire Reserve, Ink Business Preferred and Sapphire Reserve for Business, can send points to Chase's 16 airline and hotel partners. You first combine Freedom Flex points into one of those cards.
What APR do the Freedom Flex and Sapphire Reserve charge?▼
As of November 2026, the Freedom Flex offers a 0% intro APR for 15 months from account opening on purchases and balance transfers, then 18.49% to 27.99% variable. The Sapphire Reserve has no intro APR and charges 19.74% to 28.24% variable. Your rate depends on your creditworthiness.
