Average Funeral Cost in 2026: Burial vs. Cremation

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 7 min read

Funeral prices from the NFDA 2023 General Price List Study; cremation rates from the NFDA 2025 Cremation & Burial Report; state estimates from Kiplinger. Checked November 2026.

The median cost of a funeral with a viewing and burial is $8,300, and a funeral with a viewing and cremation is $6,280, according to the National Funeral Directors Association's latest price study. Those figures leave out the cemetery plot, headstone and other extras, which Kiplinger estimates can add $3,000 to $8,000 or more. Direct cremation with no service typically costs $1,000 to $3,500.

Key Takeaways

  • The median cost of a funeral with viewing and burial is $8,300, up 5.8% from $7,848 two years earlier, according to the NFDA 2023 General Price List Study.
  • The median cost of a funeral with viewing and cremation is $6,280, up 8.1% from $5,810, according to the same NFDA study.
  • NFDA's median funeral costs exclude the cemetery, a headstone or marker, and cash advance items such as flowers or an obituary.
  • NFDA projected a 2025 U.S. cremation rate of 63.4%, more than double the burial rate of 31.6%.
  • Social Security pays a one-time $255 death payment to an eligible spouse or child, and VA pays up to $1,002 toward burial for eligible non-service-connected veteran deaths on or after October 1, 2025.

Average Funeral Cost by Type

The most widely cited figures come from the National Funeral Directors Association (NFDA), the largest funeral trade group in the U.S. Its General Price List study surveys member funeral homes on what they charge. The latest edition available when we checked in November 2026 is the 2023 study, released in December 2023. NFDA reports medians, meaning half of funeral homes charge more and half charge less.

Type of funeralMedian costTwo years earlierChange
Funeral with viewing and burial$8,300$7,848+5.8%
Funeral with viewing and cremation$6,280$5,810+8.1%
Direct cremation (no service)$1,000 to $3,500—typical range, Kiplinger (October 2026)

Both NFDA medians rose more slowly than overall prices. NFDA notes that general inflation was 13.6% over the same two years. The survey went to 5,219 NFDA members in July 2023, and 809 completed it.

Direct cremation means the body is cremated shortly after death with no viewing or service at the funeral home. The family can still hold a memorial later. NFDA's release does not give a median for it, so the range above comes from Kiplinger's October 2026 cost guide.

What the Median Funeral Cost Leaves Out

NFDA's burial median includes the basic services fee, transfer of the body to the funeral home, embalming and other preparation, use of the facilities and staff for the viewing and funeral ceremony, a hearse, a service car or van, a basic printed memorial package and a metal casket. The cremation median swaps the casket and hearse for a cremation fee, an alternative cremation container and an urn. Neither includes:

  • The cemetery. Interment, meaning the plot and burial in a cemetery, is excluded.
  • A headstone or marker. Monument and grave marker costs are excluded for both burial and cremation.
  • Cash advance items. These are things the funeral home pays for on your behalf and passes through, such as flowers or an obituary.
  • A burial vault. NFDA's list does not include an outer burial container. The FTC notes that no state law requires one, but many cemeteries do.

Kiplinger estimates that extras such as a plot, vault, headstone, flowers, music and an obituary can add $3,000 to $8,000 or more to the final bill. That puts a full traditional burial well above the $8,300 median in many areas.

Cremation vs. Burial: Costs and Trends

Cremation with a service costs about $2,000 less than a funeral with burial at the NFDA medians ($6,280 vs. $8,300), and the burial figure still leaves out the cemetery. Direct cremation is cheaper again. Cost is one reason cremation keeps growing. NFDA's 2025 Cremation and Burial Report, released in October 2025, projected a U.S. cremation rate of 63.4% for 2025, more than double the burial rate of 31.6%. Axios reported in January 2026 that cremation rates ranged from 44.3% in Alabama to 85.3% in Nevada in 2025.

Social Security. The $255 payment goes to a spouse who was living with the person who died, or to a spouse living apart who can get benefits on that person's record. If there is no spouse, a child may qualify if they are 17 or younger, 18 or 19 and in school (K–12) full time, or any age with a disability that began at 21 or younger.

Veterans. The U.S. Army's benefits library, reviewed in April 2026, lists the $1,002 amounts for non-service-connected deaths on or after October 1, 2025. VA says claims for a non-service-connected burial allowance must be filed within 2 years after the veteran's burial, and there is no time limit for a service-connected claim. VA also provides a government headstone or marker at no charge to the applicant.

How to Lower Funeral Costs

A federal rule, the FTC Funeral Rule, gives you several rights that make it easier to compare prices and skip what you do not want:

  1. Get prices by phone. Funeral homes must give you price information over the phone if you ask, so you can call two or three before you choose one.
  2. Ask for the General Price List. The funeral home must give you a written General Price List (GPL) that you can keep. It itemizes every good and service.
  3. Buy only what you want. No state law requires routine embalming for every death, and no state or local law requires a casket for cremation. Funeral homes that offer cremation must make a simpler alternative container available.
  4. Buy the casket or urn elsewhere. The funeral home cannot refuse to handle a casket or urn you bought somewhere else, and it cannot charge you a fee to do it.
  5. Plan the cemetery costs separately. The Funeral Rule does not cover cemeteries without an on-site funeral home or third-party sellers such as monument dealers, so ask those sellers for written prices too.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an average funeral cost?

The median funeral with a viewing and burial costs $8,300, and a funeral with a viewing and cremation costs $6,280, according to the National Funeral Directors Association's 2023 General Price List Study. Cemetery costs, a headstone and other extras are not included.

Is cremation cheaper than burial?

Usually. A funeral with cremation has a median cost of $6,280 compared with $8,300 for a funeral with burial, and burial also brings cemetery costs. Direct cremation with no service typically costs $1,000 to $3,500, according to Kiplinger.

What does the median funeral cost not include?

NFDA's median excludes the cemetery plot and interment, a monument or grave marker, and cash advance items such as flowers or an obituary. Kiplinger estimates these extras can add $3,000 to $8,000 or more.

Does Social Security pay for funerals?

Only a little. Social Security pays a one-time $255 lump-sum death payment to an eligible surviving spouse, or to certain children if there is no spouse. You must apply within 2 years of the death.

Does the VA pay for a veteran's funeral?

VA pays part of it for eligible veterans. For non-service-connected deaths on or after October 1, 2025, it pays up to $1,002 toward burial and a $1,002 plot-interment allowance if the veteran is not buried in a national cemetery. For service-connected deaths on or after September 11, 2001, it pays up to $2,000.
