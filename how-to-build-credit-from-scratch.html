How to Build Credit From Scratch (2026)

📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ Mike Van Kempen

📋 General information, not financial advice. Terms and approvals vary by lender.

A "thin file" (no credit history) makes renting, borrowing, and even some jobs harder. The good news: you can build credit from zero with a secured card, a credit-builder loan, or authorized-user status — and you'll usually have a score within 3–6 months. Here's the step-by-step, plus the two habits that matter more than everything else.

4 Ways to Start a Credit History

MethodHow it works
Secured credit cardYou put down a refundable deposit (often $200) that becomes your limit. Easiest approval for most people.
Credit-builder loanA small loan the lender holds in savings while you make payments; you get the money (plus your payment history) at the end.
Authorized userA responsible family member adds you to their card; their good history can appear on your report.
Student / starter cardSome unsecured cards approve thin-file or student applicants directly.

Step-by-Step: Build Credit From Zero

  1. Pick one starter account. A no-annual-fee secured card is the simplest. Confirm it reports to all three bureaus (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion) — that's what builds your file.
  2. Use it for one small recurring charge. Put a single subscription or your gas on it. You don't need to carry a balance to build credit — you just need activity.
  3. Pay in full, on time, every month. Set autopay for the statement balance. Payment history is 35% of your score; one missed payment can undo months of progress.
  4. Keep utilization low. Stay under 30% of your limit — ideally under 10%. On a $200 secured card, that means keeping the balance under ~$20–$60 when it reports.
  5. Be patient and don't over-apply. Let the account age. Avoid opening several cards at once — each application is a small ding and a young file is fragile.
  6. Graduate and grow. After 6–12 months of on-time payments, many secured cards refund your deposit and upgrade you, or you can qualify for a better unsecured card.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I build credit with no history?

Start with a secured card, credit-builder loan, or authorized-user status. Use it for small purchases, pay in full and on time, keep utilization low. You'll usually have a score in 3–6 months.

How long does it take?

About six months of an account reporting to generate a FICO score; a "good" score (670+) usually takes a year or more of on-time payments and low balances.

What's the best first card?

For most, a no-annual-fee secured card (refundable ~$200 deposit) that reports to all three bureaus. Some student/starter unsecured cards also work.

Does being an authorized user help?

Yes, if the issuer reports authorized users (most do). A responsible primary cardholder's on-time payments and low balance can build your history.

What hurts a new score most?

Late payments and high utilization. Also applying for many cards at once or closing your only account. Keep it simple: one or two accounts, paid on time, low balances.
