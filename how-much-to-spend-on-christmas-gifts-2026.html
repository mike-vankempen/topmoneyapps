How Much to Spend on Christmas Gifts in 2026 (By Relationship)

📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

📋 Benchmarks informed by PwC's Holiday Outlook 2026 and NRF survey data. See our average holiday spending breakdown.

There's no single "right" amount to spend on a Christmas gift — but there is a smart way to decide. Set your total budget first (a good rule: 1–1.5% of your annual income, which lands most people near the $708 average gift spend), then divide it across your list by relationship. Here's a realistic per-person guide, plus how to keep the whole thing from turning into a January credit-card bill.

Step 1: Set Your Total Budget First

The single biggest mistake is deciding gift-by-gift and discovering the damage in January. Instead, pick one total number you're comfortable spending — a sensible guardrail is 1% to 1.5% of your annual income on gifts. On a $60,000 salary that's about $600–$900; on $100,000, about $1,000–$1,500. Then everything below is just dividing that total across your list.

How Much to Spend By Relationship

These are typical ranges, not rules — adjust to your budget and your relationships. The point is a consistent framework, so no one feels over- or under-valued:

The "four gifts" rule for kids: something they want, something they need, something to wear, and something to read. It caps the pile, keeps spending sane, and still feels generous.

Step 2: Add Up and Adjust

Total your per-person amounts. If the sum blows past the budget you set in Step 1, don't just swipe the card — trim the list instead. The easiest cuts: propose a gift exchange (Secret Santa with a dollar cap) for extended family or your friend group, so everyone buys one gift instead of ten. It's less money, less shopping, and usually more fun.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I spend on Christmas gifts in 2026?

A good rule is 1–1.5% of your annual income on total gifts — about $600–$900 on a $60,000 salary. That aligns with the ~$708 average gift spend (PwC 2026). Set the total first, then divide it across your list.

How much for my spouse or partner?

Typically $100–$250, but agree on a shared limit together to remove the guesswork. Thoughtful and personal beats expensive.

How much should I spend on my kids?

Around $150–$250 per child is common, kept equal between kids. The "four gifts" rule (want, need, wear, read) caps the pile.

How much for friends, coworkers, and teachers?

Close friends $25–$50, coworkers/teachers $10–$25 (often group gifts), acquaintances a token $10–$15. A Secret Santa with a cap keeps big groups fair.

How can I spend less without looking cheap?

Set a budget and per-person cap, do a gift exchange, give experiences or homemade gifts, shop Cyber Week, and use a cash-back card. Thoughtfulness is what people remember — avoid BNPL debt.
