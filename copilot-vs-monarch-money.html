Copilot vs Monarch Money (2026): Which Is Better?

📅 Updated August 2026 ⏱ 9 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen

Copilot and Monarch Money are the two highest-rated AI budgeting apps of 2026, both earning 4.9 stars. They serve different users. Copilot is iPhone-only with best-in-class AI categorization and a design that rivals Apple's own apps. Monarch is cross-platform, couples-friendly, and includes investment tracking — the closest thing to a full Mint replacement that exists. This guide breaks down exactly which one is right for you.

Quick Verdict: Copilot vs Monarch Money

Choose Copilot if you're on iPhone, value design above everything else, and want the most accurate AI transaction categorization available. Copilot's UI is exceptional — it's the most polished budgeting app on iOS, and its AI learns your personal spending patterns faster than anything else.

Choose Monarch Money if you're on Android, share finances with a partner, want investment and net worth tracking alongside your budget, or are migrating from Mint. Monarch has a more complete financial dashboard and works on every device.

⭐ Our Take

If you're iPhone-only and don't need investment tracking: Copilot. If you're on Android, in a couple, or want a Mint-style full financial overview: Monarch Money. Both are excellent — you can't go wrong with either. The choice comes down to platform, household complexity, and whether you want investment tracking built in.

Copilot Money: The Definitive iPhone Budgeting App

Copilot launched in 2020 and quickly built a devoted following among Apple users who cared about both design and financial insight. The app is iOS-native through and through — it uses SwiftUI, integrates with Face ID, and has a visual language that feels like it could have been made by Apple itself.

What Copilot does best

The AI transaction categorization is Copilot's crown jewel. The model learns your personal spending patterns quickly — after 2–3 weeks, most users find that fewer than 5% of transactions need correction. When you routinely buy coffee at a merchant that shows up as "SQ*INDIGO EAST," Copilot learns that. It doesn't just rely on merchant databases; it watches your behavior and adapts.

The monthly spending reports are arguably the most beautiful in any personal finance app. They show your spending across categories with tasteful charts, month-over-month comparisons, and a clear narrative of how your month went. For people who check their finances emotionally (which is most of us), the visual quality of Copilot reduces friction and makes the habit sustainable.

Where Copilot falls short

Copilot is iOS only — there's no Android app and no web interface. This is a deal-breaker for Android users and households where partners use different platforms. Investment tracking is absent, and net worth tracking is limited. If you want to see your 401(k), IRA, and brokerage accounts alongside your budget, you'll need a separate tool (Empower fills this role well and is free).

Monarch Money: The Full Financial Dashboard

Monarch launched in 2021 and accelerated dramatically after Mint shut down in early 2024. Hundreds of thousands of Mint users migrated to Monarch, which had already built the most comprehensive Mint-like feature set of any successor app. Monarch works on iOS, Android, and the web — and its web app is genuinely excellent.

What Monarch does best

Monarch is the only app that comes close to doing everything: budgeting, transaction tracking, investment portfolio monitoring, net worth tracking, cash flow forecasting, and goal management — all in one place. The AI cash flow forecasting is particularly strong: Monarch analyzes your income cadence, recurring bills, and spending trends to project your account balance weeks or months ahead. This proactive view helps you avoid overdrafts and plan large expenses without surprises.

For couples, Monarch has no peer. Both partners can connect all their accounts, maintain individual spending views, see a combined budget, and comment on transactions. The collaboration features were clearly designed by people who manage shared finances — not as an afterthought.

Where Monarch falls short

Monarch's free trial is only 7 days, compared to Copilot's 30 days. This makes it harder to evaluate before committing. Monarch's iOS design, while excellent, doesn't quite reach Copilot's level of polish — it feels more like a web app ported to mobile, while Copilot feels native. And transaction categorization, while good, requires slightly more correction than Copilot in head-to-head testing.

Price Comparison

Copilot costs $13/month or $95/year. Monarch costs $14.99/month or $99.99/year. The annual pricing is nearly identical ($95 vs $100). The main pricing difference is the free trial: Copilot offers 30 days, Monarch offers only 7. Both offer a student discount if you sign up with an .edu email.

Who Should Choose Copilot?

  • You use iPhone and don't need to budget from Android or a browser
  • Design and visual quality matter to you
  • You want the most accurate AI transaction categorization
  • You're a solo user, not managing shared finances
  • You track investments separately (e.g., in Empower or Fidelity's own app)

Who Should Choose Monarch Money?

  • You're on Android or want to access your budget from a web browser
  • You share finances with a partner and want true joint budgeting
  • You want investment tracking and net worth monitoring alongside your budget
  • You're coming from Mint and want the closest functional replacement
  • You want detailed cash flow forecasting

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Copilot or Monarch Money better? ▼

Copilot is better for iPhone users who want the best AI categorization and most polished design. Monarch is better for Android users, couples, Mint refugees, and anyone who wants investment and net worth tracking. Both score 4.9 stars. The choice comes down to your platform, household situation, and whether you need investment tracking.

Does Copilot Money work on Android? ▼

No — Copilot is iOS only with no Android version announced. If you're on Android, Monarch Money is the top alternative. It offers comparable AI budgeting features, investment tracking, and a strong web app.

Which is better for couples — Copilot or Monarch? ▼

Monarch Money is significantly better for couples. It was designed with shared finance in mind — both partners connect accounts, maintain individual views, see a combined budget, and can comment on transactions together. Copilot has basic sharing but lacks Monarch's depth for managing joint finances.

Is Copilot or Monarch a better Mint replacement? ▼

Monarch Money is the better Mint replacement. It covers account aggregation, budget categories, spending tracking, net worth monitoring, and investment tracking — closely matching Mint's full feature set. Copilot is a more focused, AI-first budgeting experience that doesn't attempt to replicate everything Mint did.

Does Monarch Money have better AI than Copilot? ▼

Copilot has better AI transaction categorization — it learns your individual habits faster and requires fewer manual corrections. Monarch's AI leads in cash flow forecasting, projecting your balance weeks ahead. Both are excellent AI budgeting apps; the advantage depends on which feature you value most.
