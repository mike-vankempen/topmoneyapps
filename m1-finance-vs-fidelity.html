M1 Finance vs. Fidelity (2026): Which Investing App Is Better?

📅 Updated November 27, 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen

Fidelity is the better fit for most investors because it has no account fee, no minimum and lets you trade during market hours instead of in scheduled windows, while M1 Finance suits hands-off investors who want a whole portfolio rebalanced automatically. Both charge $0 commission on U.S. stocks and ETFs. Here is how they compare on fees, trading, cash, margin and account types.

Key Takeaways

  • Fidelity has a TopMoneyApps score of 4.93/5 and M1 Finance 3.92/5 in the investing category.
  • M1 Finance charges a $3 monthly platform fee unless you hold at least $10,000 in M1 assets; Fidelity lists a $0 account service fee and no minimum to open.
  • M1 executes all trades in scheduled trade windows (around 9:30 a.m. and 3 p.m. ET), while Fidelity offers fractional share trading during normal market hours.
  • M1's High-Yield Cash account paid 3.35% APY as of September 2026; Fidelity's SPAXX money market fund had a 7-day yield of 3.61% as of November 25, 2026.
  • Moving an IRA out of M1 in full costs $200 ($100 transfer fee plus $100 closing fee); Fidelity lists $0 for account transfers out and IRA closeouts.

M1 Finance vs. Fidelity: Quick Verdict

Fidelity ranks higher in our investing rankings, with a TopMoneyApps score 4.93/5 versus TopMoneyApps score 3.92/5 for M1 Finance. Both charge $0 commission on U.S. stocks and ETFs and both offer fractional shares from $1. The big differences are fees on small accounts, how trades are executed and how many products each offers.

Choose Fidelity if you want a full-service broker with no account fee, trading during market hours, options, mutual funds, HSAs and 529 college savings plans in one place.

Choose M1 Finance if you want to build a portfolio of stocks and ETFs as a "pie" (a set of target percentages), deposit money on a schedule and let M1 invest and rebalance it for you. Expect a $3 monthly fee until you reach $10,000.

⭐ Our Verdict

Fidelity is the stronger all-around pick: no account fee, no minimum, free transfers out and a far wider product range. M1 Finance is a niche fit for automated, long-term investors who like its pie-based portfolios and either have $10,000 or more there or accept the $3 monthly fee.

Fees and Minimums

Neither broker charges a commission on U.S. stocks or ETFs. M1 says it "doesn't charge commission, trading or management fees for self-directed brokerage accounts," though regulatory, closure and ADR fees can apply.

The main cost difference is M1's platform fee. M1 charges a $3 monthly platform fee or a $3 monthly IRA fee (never both in the same month). It is waived for any month in which you hold at least $10,000 in total M1 assets for at least one day, or have an active M1 personal loan. That is $36 a year, which equals 3.6% of a $1,000 account. M1 also closes investment accounts worth $50 or less that have had no activity for 90 days or more.

M1 used to sell a paid membership called M1 Plus for $125 a year. It ended in 2024; since May 15, 2024, its features are standard for all clients and the $3 platform fee took its place.

Fidelity lists no minimum to open a retail brokerage account or IRA and a $0 account service fee. Its main per-trade charges are $0.65 per options contract and fees on some outside mutual funds: transaction-fee funds cost $49.95 per purchase, and no-transaction-fee funds from other companies cost $49.95 if you sell within 60 days.

Leaving costs more at M1. M1 charges a $100 outgoing transfer fee for all account types plus a $100 closing fee for retirement accounts, so moving an IRA out in full costs $200. Fidelity's pricing page lists $0 for account transfers out and IRA closeouts.

How Trading Works

This is the biggest practical difference. At M1, "all trades... are executed in scheduled trade windows rather than instantly." The morning window begins around 9:30 a.m. ET and the afternoon window around 3 p.m. ET. Accounts under $25,000 get one window a day; accounts at $25,000 or more get both. You can't pick an exact price, so M1 works best for buy-and-hold investing, not short-term trading.

M1 is built around "pies." You pick stocks and ETFs, assign each a target percentage, and M1 splits every deposit to keep you near those targets. Its minimum order is $1, fractional shares go down to 1/100,000 of a share, and auto-invest kicks in once your cash reaches $25.

Fidelity lets you buy fractional shares from $1 on NYSE- and Nasdaq-listed stocks and ETFs, during normal market hours, and you can set up recurring investments. If you like M1's portfolio approach, Fidelity offers self-directed basket portfolios, and its Fidelity Go robo-advisor manages a portfolio for you. New to the idea? See dollar-cost averaging.

Cash and Margin

Cash. M1's High-Yield Cash account paid 3.35% APY as of September 2026, with a promotional 4.50% APY for one year on new accounts funded with $10,000 or more within 30 days. Cash is swept to partner banks and FDIC-insured up to $4.75 million. Fidelity holds uninvested cash in its core position; the SPAXX money market fund had a 7-day yield of 3.61% as of November 25, 2026. A money market fund is not FDIC-insured. (For how yields are quoted, see APR vs. APY.)

Margin. Margin is a loan from the broker secured by your investments. M1's homepage advertised borrowing at 5.90% when checked, noting that rates may vary; you need at least $2,000 invested, and it isn't available on retirement or custodial accounts. Fidelity's base margin rate has been 10.825% since September 18, 2026, and it charges 12.075% on balances under $25,000, falling to 10.325% at $250,000 to $499,999. Borrowing to invest can magnify losses, so treat any margin rate as a cost, not a feature.

Account Types and Investments

M1 Finance

  • Accounts: individual and joint brokerage, custodial, traditional, Roth and SEP IRAs, trust accounts, and a High-Yield Cash account.
  • Rollovers: 401(k), 403(b) and 457 rollovers into an M1 IRA with no fees.
  • Crypto: provided by Bakkt, with a 1% fee on buys and sells and a limit of 10 trades a month.
  • Not offered: options and mutual funds, according to NerdWallet.
  • M1 Advisor: an AI-powered assistant that M1 describes as a free advisory service.

Fidelity

  • Accounts: brokerage, traditional, Roth, rollover and SEP IRAs, HSAs, 529 plans, custodial accounts and the Fidelity Youth Account.
  • Investments: stocks, ETFs, options and mutual funds, including Fidelity's own funds with no transaction fee.
  • Crypto: Fidelity Crypto offers bitcoin, ether, Solana, Litecoin and a few others, and crypto is available in traditional, rollover and Roth IRAs.
  • Managed options: Fidelity Go and other managed portfolios.

Fidelity reported $20.1 trillion in total assets under administration as of September 30, 2026. M1 Finance LLC is a FINRA and SIPC member and rollover checks are made payable to Apex Clearing; Fidelity Brokerage Services LLC is a NYSE and SIPC member. SIPC protects up to $500,000 per customer, including $250,000 in cash, if a broker fails. It does not cover market losses.

Who Should Choose M1 Finance?

  • Long-term investors who want a custom portfolio invested and rebalanced automatically
  • People who will hold $10,000 or more at M1, so the $3 monthly fee is waived
  • Investors who want a high-yield cash account next to their portfolio
  • Borrowers who want a lower advertised margin rate and accept the risk

Who Should Choose Fidelity?

  • Beginners with small balances who want no account fee and no minimum (see best investing apps for beginners)
  • Investors who want options, mutual funds, HSAs or 529 plans
  • Anyone who wants to trade during market hours rather than in scheduled windows
  • Retirement savers who want free IRA transfers (see Roth vs. traditional IRA)

Frequently Asked Questions

Is M1 Finance or Fidelity cheaper?▼
Fidelity is usually cheaper. Both charge $0 commission on U.S. stocks and ETFs, but M1 charges a $3 monthly platform or IRA fee unless you hold at least $10,000 in M1 assets, and $200 to move an IRA out in full. Fidelity lists a $0 account service fee and $0 for transfers out.
Does M1 Finance let you trade in real time?▼
No. M1 executes all trades in scheduled trade windows, with a morning window around 9:30 a.m. ET and an afternoon window around 3 p.m. ET. Accounts under $25,000 get one window a day; accounts at $25,000 or more get both.
Does M1 Finance still have M1 Plus?▼
No. M1 Plus, which cost $125 a year, ended in 2024. Since May 15, 2024, its features are standard for all clients, and M1 charges a $3 monthly platform fee instead unless you hold $10,000 or more.
Which pays more on cash, M1 or Fidelity?▼
They were close when checked. M1's High-Yield Cash account paid 3.35% APY as of September 2026 and is FDIC-insured through partner banks. Fidelity's SPAXX money market fund had a 7-day yield of 3.61% as of November 25, 2026, but it is not FDIC-insured.
Can I buy mutual funds or options on M1 Finance?▼
No. According to NerdWallet, M1 Finance does not offer options or mutual funds. Fidelity offers both, with $0.65 per options contract and no transaction fee on Fidelity's own funds.
