Empower vs. YNAB (2026): Free Tracker or Paid Budget App?

📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen

Choose YNAB if you want a hands-on, category-by-category budget and will pay $109 a year for it; choose Empower if you mainly want a free view of your net worth, investments and retirement progress. Empower's free dashboard has only basic budgeting, while YNAB has no free plan and limited investment tracking. Here is how the two compare on price, features, fees and ratings as of November 2026.

Key Takeaways

  • Empower has a TopMoneyApps score of 4.33/5 and YNAB has a TopMoneyApps score of 3.58/5.
  • The Empower Personal Dashboard app is free; YNAB costs $14.99 a month or $109 a year as of November 2026, after a 34-day free trial.
  • Empower's optional Personal Strategy wealth management requires at least $100,000 and charges an annual advisory fee of 0.89% on balances under $1 million.

Empower vs. YNAB: Quick Verdict

Empower ranks higher on our list, with a TopMoneyApps score 4.33/5 versus a TopMoneyApps score 3.58/5 for YNAB. The two apps solve different problems, though. Empower is mainly a free tracker for net worth, investments and retirement. YNAB (short for You Need A Budget) is a paid app built around zero-based budgeting, a method where you assign every dollar of income to a purpose before you spend it.

Choose Empower if you have investment and retirement accounts to watch, want to see your whole net worth in one place and don't want to pay for software.

Choose YNAB if your main goal is controlling day-to-day spending, you want separate targets for groceries, rent and irregular bills, and you are willing to learn a method and pay a subscription.

⭐ Our Verdict

Empower is the better free option for tracking wealth; its trade-off is basic budgeting and possible sales calls about its paid advisory service. YNAB is the more complete budgeting tool; its trade-offs are the $109 yearly cost, a learning curve and manual tracking for investment accounts. Some people use both.

Pricing and Fees

Empower

The Empower Personal Dashboard app is listed as free on the Apple App Store, and NerdWallet reports there is no paid version of the dashboard. Empower makes money from Personal Strategy, its optional wealth management service run by Empower Advisory Group, an SEC-registered investment adviser. As of November 2026, Empower lists these annual advisory fees, charged as a percentage of the assets it manages:

  • Investment Services ($100,000 to $249,999): 0.89%, with a dedicated advisor.
  • Wealth Management ($250,000 to $999,999): 0.89%, with an advisor and team.
  • Private Client ($1 million minimum): 0.79% on the first $3 million, 0.69% on the next $2 million, 0.59% on the next $5 million and 0.49% above $10 million.

At 0.89%, a $100,000 account costs $890 a year, and a $250,000 account costs $2,225 a year. These advisory fees do not include the expense ratios of the funds you hold. Empower says financial planning is included in the fee and there are no trade commissions. Client assets are held by Pershing Advisor Solutions, a BNY Mellon company.

YNAB

As of November 2026, YNAB costs $14.99 a month or $109 a year (about $9.08 a month), in US dollars plus tax where applicable. YNAB says the annual plan saves $70 compared with paying monthly. There is no free plan. New users get a 34-day free trial with no credit card required when signing up directly (app stores may ask for card details), and YNAB offers college students a free 365-day trial. One subscription can be shared with up to six people, such as a partner or family.

Budgeting Features

YNAB is a budgeting method as much as an app. Each time money arrives, you ask "What's it for?" and assign it to categories. YNAB's method covers spending for now, setting aside monthly amounts for irregular costs like car repairs and holidays, getting a month ahead on bills, and changing your plan whenever you need to. If you are new to this style, our zero-based budgeting guide explains it step by step. NerdWallet notes the method is involved, which can make it hard to stick with.

Empower sorts your linked bank and credit card transactions into categories automatically, by date, category or merchant. NerdWallet describes its budgeting as basic: you can set one overall monthly spending target, but you can't create multiple categories with separate limits. MyBankTracker calls the budgeting tool "a bit lackluster." If budgeting is your top priority, see our list of the best budgeting apps.

Net Worth, Investments and Retirement

This is Empower's strength. Its free tools include a Retirement Planner, Net Worth tracker, Budgeting & Cash Flow, Portfolio Analysis, Savings Planner, Debt Paydown, Emergency Fund and Transactions views. The app pulls together bank accounts, 401(k)s, IRAs, brokerage accounts and debts, and the retirement planner shows whether you are on track for your target date. Empower says it serves more than 20 million people.

YNAB handles investments in a limited way. Its help center says investment, retirement and tax-advantaged accounts won't automatically import transactions. It suggests adding them as unlinked "tracking" asset accounts and updating the balance yourself, or simply tracking your contributions in a budget category. Those balances are informational only. For more net worth options, see the best apps to track net worth.

Bank Syncing

YNAB connects to banks through two data providers, MX and Plaid, plus select Apple Wallet accounts. YNAB says most linked accounts refresh once or twice a day, and a posted transaction can take up to three days to appear. You can also import a file from your bank or enter transactions by hand. YNAB's pricing page says direct import works with select banks in the US, Canada, the UK and the EU, and YNAB says it will never sell your data.

Empower links bank, credit card, debt, retirement and investment accounts in one dashboard. The sources we checked did not state a limit on how many accounts you can link.

The Trade-Off With Empower's Free App

Empower's dashboard is free because it is also a path to its paid advisory service. MyBankTracker reported in January 2026 that once your investable assets reach $100,000, you should "expect to get regular calls" offering a free consultation. Becoming an advisory client is optional. If you are comparing paid advice with lower-cost automated investing, our robo-advisor rankings cover that choice.

YNAB has no advisory product to sell; its cost is the subscription itself.

Who Should Choose Empower?

  • People with 401(k), IRA or brokerage accounts who want one view of their net worth
  • Savers who want a free retirement planner and portfolio analysis
  • Anyone who wants light spending tracking without a subscription

Who Should Choose YNAB?

  • People living paycheck to paycheck who want a plan for every dollar
  • Couples or families who want to share one budget (up to six people)
  • College students, who can try it free for 365 days

Empower's paid product is its optional Personal Strategy wealth management service, which requires at least $100,000 and charges an annual advisory fee of 0.89% on balances under $1 million.
How much does YNAB cost in 2026?▼
As of November 2026, YNAB costs $14.99 a month or $109 a year in US dollars, plus tax where applicable. New users get a 34-day free trial with no credit card required when signing up directly, and college students can get a free 365-day trial. There is no free plan.
Is Empower or YNAB better for budgeting?▼
YNAB is built for detailed budgeting: you plan a job for every dollar across your own categories. Empower's budgeting is basic; NerdWallet reports it lets you set one overall monthly spending target and does not let you set separate limits for multiple categories.

Only in a limited way. YNAB says investment and retirement accounts will not automatically import transactions, so it suggests adding them as unlinked asset accounts and updating the balance yourself. Empower links investment and retirement accounts and adds portfolio analysis and a retirement planner.
Will Empower call me about its advisory service?▼
