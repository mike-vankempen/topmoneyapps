Social Security COLA for 2027: How Much Checks Rise

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 6 min read

General information, not financial advice. Figures are from the Social Security Administration 2027 COLA Fact Sheet, published October 14, 2026.

Social Security benefits will rise 3.6% in 2027. The Social Security Administration (SSA) estimates the average retired worker's monthly check will go from $2,095 to $2,170, an increase of about $75 a month. The raise applies to benefits payable in January 2027 for nearly 72 million Social Security beneficiaries, and to Supplemental Security Income (SSI) payments starting December 31, 2026. Below are the new averages by beneficiary type and the other 2027 figures that changed.

Key Takeaways

  • The Social Security cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) for 2027 is 3.6%, up from 2.8% for 2026, according to the Social Security Administration.
  • The SSA estimates the average retired worker's benefit rises from $2,095 to $2,170 a month in January 2027, an increase of $75.
  • The 2027 COLA affects about 76 million people: nearly 72 million Social Security beneficiaries and about 7.3 million SSI recipients.
  • The maximum earnings subject to Social Security tax rise from $184,500 in 2026 to $192,300 in 2027.
  • The SSI federal payment for an individual rises from $994 to $1,029 a month, with the increase starting December 31, 2026.

The SSA publishes estimated average monthly benefits for January 2027, before and after the 3.6% increase. These are averages; your own increase is 3.6% of your own benefit, so a larger check gets a larger dollar raise.

When the 2027 Increase Shows Up

  • Social Security: the 3.6% raise applies to benefits payable in January 2027.
  • SSI: increased payments to about 7.3 million SSI recipients begin on December 31, 2026.
  • Online notice: the SSA says COLA notices will be available to most beneficiaries in the Message Center of their my Social Security account in late November 2026, for people who created an account and opted into paperless notices by November 18. The SSA's FAQ page says some retirement and disability beneficiaries can view theirs starting in early December.
  • Paper notice: mailed in December to people not enrolled in paperless notices. The SSA describes it as a simplified, one-page notice.

Your net check may rise by less than 3.6%. If Medicare Part B premiums are deducted from your benefit, a change in the premium changes your net deposit. The SSA says the 2027 Medicare premium will appear in the my Social Security Message Center starting in late November; it was not on the COLA fact sheet when we checked.

The payroll tax rate does not change: the SSA lists 7.65% for employees and 15.30% for the self-employed in both years. The Medicare portion has no earnings cap. SSI resource limits stay at $2,000 for an individual and $3,000 for a couple.

How the Earnings Test Works if You Claim Early and Keep Working

If you collect benefits before your full retirement age and still have a job, the SSA temporarily withholds part of your benefit once your earnings pass the limit.

  • Under full retirement age all year: $1 is withheld for every $2 you earn above $25,560 in 2027. Example: earning $35,560 puts you $10,000 over, so $5,000 in benefits is withheld.
  • The year you reach full retirement age: $1 is withheld for every $3 above $67,920, counting only earnings in the months before you reach full retirement age.
  • From the month you reach full retirement age: no earnings limit applies.

How the COLA Is Calculated

The COLA is tied to inflation. The SSA compares the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W), which the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes, for the third quarter of 2026 with the third quarter of 2025. The percentage increase between those two averages becomes the next year's COLA. That is why the 2027 figure was announced in October 2026, after September inflation data came out.

The 2027 increase of 3.6% is larger than the 2.8% COLA that took effect in January 2026. A higher COLA means inflation measured by the CPI-W ran faster over the past year; it raises your check but does not mean your purchasing power grew.

What the COLA Means for Your Planning

A COLA is meant to keep a benefit's buying power roughly level with inflation as measured by the CPI-W. It is not a real raise. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Social Security COLA for 2027?

The 2027 cost-of-living adjustment is 3.6%. The Social Security Administration announced it on October 14, 2026. It applies to Social Security benefits payable in January 2027 and to SSI payments starting December 31, 2026.

How much will my Social Security check go up in 2027?

Your benefit rises by 3.6%. The SSA estimates the average retired worker's check goes from $2,095 to $2,170 a month, about $75 more. Your net deposit can differ if your Medicare Part B premium changes.

When will I get my 2027 COLA notice?

The SSA says most beneficiaries enrolled in paperless notices by November 18, 2026 can see their notice in the my Social Security Message Center starting in late November. Paper notices are mailed in December.

What is the Social Security wage base for 2027?

The maximum earnings subject to Social Security tax rise to $192,300 in 2027, from $184,500 in 2026. Medicare tax has no earnings cap.

How is the Social Security COLA calculated?

The SSA measures the increase in the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W) from the third quarter of the prior year to the third quarter of the current year. For 2027, that was the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026.
