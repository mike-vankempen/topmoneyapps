Illinois Pick 3 & Pick 4 Odds (2026): We Audited 54,029 Draws

📅 Updated September 2026 ⏱ 10 min read ✍️ By Mike Van Kempen 📊 Source: Illinois Lottery
This is an independent data analysis for informational and entertainment purposes. It is not a betting system and does not improve your odds — because, as the data shows, nothing can. Please play responsibly.

Every day, thousands of Illinois players chase "hot," "cold," and "due" numbers in Pick 3 and Pick 4. So we did what nobody selling a system ever does: we pulled every draw since 1982 — 54,029 of them — and ran 962 statistical tests to see whether any pattern exists that a player could actually use. The answer is a clean, well-documented no. Here are the real odds, the real payouts, and the data that ends the "hot numbers" myth for good. More in our Personal Finance Statistics Hub →

The odds & payouts, straight from the rulebook

Pick 3 asks you to match a 3-digit number (000–999); Pick 4 a 4-digit number (0000–9999). A straight bet must match the exact order; a box bet wins if your digits arrive in any order — better odds, smaller prize. Here is what a $1 wager gets you:

Where your dollar really goes

Every one of those bets returns about 50 cents on the dollar over time. That is the single most important number in this entire article: whatever you bet, the state keeps roughly half. It is one of the worst house edges in legal gambling — for comparison, slot machines typically return 88–95 cents on the dollar.

Are the numbers actually random? Yes.

If any digit came up more often than others, you could tilt the odds. It doesn't. Across all 27,347 Pick 3 draws, every digit 0–9 lands almost exactly 10% of the time in every position — the tiny wiggles below are exactly the size you get from chance, and they sit comfortably inside the 95% confidence band.

Why this doesn't help you

In a fair 44-year history, the single most-drawn combo typically lands around 43–50 times and the least around 12 — so "394 at 49" and "685 at 8" are within, or a hair beyond, the normal range. More importantly, past draws have zero influence on the next one: 685 is exactly as likely as 394 tonight, both at 1 in 1,000. "Due" numbers are the gambler's fallacy in a lottery costume.

The three "patterns" people send us — and why none pay

When you analyze 54,029 draws, a few coincidences always surface. Here are the three most convincing-looking ones we found, and why each collapses on inspection.

1. "Pick 4 evening numbers ending in 9"

In the computer-RNG era, 11.9% of evening Pick 4 draws ended in 9, versus 10% expected (z = 4.0, one-in-ten-thousand on its own). Tempting. But it is the largest of 140 position-and-digit cells we checked, and the largest of 140 reaches that size about 1% of the time by chance. The tell: it vanishes everywhere else — midday draws ending in 9 run 9.5%, and ball-era evenings ran 9.5%. And even if it were real, a $1 straight bet on a combo ending in 9 would return 59 cents — still a 41% loss.

2. "Pick 3 evening numbers repeat"

Since the 2015 switch to a computer RNG, the exact evening Pick 3 number repeated the next evening 11 times versus about 4 expected (p = 0.003). Every one is official-verified — it really happened. But repeats at every other lag, in midday draws, and in Pick 4 are all at or below expectation, and over the full 44-year history the "always bet last night's number" strategy hits at exactly the chance rate. It is one flagged coincidence among hundreds of tests, not a signal.

3. "The number 685 is ice cold"

685 has been drawn just 8 times in 27,347 draws (expected: 27) and never in the RNG era. Spooky — until you remember we're looking at the minimum of 1,000 combinations. A minimum this low shows up in about 2% of fair lottery histories, and a "cold" number is not "due." Its odds tonight are 1 in 1,000, identical to every other combination.

We tried to beat it. Everything failed.

This is where most "analysis" stops. We kept going and actively tried to build a winning system:

  • 962 statistical tests. 61 came back "significant" at p<0.05 — but about 48 are expected by chance alone, and zero survived a false-discovery-rate correction. The smallest p-value (0.0011) is right where the best of 962 coin-flips should land.
  • 100 simulated fair lotteries. We generated 100 fake histories with random digits and ran the identical battery. Nine of them threw up more "significant" results than the real Illinois data, and four produced a result that survived correction — something the real data never did. The real lottery looks more random than many fakes.
  • Every popular strategy, back-tested. Hot, cold, due, repeat-last, and Markov systems — 15 rules per game, walk-forward — all landed at chance.
  • Machine learning. A LightGBM model and a neural network scored within a few thousandths of the no-skill baseline; logistic regression did worse than guessing (it overfit noise). No model predicted a digit better than 10% or a straight combo better than 1 in 1,000.

📊 The bottom line

Across 54,029 draws and 962 tests, the Illinois Pick 3 and Pick 4 games contain no detectable — let alone exploitable — pattern. The switch from numbered balls (through 2015) to a computer RNG (since October 2015) changed nothing (G-test p = 0.33). The draws are random and the payouts are fixed, which means there is exactly one honest strategy: treat it as entertainment you can afford to lose, never as an investment. If you want your money to actually grow, that is a different — and winnable — game.

So what is the "best" way to play?

Since no bet beats the ~50% house edge and no number is ever due, the math is simple:

  • Box bets win more often; straight bets pay more. Neither has a better expected value — pick whichever you find more fun.
  • Your own numbers are as good as any system. Birthdays, a quick pick, or a $49 "prediction app" all have identical odds. Save the money you'd spend on the app.
  • Set a fixed entertainment budget and never chase losses. The house edge is relentless over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the odds of winning Illinois Pick 3? ▼

A straight bet (exact order) is 1 in 1,000 and pays $500 on $1. A 6-way box is about 1 in 167 ($80); a 3-way box is about 1 in 333 ($160). Every option returns roughly 50 cents per $1 over time.

What are the odds of winning Illinois Pick 4? ▼

A straight bet is 1 in 10,000 and pays $5,000 on $1. A 24-way box is about 1 in 417 ($200). Like Pick 3, the expected return is about 50 cents per dollar wagered.

Are there hot or cold numbers? ▼

None you can use. Across 54,029 draws, the hottest Pick 3 combo (394, 49 times) and coldest (685, 8 times) both fall within chance. Every digit hits about 10% and no number is more likely to come up next.

Can you predict Pick 3 or Pick 4 numbers? ▼

No. We back-tested hot/cold/due/repeat/Markov systems and machine-learning models (LightGBM, a neural net, logistic regression) with walk-forward testing. None beat random guessing or predicted digits better than 10%.

Is the Illinois Lottery random or rigged? ▼

The data is consistent with fair randomness. Numbered balls (through Sept 2015) and the computer RNG (since Oct 2015) show no difference (G-test p = 0.33). Nothing is tilted for or against players — it is random, with a fixed house edge in the payouts.

What is the best way to bet Pick 3 or Pick 4? ▼

No bet has positive expected value; all return about 50 cents per dollar. Box wins more often, straight pays more. Pick whatever is fun, set a budget you can lose, and skip paid "prediction" systems — they can't beat 1-in-1,000 odds.
