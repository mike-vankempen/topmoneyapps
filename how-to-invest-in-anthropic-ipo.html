Can You Invest in Anthropic Right Now?

Not yet — and that's the key thing to understand. On October 26, 2026, Anthropic filed its S-1 registration statement with the SEC, the formal first step toward an IPO. The filing says it intends to list Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ANT. But filing isn't trading: until the IPO prices and the stock debuts, there are no public Anthropic shares to buy on a normal brokerage.

Scam warning: Because "Anthropic IPO" is one of the most-searched investing terms right now, scammers are actively pitching fake "pre-IPO Anthropic shares" and allocation offers. There is no legitimate way for the general public to buy Anthropic shares before the IPO. Treat any unsolicited offer — especially over social media, email, or messaging apps — as a scam.

How to Buy Anthropic (ANT) When It Lists

Most individual investors won't get shares at the IPO price — those allocations typically go to institutions and a limited set of brokerage IPO-access programs (and the brokers offering Anthropic access haven't been announced). Here's the realistic path for a retail investor:

  1. Open and fund a brokerage account now. Any major broker that trades Nasdaq stocks will let you buy ANT once it's live. Having the account funded ahead of time means you're ready on day one.
  2. Check your broker's IPO-access program. Some brokerages offer retail IPO allocations — for example, Robinhood's IPO Access lets eligible users request shares at the IPO price before a stock starts trading. Allocations are small and never guaranteed, but it's one of the few ways a retail investor can participate at the offer price if Anthropic's underwriters make shares available.
  3. Decide: buy at open, or wait. The first trading day is historically the most volatile. Many investors deliberately wait days or weeks for the initial swings to settle rather than chasing the open.
  4. Use a limit order, not a market order. On a hot first day, a market order can fill far above where you expected. A limit order caps the price you'll pay.
  5. Size the position for the risk. A brand-new, richly valued stock is speculative. Keep any position small relative to your overall portfolio.

The Bull and Bear Case

We don't take a position on whether ANT is a good buy — that depends on your goals and risk tolerance. Here's a balanced look at what each side argues:

Why to be cautious

When is the Anthropic IPO date?

No official date. Reports point to late November 2026, with a rules-based earliest of around November 16 — a best case, not a confirmed date. The final SEC pricing usually lands a day or two before trading begins.
