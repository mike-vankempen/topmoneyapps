How to File a Life Insurance Claim, Step by Step

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 7 min read

General information, not legal or tax advice. Based on guidance from the Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner and the NAIC.

To file a life insurance claim, contact the insurance company (or the agent who sold the policy), tell it about the death, and send a completed claim form with a certified copy of the death certificate. If there is more than one beneficiary, each one usually files a separate claim form. Most claims are paid within weeks: MassMutual says most are processed in 30 to 60 days, and claims can take longer if the death happened in the policy's first two years.

Key Takeaways

  • To claim a life insurance death benefit, a beneficiary notifies the insurer or agent and submits a claim form plus a copy of the death certificate; when there are several beneficiaries, each completes a claim form (Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner).
  • MassMutual says most life insurance claims are processed within 30 to 60 days; Mutual of Omaha says claims may take between 14 and 60 days to pay.
  • Most policies have a two-year contestability period, during which the insurer can investigate the application before paying a claim.
  • The NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator is free and has matched more than $16.9 billion in benefits from more than 780,000 matches through July 31, 2026.
  • Life insurance death benefits received by individuals are generally tax-free, but interest earned on delayed benefits can be taxable (MassMutual).

Step-by-Step: Filing the Claim

  1. Find the policy and the insurer. Look for the policy document, premium bills in the mail, or premium payments on bank statements. NerdWallet notes you don't need the policy number: the insurer can look it up from the insured person's details. If you can't find the company, use the free NAIC locator described below.
  2. Get certified copies of the death certificate. Mutual of Omaha suggests getting several copies from the funeral home and says a death certificate can take two to four weeks to arrive. You can also order them from your state's vital records office.
  3. Notify the insurer. Call the company's claims line, use its online claim form, or ask the agent. Ask which documents it needs and where to send them.
  4. Complete the claim form. Some companies call it a claimant statement or request for benefits. Expect to give the insured person's full name, date of birth, Social Security number and date of death, plus your relationship to them.
  5. Choose how to be paid. Most forms ask whether you want a lump sum or another option (see the table below).
  6. Submit and keep copies. Send the form and death certificate online, by mail or through the agent. Keep a copy of everything and write down the date you sent it.

How Long Does a Life Insurance Claim Take?

There is no single national deadline. Insurers' own guides give these ranges:

  • MassMutual: most claims are processed within 30 to 60 days, and simple claims can be faster.
  • Mutual of Omaha: claims may take between 14 and 60 days to pay, and some are paid within a few days.
  • NerdWallet: insurers usually process claims within 30 days of receiving the paperwork.

State law can add protections. For example, Washington's insurance regulator says that insurers there must pay at least 8% interest starting from the date of death, plus an additional 3% on claims not settled within 90 days of proof of death. Rules differ by state, so check with your state insurance department if a payment seems slow. Mutual of Omaha also notes there is no time limit for filing a claim, but filing promptly gets the money to you sooner.

Payout Options

OptionHow it works
Lump sumOne payment of the full death benefit
InstallmentsFixed payments monthly, quarterly or yearly for a set period or until the money runs out
Interest onlyThe insurer keeps the benefit and pays you the interest; the balance passes to your beneficiaries at your death
AnnuityIncome over time, where the policy offers it (MassMutual)
Retained asset accountAn interest-bearing account held by the insurer that works like a checking account, usually with a checkbook

The first three options and the retained asset account are described by the Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner. Which ones you get depends on the policy and insurer. If a policy names the estate as beneficiary, MassMutual notes the payout may go through probate, the court process for settling an estate.

The Contestability Period

Most policies have a contestability period covering the first two years of coverage. If the insured person dies during it, the insurer can investigate whether important information was left off the application. Washington's regulator says that if it was, the claim will likely be denied regardless of the policyholder's intent. These reviews can make a claim take longer.

Policies can also have exclusions. Common ones listed by Washington's regulator include war, military service, suicide within two years of the issue date (this one does not apply to group policies) and some airplane accidents, as the policy defines them.

Why Claims Get Delayed or Denied

  • Missing or incorrect paperwork: an incomplete form or no certified death certificate.
  • Lapsed policy: premiums stopped and the coverage ended before the death, or a term policy expired.
  • Contestability review: the death happened in the first two years and the insurer is checking the application.
  • Exclusions: the cause of death falls under a policy exclusion.
  • Loans or withdrawals: money borrowed from a permanent policy while the insured was alive can reduce the payout (Mutual of Omaha).

If a claim is denied, you should receive a letter explaining why. Ask for a written explanation, send any missing records, and appeal through the insurer's process; there may be a time limit to appeal. If that fails, you can file a complaint with your state's insurance department, and an attorney can review the case.

Can't Find the Policy? Use the NAIC Locator

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) runs a free Life Insurance Policy Locator. You enter your contact details and the deceased person's information from the death certificate, including Social Security number, legal name, date of birth and date of death. Participating insurers search their records. If a policy is found and you are the beneficiary, the company contacts you; if not, you won't hear back. The NAIC says searches may take 90 business days or more.

Since it launched in November 2016, the locator has received more than 1.5 million requests and produced more than 780,000 matches worth $16,991,229,586 through July 31, 2026, according to the NAIC. For how many Americans carry coverage, see our life insurance statistics.

Is the Payout Taxable?

Generally no. MassMutual says death benefits received by individuals are generally tax-free, but interest earned on delayed benefits, or proceeds paid to an estate, can be taxable. Frequently Asked Questions

How long do you have to file a life insurance claim?

Mutual of Omaha says there is no time limit for filing a claim, but filing soon after the death gets the benefit to you faster.

How long does it take to get paid?

Insurers say most claims are paid within weeks. MassMutual says most are processed within 30 to 60 days, and Mutual of Omaha says claims may take between 14 and 60 days. Deaths in the first two years of a policy can take longer.

Do I need the original policy to file a claim?

Not always. NerdWallet notes you do not need the policy number because the insurer can look it up, but you do need the insurer's name, a claim form and a certified death certificate. Some insurers ask for more documents, so ask the company what it requires.

What if I do not know which company issued the policy?

Use the free NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator. Participating insurers search their records, and if you are a beneficiary the company contacts you. Searches may take 90 business days or more.

Can a life insurance claim be denied?

Yes. Common reasons include a lapsed policy, missing paperwork, a policy exclusion, or information left off the application discovered during the two-year contestability period. You can appeal and file a complaint with your state insurance department.
