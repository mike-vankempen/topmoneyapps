Average Cost of Owning a Dog in 2026: Yearly and Monthly

By Mike Van Kempen Updated November 2026 6 min read

Cost estimates from the ASPCA and Rover; vet spending from the AVMA; insurance premiums from NAPHIA; prices from the BLS CPI. Checked November 2026.

Owning a dog costs about $1,390 to $5,295 a year, or $120 to $435 a month for a medium-size dog, according to Rover's 2025 survey of dog owners. The ASPCA's basic budget puts recurring costs at $1,391 a year and the first year at $3,221 once one-time costs are added. Vet care is the biggest expense, and vet prices rose 4.6% in the year to September 2026.

Average Yearly Cost of Owning a Dog

There is no single official figure for the cost of a dog, so it helps to look at two sources side by side. The ASPCA publishes a basic cost-of-care budget, last updated in 2021. Rover, a pet-sitting marketplace, surveyed 1,000 dog owners in February 2025 and publishes a low-to-high range.

CostASPCA (2021 update)Rover (2025 survey)
One-time, first-year costs$1,030$1,150 to $4,420 upfront
Recurring yearly costs$1,391$1,390 to $5,295
Total first year$3,221 (includes $800 in special costs)—
Monthly, medium-size dogabout $116 (yearly cost ÷ 12)$120 to $435

ASPCA's yearly figure covers food, routine medical care, preventive medicine, pet health insurance, toys, treats, a license and grooming supplies. Its first-year total adds the one-time costs plus two "special" costs: $300 for professional grooming and $500 for dental care. Boarding is extra, at about $25 a day in ASPCA's estimate. Because the ASPCA figures are from 2021, they likely understate today's prices; ASPCA's yearly total sits right at the bottom of Rover's 2025 range.

Cost by Life Stage: Puppy, Adult and Senior

Costs are highest at the start and again near the end of a dog's life. Rover's 2025 yearly ranges by age:

Life stageYearly cost range (Rover, 2025)
Puppy$1,520 to $6,415
Adult dog$1,260 to $4,170
Senior dog$2,555 to $4,680

In Rover's ranges, senior dogs have the highest minimum yearly cost. The upper ends of the ranges reflect choices such as premium food, daycare, dog walkers and frequent professional grooming.

Where the Money Goes: Vet Care and Food

Vet care and food are the two biggest costs. In the American Pet Products Association's (APPA) 2025 survey of dog owners, vet care was the largest share of spending at 29%, followed by food at 27%. APPA also found that owners cut back on extras: average spending on treats fell 11%, and 51% of owners now groom their dogs at home.

  • Vet care. Dog-owning households spent an average of $595 a year on veterinary care, according to the American Veterinary Medical Association's (AVMA) 2026 Pet Ownership and Demographic Sourcebook.
  • Pet insurance. The average accident and illness policy for a U.S. dog cost $836 a year in 2025 (about $70 a month), and an accident-only policy cost $190 a year, according to the North American Pet Health Insurance Association (NAPHIA). About 5.23 million U.S. pets were insured.
  • Food and treats. Across all pets, Americans spent $68.3 billion on food and treats in 2025, the largest category in APPA's industry totals.
  • Boarding and grooming. These vary most by owner. ASPCA estimates $25 a day for boarding and $300 a year for professional grooming.

Accident and illness insurance helps with large, unexpected bills such as surgery, but premiums add up. Compare the yearly premium and deductible with what you could cover from savings. Our emergency fund statistics show how many households can handle a surprise expense.

Pet Prices Are Still Rising

The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) tracks pet prices in the Consumer Price Index (CPI). From September 2025 to September 2026:

CPI category12-month change (Sept. 2026)
Veterinarian services+4.6%
Pet services, including veterinary+3.1%
Pet food and treats+1.5%
Pets and pet products+1.3%

September is the latest 12-month comparison available. BLS did not publish 12-month changes for October 2026 because it did not collect October 2025 prices during the 2025 lapse in federal appropriations. Over that year, vet prices rose about three times as fast as pet food prices.

How Many Americans Own Dogs

Estimates differ because the two main surveys use different methods:

  • APPA: 71 million U.S. households, or 53%, owned a dog in 2025, up from 51% in 2024. Total U.S. pet industry spending was $158 billion in 2025, and APPA projects $165 billion for 2026.
  • AVMA: 58.9 million households, or 43.7%, owned a dog in its 2026 survey, with 88.9 million dogs in total.

How to Budget for a Dog

  1. Price the first year. Add adoption or breeder fees, a crate, bed, leash, vaccines and spaying or neutering to your first-year plan. ASPCA puts one-time costs at about $1,030; Rover's upfront range runs to $4,420.
  2. Set a monthly line item. Budget $120 to $435 a month for a medium-size dog, based on Rover's 2025 range, and track what you actually spend. Our average monthly expenses page shows how this compares with other household costs.
  3. Save for the vet. Put a fixed amount aside each month in a sinking fund for vet bills, or price out an insurance policy.
  4. Cut the extras, not the care. Grooming at home, buying food in bulk and limiting treats are the cuts APPA found owners already making.

A budgeting app can make it easier to see what your dog really costs once you tag pet spending as its own category.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to own a dog per year?

Rover's 2025 survey puts the yearly cost of a dog at $1,390 to $5,295, depending on size, age and lifestyle. The ASPCA's basic budget, last updated in 2021, is $1,391 a year.

How much does a dog cost per month?

A medium-size dog costs about $120 to $435 a month, according to Rover's 2025 survey. The ASPCA's yearly estimate of $1,391 works out to about $116 a month.

How much does a dog cost in the first year?

The ASPCA puts the first-year cost at $3,221, including $1,030 in one-time costs, $1,391 in recurring costs and $800 for professional grooming and dental care. Rover's 2025 survey found upfront costs of $1,150 to $4,420 on top of yearly costs.

How much is pet insurance for a dog?

The average accident and illness policy for a U.S. dog cost $836 a year in 2025, and an accident-only policy cost $190 a year, according to the North American Pet Health Insurance Association.

How much do dog owners spend on the vet?

Dog-owning households spent an average of $595 a year on veterinary care, according to the AVMA's 2026 Pet Ownership and Demographic Sourcebook. Vet care was the largest share of dog owners' spending, 29%, in APPA's 2025 survey.
