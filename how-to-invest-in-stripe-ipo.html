Is Stripe publicly traded?

No. Stripe — the payments company behind checkout for millions of businesses — remains privately held. It has not filed a public S-1 registration with the SEC, has not announced an exchange or ticker, and has repeatedly said it is in no hurry to go public. That means there is no legitimate way to buy Stripe shares through a standard brokerage account like Robinhood, Fidelity, or Schwab right now.

⚠️ Scam warning: Because Stripe is a famous name, scammers advertise "pre-IPO Stripe shares" or "early access" slots. There is no public Stripe stock and no official IPO allocation for retail investors. Never send money to anyone promising guaranteed Stripe IPO shares — verify everything through the SEC's EDGAR database and your own broker.

How to be ready for the Stripe IPO

  1. Open a brokerage account that offers IPO access. Brokers like Robinhood, SoFi, and Webull sometimes give everyday investors a shot at IPO shares at the offering price. Set this up before any listing.
  2. Keep some cash settled and ready. IPO windows move fast. Settled cash lets you act when shares open.
  3. Watch for the official S-1. When Stripe files, the prospectus reveals real financials, the ticker, and the exchange. That's your signal — not a social-media rumor.
  4. Decide your rules in advance. First-day IPO prices are volatile. Know your max price and position size before you click buy, and remember many brokers restrict flipping IPO shares for ~30 days.
